# Namesake stunt (a regular person with a famous rival's name)

## Shape

The brand finds an ordinary person who shares the name of a famous figure linked to a rival, such as a competitor's CEO. That person introduces themselves by full name to camera, then uses or praises the brand's product like any happy customer. The name does the joke; the review stays sincere.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the person says their full name and where they are from, straight to camera.
2. 2 to 15 s: the product in their hands, a short honest first impression.
3. 15 to 35 s: one or two things they like, said plainly, with the name shown on screen again.
4. Last seconds: a closing line that winks at the name without naming the rival brand.

## Make it yours

Look for real people who share the name of a rival founder, a famous investor or a well-known product figure in your category. A project management startup films a namesake of a rival's well-known founder reviewing its boards. An AI search engine films someone sharing a famous tech executive's name trying its answers. A design tool films a namesake of a famous designer. The stunt only works with a real person who really uses the product.

## Variations

- Switch story: the namesake shows how they moved from the rival's product (see the second example).
- Several namesakes: a group of people sharing the same famous name, one line each.
- Reverse: the namesake of your own founder reviews a rival, as self-mockery.

## Why it works

The name creates an instant surprise and a joke people want to pass on, which is social currency (Berger 2013). The sincere review under the joke lets the product message land while viewers are smiling.

## Watch out

- Get legal review first: comparative advertising rules and the use of a famous person's name vary by country.
- The person must be real, carry that name, consent in writing, and use the product honestly. Disclose any payment.
- Never imply the famous figure endorses your product.
