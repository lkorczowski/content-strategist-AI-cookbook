# Drawn skit (illustrated situation)

## Shape

A situation from daily life is acted out with drawings, sketched live or animated simply. One voice plays every role, changing tone for each character. No face appears: the drawings and the voice carry the scene.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the first drawing and a line that sets the situation (a relationship, a conflict, a meeting).
2. 2 to 40 s: the characters talk, each drawn simply, the voice switching roles.
3. 40 to 100 s: the situation escalates, and the drawings exaggerate the reactions.
4. Last seconds: the punchline, or a small lesson the viewer can take away.

## Make it yours

Draw the relationships your users live with their tools, money or clients. A personal finance app draws a "toxic relationship" with a bank fee. An AI legal assistant draws a contract clause that keeps ghosting its reader. A developer platform draws the drama between a frontend and a backend that stopped talking. You can sketch on a tablet with screen capture, or use simple animation.

## Variations

- Whiteboard: drawn live on a board, the hand visible.
- Explainer twist: the skit turns into a 20-second explanation at the end.
- Series: the same drawn characters return each week.

## Why it works

Simple drawings make any situation light, so viewers accept a topic they would skip as a lecture. A relationship they recognize makes them tag the person it reminds them of.

## Watch out

- Keep the drawing style consistent across episodes; viewers follow characters by their look.
- If you use AI images, check platform labels for synthetic content and your rights to the output.
- The voice carries every role: record clean audio.

Write a "drawn skit" for [company], whose product [product] helps [audience] with [problem].
1. Propose 5 situations from [audience]'s daily life with [problem], told as a relationship between characters (a person and a tool, two roles, a person and a document).
2. For the best one, write the script with 2 or 3 characters: lines for each, voice tone notes, a drawing note per beat (what to sketch, what to exaggerate), the punchline or takeaway.
Rules: no face needed, consistent character designs, label AI-generated images where platforms require it, no invented claims about [product].
