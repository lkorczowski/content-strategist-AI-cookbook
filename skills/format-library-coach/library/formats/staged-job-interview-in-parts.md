# Staged job interview in parts

## Shape

A staged job interview plays out across a table: the founder or boss on one side, an unusual candidate on the other. One textbox stays on top for the whole video ("Tough interview with this candidate"), and a short "part 2" label flashes in the centre for a few seconds. Each part ends at a moment that makes viewers want the next one.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: both people in frame, the textbox on top, the first absurd answer already landing.
2. 2 to 25 s: questions and answers, cut hard, with the candidate escalating.
3. 25 to 50 s: the turn: the candidate reveals something unexpected (they already run a rival company, they want the boss's job).
4. Last seconds: the cliffhanger for the next part, or the verdict.

## Make it yours

The candidate is a character that embodies your market. A recruiting software startup interviews a candidate who answers every question with a buzzword. An AI coding assistant interviews a developer who insists on writing everything by hand. A fintech for freelancers interviews someone who wants to be paid in exposure. The interviewer stays calm and sure of the business, which makes the candidate funnier. This shape was shot in a real program, as a series of up to four parts.

## Variations

- Voice change: pitch the interviewer's voice up or down so the character reads younger or older.
- Reverse interview: the candidate grills the founder about the company.
- Breakup version: the same table, but a staged breakup between a customer and an old tool.

## Why it works

Job interviews are a situation everyone has lived, so the setup is understood in one second. Splitting it into parts leaves the outcome open, so viewers look for the next part and the earlier ones (Zeigarnik 1927).

## Watch out

- Make it obvious the scene is staged: a real candidate must never be filmed or mocked.
- Remove real brand names and exact numbers from the dialogue.
- Cut every hesitant moment: the interviewer must sound fully confident.
