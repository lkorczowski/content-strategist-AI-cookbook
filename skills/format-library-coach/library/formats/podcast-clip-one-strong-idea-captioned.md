# Podcast clip: one strong idea, captioned

## Shape

The strongest minute of a long conversation, cut so the first words are the claim itself. Word-by-word captions run across the screen, and the edit switches between speakers on a studio set. The clip ends when the idea lands.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the claim, with no setup and no greeting.
2. 2 to 40 s: the reasoning or the story behind it, tight cuts, filler removed.
3. 40 to 90 s: the turn: the example, the number from the guest or the emotional moment.
4. Last seconds: the line that closes the idea, then cut. No outro.

## Make it yours

If you record a podcast, a webinar or long interviews, each episode holds several shorts. Look for the moment where you say something your market argues about. A founder of an AI legal assistant clips the minute where they explain why most contracts are never read. A developer tools CEO clips a guest's story of a failed launch. A founder of a climate data startup clips the moment a customer describes the problem in their own words. The [statur-clipping](../../../statur-clipping/) skill helps you find and cut these moments.

## Variations

- Guest focus: clip the guest's best answer, with your question kept to one line.
- Emotional moment: keep the pause and the silence before the answer.
- Process clip: one guest explains a numbered method, captioned step by step.

## Why it works

Starting on the claim opens a question the rest of the clip answers (Loewenstein 1994). A strong idea in a real voice is easy to share because it makes the sharer look thoughtful (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- Clip only conversations you own or have written permission to clip, and tell guests how their clips will be used.
- Keep the meaning intact. A cut that changes what someone said damages trust fast.
- Check captions word by word; errors read as carelessness.

## For your AI

```
From this transcript of [episode or call], find podcast clips for [company], speaking to [audience].
1. List the 5 strongest 45 to 120 second moments: start and end timestamps, the opening claim, and why it would stop [audience].
2. For the best one, write: the exact first line, the cuts to remove filler, the turn, the closing line, and caption notes (word by word, highlight words).
3. Suggest one on-screen title under 8 words.
Rules: only content we own or have permission to clip, keep each speaker's meaning intact, no added claims.
```
