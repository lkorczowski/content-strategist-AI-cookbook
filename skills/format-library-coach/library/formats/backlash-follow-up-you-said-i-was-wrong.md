# Backlash follow-up: you said I was wrong

## Shape

After a video triggers pushback, the creator comes back with the critical comments on screen. They say plainly what they got right and what they got wrong. Then they settle the question with evidence: a source, a demo or a term the critics used.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the opening that names the clash ("I made a video saying X and you jumped on me").
2. 2 to 30 s: two or three critical comments on screen, read out loud.
3. 30 to 90 s: the evidence. What the critics got right, what you stand by, and the proof for each.
4. Last seconds: the corrected one-line takeaway, and an invitation to keep the debate going.

## Make it yours

You need a first video that people argued with. Then you film the follow-up within days, while the thread is still alive: an AI scheduling tool founder who said "stop taking meetings before 10" answers the people who work across time zones, a pricing tool founder who said "never offer a free plan" shows when free plans work, a cybersecurity startup founder who called a habit risky shows the data behind it. Owning the part you got wrong is the strongest moment of the video.

## Variations

- Deleted video version: explain why you took a video down and what you learned (see the second example).
- Expert term reveal: the critics were right, and there is a name for what they meant.
- Stitch the original: open on your own first video, then cut to the comments.

## Why it works

A public disagreement is a story with two sides, and viewers who saw the first video want to know how it ends (Zeigarnik 1927). A creator who admits a mistake on camera is rare enough to be worth sharing. A second video on a heated topic can reach people who missed the first.

## Watch out

- Hide usernames and profile photos of the critics, and keep the focus on the argument.
- Bring real evidence. A follow-up that repeats the claim louder restarts the fight.
- If the controversy involves an ad or a paid partnership, say so clearly.

## For your AI

```
Script a "backlash follow-up" video for me, founder of [company] ([product] for [audience]).
My first video said: [claim]. The main criticisms in the comments: [paste 5 to 10 comments].
1. Sort the criticisms: where they are right, where I stand by my claim, where it depends.
2. Write the opening line (names the clash in under 15 words), pick 2 or 3 comments to show, and write the evidence for each: [sources or data I have].
3. Write the corrected one-line takeaway and the closing line.
4. Add shot notes: comment overlays, greenscreen of sources, facecam.
Rules: admit what I got wrong first, hide commenters' names, no invented sources or numbers, calm tone.
```
