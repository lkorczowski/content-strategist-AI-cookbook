# Map explainer

## Shape

A map fills the screen and a voice explains a crisis, a market or a culture through it. The map zooms in on a place, draws a flow (a river, a trade route, a supply chain) or colors regions as the story moves. The speaker can stand in front of the map with a greenscreen or stay off screen.

## Beats

1. 0 to 3 s: the map and the question, with the area already highlighted ("Why is this river running dry?").
2. 3 to 60 s: the zoom in, with the key places named and marked.
3. 60 to 240 s: the flow or the change over time, animated on the map, with the reason behind it.
4. Last seconds: zoom out to the full map and one sentence on what it means next.

## Make it yours

Draw the map of your market or of where your users are. A freight software company maps where goods get stuck. An AI agriculture startup maps where a crop is under threat and why. A remote hiring platform maps where developers live versus where companies hire. The map makes an abstract market concrete, and your expertise shows in what you choose to highlight.

## Variations

- Greenscreen host: you stand in front of the map and point.
- Your own data: a map built from anonymized usage of your product.
- Series: one region or one country per episode, same intro.

## Why it works

A map gives the viewer a place to look while the voice explains, so complex topics feel simple. Learning why a place works the way it does is the kind of fact people repeat in conversation, which makes it shareable.

## Watch out

- Use map data and images within their license; many map tiles need attribution.
- Borders and place names can be sensitive. Check them against a neutral reference.
- Product usage maps must be anonymized and must reveal nothing about specific customers.

## For your AI

```
Write a "Map explainer" video for [company], which works in [market] for [audience].
1. Propose 5 topics in [market] that a map explains better than words: a flow, a crisis, a cluster of users, a regional difference.
2. For the best one, write the opening question with the area to highlight, then the script in 4 parts: zoom in, key places, flow or change, zoom out.
3. Next to each sentence, describe the map action (zoom, highlight, arrow, color).
4. List the data sources and the map license to credit.
Rules: licensed maps with attribution, neutral borders and names, anonymized product data only, 60 to 300 seconds.
```
