# Who is this person? (photo explainer)

## Shape

A photo of a person fills the background, and the speaker opens with "So this is [name]". They say who the person is, the one thing they changed, and why it still matters today, in simple words. More photos and documents follow as the story moves forward.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the photo and "So this is [name]", followed by one line on why they matter.
2. 2 to 40 s: who they were, where they came from, one telling detail.
3. 40 to 140 s: the one thing they changed, explained simply, with one example.
4. Last seconds: why it matters today, linked to the viewer's world.

## Make it yours

Every field has people who shaped it whom your audience has never heard of. You introduce them, and you become the person who knows the history. A founder of an AI search tool explains the researcher behind the idea of ranking pages. A founder of a spreadsheet tool for finance teams explains the person who invented the spreadsheet. A founder of a privacy tool explains the person behind a key privacy law. Pick people whose work connects directly to the problem your product solves.

## Variations

- Living person: a current figure in your industry, explained before a big event.
- Unknown hero: someone whose name nobody knows but whose invention everybody uses.
- Two photos: two people whose rivalry shaped your field.

## Why it works

A face on screen and an unfamiliar name create a simple question, "who is this?", that the viewer stays to answer (Loewenstein 1994). Knowing the story behind a field gives viewers something to retell at work.

## Watch out

- Use photos you have the right to use: public domain, licensed, or used within fair use rules.
- Stick to documented facts about real people, and avoid claims that could be defamatory.
- Take extra care with living private individuals; prefer public figures and public records.
