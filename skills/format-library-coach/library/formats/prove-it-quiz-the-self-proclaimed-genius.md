# Prove-it quiz: the self-proclaimed genius

## Shape

A person boasts about being very smart, with a school or an IQ as proof. The interviewer answers with a real technical question from a field, word for word. The answer, right or wrong, is the payoff, and the correct reasoning closes the video.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the boast, in the person's words ("I went to Stanford... I'm pretty smart").
2. 2 to 10 s: the interviewer's comeback, "Tell me this, genius", and the start of a real question (a patient case, an engineering problem).
3. 10 to 50 s: the person thinks aloud. Keep the hesitations and the guesses.
4. Last 10 to 20 s: the answer and the verdict, then the correct reasoning in one or two lines, spoken or on screen.

## Make it yours

The question comes from the field your product serves, so the video shows the depth of that field. An AI study tool for medical students asks a clinical case. An AI coding assistant asks a short debugging question. A legal research tool asks a contract question a first-year lawyer should know. You can close with one line saying your product answered the same question, as long as that is true. The person in frame can be an actor, as long as the video is labelled as staged.

## Variations

- Real expert: ask a person who claims a job title, with a question from that job.
- Viewer round: freeze before the answer and ask viewers to comment theirs.
- Series: same boast pattern, a new field each episode.

## Why it works

The boast creates a small stake: the viewer wants to know whether the person can back it up, and that question holds until the answer (Loewenstein 1994). The real question also teaches something, which gives a reason to share it.

## Watch out

- If the scene is staged, say so in the caption or on screen. Presenting actors as strangers misleads viewers.
- Check the question and the answer with a qualified person, especially for health, law or safety topics.
- Disclose that the account belongs to the company.

## For your AI

```
Plan a "prove-it quiz: the self-proclaimed genius" video for [company], which makes [product] for [field].
1. Write 5 boasts a person might make (school, IQ, job title) and, for each, one real [field] question that can be answered in under 30 seconds. Give the correct answer and the reasoning in 2 lines.
2. For the best pair, write the script: boast, comeback line, question word for word, room for hesitation, answer, verdict, reasoning card.
3. Say whether to cast an actor or a real volunteer, and write the on-screen label if it is staged.
Rules: question and answer checked by a [field] professional, staged scenes labelled as staged, no humiliation, disclose that the account belongs to [company].
```
