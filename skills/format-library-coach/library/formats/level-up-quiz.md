# Level-up quiz

## Shape

The screen shows three items and one rule: "If you know one of these, go to the next level." Each round shows three harder items. The viewer keeps going until they drop out, then comments the level they reached.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the rule and level 1 on screen, with three items almost everyone knows.
2. 2 to 30 s: levels 2 to 5, one every few seconds, three items each, harder each time.
3. 30 to 60 s: the expert levels, where only insiders recognize anything. Slow down a little here.
4. Last seconds: the final level with three items only a handful of people know, then "Comment your level."

## Make it yours

Choose items your audience knows by heart, in order of rarity. A developer tool runs levels of error messages or keyboard shortcuts. A growth analytics SaaS runs levels of marketing metrics, from clicks to cohort retention. An AI research tool runs levels of famous papers or model names. You need no face: text on a plain background or over screenshots carries it. The comment section becomes a list of people sorted by expertise, which tells you who your audience is.

## Variations

- Facecam host: you read each level and react to how hard it gets.
- Product levels: items are features of your category, the last level is a feature only power users find.
- Reverse: start at the hardest level and go down.

## Why it works

Reaching a high level is a small badge, and commenting it gives the viewer social currency in front of peers (Berger 2013). People stay for the next round to see where they drop out, and they compare levels with friends they tag.

## Watch out

- The difficulty must rise steadily. A jump that is too fast loses everyone early.
- Logos, film posters and screenshots of other brands can raise rights issues; use names in text when in doubt.
- Check every item is real and spelled right: experts will spot a mistake.

## For your AI

```
Write a "Level-up quiz" video for [company], whose audience is [audience].
1. Pick the topic: [terms, tools, errors, references] that [audience] knows.
2. Build 8 levels of 3 items each, from items 90 percent of the audience knows to items only experts know. Explain in one line why each level is harder.
3. Give the on-screen rule (under 12 words), the timing per level, and shot notes (text-only background or facecam host).
4. End with the call to comment the level reached.
Rules: steady difficulty, every item real and correctly spelled, text instead of third-party logos when rights are unclear, 45 to 75 seconds.
```
