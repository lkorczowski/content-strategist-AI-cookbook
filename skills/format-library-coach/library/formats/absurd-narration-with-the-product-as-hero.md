# Absurd narration with the product as hero

## Shape

A deadpan or absurd voice tells a made-up epic about an ordinary situation, over simple footage. The story escalates far beyond what the images show. The product or the team becomes the hero who saves the day, in the same overblown tone (the "brainrot" register).

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the voice opens with "Imagine you're..." and an odd point of view.
2. 2 to 30 s: the ordinary situation told as a drama, each line more exaggerated.
3. 30 to 70 s: the crisis peaks, and the hero (product or team) arrives.
4. Last seconds: a grand, absurd conclusion over a very ordinary final shot.

## Make it yours

Tell your user's day as an epic and cast the product as the hero. An AI inbox assistant narrates the life of an email lost under 400 others until the assistant rescues it. A cloud backup startup narrates a laptop's last minutes before the coffee spill, saved by the backup. An expense app narrates the tragic journey of a crumpled receipt. All you need is phone footage and a voice with perfect timing.

## Variations

- Object point of view: the story is told by an object in the office.
- Nature documentary: the narration copies the tone of a wildlife film.
- Series: the same narrator, a new ordinary drama each week.

## Why it works

The gap between the grand voice and the plain footage is the joke, and the escalation keeps the viewer waiting for how far it will go. Absurd stories are easy to retell, which helps sharing (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- If you use an AI voice, follow platform rules on synthetic media labels and use a voice you have rights to.
- Keep the hero moment brief. A long product pitch breaks the tone.
- Absurd humour ages fast; refresh the register when it stops landing.

## For your AI

```
Write an "absurd narration" video for [company], whose product [product] helps [audience] with [problem].
1. Propose 5 ordinary moments linked to [problem] that could become a made-up epic.
2. For the best one, write the voice-over (40 to 90 s): the "Imagine you're..." opening, 4 to 6 escalating lines, the hero entrance of [product], the grand conclusion. Add simple b-roll notes for each line.
Rules: brief hero moment, label AI voices where platforms require it, voices we have rights to, no invented product claims.
```
