# Off-camera interview: the story of one order (big-claim textbox)

## Shape

The founder sits or stands where the business happens and answers an interviewer who stays off camera. They tell the story of one order: who called, what they asked for, what went wrong or what it cost. A big claim sits on screen as a textbox the whole time, and the best stories continue as part 2 and part 3.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the founder mid-sentence, with a bold claim textbox on top ("This client almost cancelled over one email").
2. 2 to 40 s: the setup of the order, in plain words: the client type, the request, the deadline.
3. 40 to 70 s: the turn: the problem, the number, the strange detail. Cut to b-roll of the place and the product at each sentence.
4. Last seconds: the open end that sets up the next part, or the outcome in one line.

## Make it yours

You record one long interview, 20 minutes or more, and cut it into several stories. The last part of the session often holds the best ones, once you are relaxed. An AI agent startup tells the story of the customer who asked it to run their whole support queue in a weekend. A data platform tells how one enterprise deal almost died over a single security question. A dev tools company tells the night a client's launch depended on their API. This shape was shot in a real program, and the story serial was the format that program decided to repeat.

## Variations

- Serial: the strongest story becomes three parts, each ending on the next question.
- Price reveal: the interviewer asks "how much does that cost?", and the founder breaks it down.
- Two voices: a cofounder adds one detail the founder left out.

## Why it works

The claim textbox opens an information gap in the first second, and the story closes it slowly (Loewenstein 1994). A part 2 leaves the task unfinished, so viewers come back for the ending (Zeigarnik 1927).

## Watch out

- Never name or identify a client without written consent.
- Every number on screen must be true and checkable.
- Keep the brand name for later in the video; an early pitch makes people scroll.

## For your AI

```
Plan an off-camera interview video for [company], which sells [product] to [audience]. I will paste the transcript of a long interview with [founder].
1. List the 5 strongest stories about a single order or client: who, what they asked, the twist, the outcome.
2. For the best one, write the claim textbox (under 10 words, a number only if the transcript gives it), the cut list (where to start and end, which sentences to keep) and the b-roll to place on each sentence.
3. If the story is long, split it into parts and write the last line of each part as a cliffhanger.
Rules: no client name or detail that identifies them without consent, numbers only from the transcript, the company name appears after the first 10 seconds.
```
