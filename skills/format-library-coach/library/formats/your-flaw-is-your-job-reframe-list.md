# Your flaw is your job (reframe list)

## Shape

A fast list of flaws people are told they have, each one answered at once by a job or a move that turns it into an asset ("You talk too much. Start a podcast."). The speaker delivers it to camera with a steady rhythm. Each pair takes a few seconds.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the first pair, delivered at once, with no introduction.
2. 2 to 30 s: four to six more pairs, same rhythm, each flaw as text on screen.
3. 30 to 60 s: the turn. The most personal flaw, said slower, with the strongest reframe.
4. Last seconds: a closing line for the viewer ("Send this to the friend who...").

## Make it yours

Write the flaws of your user persona and answer them with uses of your product or roles in your team. An AI research assistant for analysts: "You read everything twice. Become the fact-checker your team relies on." A project management tool for agencies: "You panic when plans change. You'd make a great operations lead." A hiring post for a startup: each flaw becomes a role you are recruiting for, and the last line points to the jobs page.

## Variations

- Hiring post: flaws answered by open roles at your company.
- Product uses: flaws of your buyers answered by what they can do with your product.
- Inverse: strengths people brag about, answered by the hidden cost.

## Why it works

Viewers recognize themselves or a friend in one of the flaws and send the video to that person, which is the relatable trigger. The reframe turns a criticism into pride, and sharing it makes the sender look kind and perceptive (Berger 2013, emotion and social currency).

## Watch out

- Avoid flaws tied to health, disability or identity. Keep them about habits and personality.
- The reframes must be real options. A forced reframe loses the share.
- A hiring post must lead to real open roles.
