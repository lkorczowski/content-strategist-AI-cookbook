# Pop culture characters as your industry's archetypes

## Shape

The speaker takes the cast of a famous film or series and maps each character to a type of person in the viewer's world: employees, customers, investors. A still of each character appears behind the speaker while they describe the matching type in one or two lines. The video ends on the character the viewer is most likely to be, or to work with.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the premise in one line ("each character of [film] is a type of [person in your world]") with the cast on screen.
2. 2 to 20 s: the first character, the most obvious match, to set the game.
3. 20 to 100 s: four to six more characters, one type each, with a concrete behavior for each.
4. Last seconds: the twist character, or a question: "which one are you?"

## Make it yours

You pick a cast your audience knows and map it to the people your product deals with every day. An HR software startup maps a fantasy saga's cast to employee types. A sales CRM maps a heist film's crew to buyer personas. An AI tool for investor updates maps a family drama's characters to types of investors on a cap table. The behaviors must be specific to your field, so only insiders laugh at the right moments.

## Variations

- Plot as your industry: tell a film's plot as if it were an event in your field (see the second example, which retells a film as a geopolitical conflict).
- Bad versions: each character as the toxic version of a role (see the first example).
- Viewer vote: end with "who did I miss?" and make part 2 from the comments.

## Why it works

Viewers recognize their coworkers or themselves in a character and tag them, which spreads the video through comments. The famous cast gives instant context, so the viewer gets the joke in one second.

## Watch out

- Use stills or clips within fair use and platform rules, or use the platform's own tools. Avoid long film clips and copyrighted music.
- Keep the jokes about roles. Mocking real, identifiable people or groups can turn into harassment.
- Choose a cast your audience actually knows. A niche reference loses most viewers in the first seconds.
