# 24-hour makeover of someone's brand

## Shape

The team picks a small business and redoes its brand, page or shop under a time limit. The video shows the before, the process at speed and the reveal to the owner. The owner's reaction closes it.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the before on screen and the challenge ("we have 24 hours to fix this brand").
2. 2 to 60 s: the diagnosis, then the process in fast cuts: screens, sketches, the team at work, the clock.
3. 60 to 150 s: the reveal to the owner, live, with the before and after side by side.
4. Last 10 s: the owner's reaction and a teaser of the next business.

## Make it yours

Agencies and SaaS for small businesses can run this with their own tool. You pick a real business, set the clock and use your product to do the makeover. Examples: a website builder for restaurants rebuilding a local café's site in a day, an AI logo and branding tool redoing a bakery's identity, an online store platform rebuilding a craft shop's catalog with better photos and descriptions.

## Variations

- Viewers choose: followers nominate the business in the comments.
- Shorter clock: a one hour makeover, filmed in one take.
- Follow-up episode: you come back later to show what changed for the business, with the owner's own words.

## Why it works

The clock creates tension and the before sets up a transformation viewers want to see. The owner's reaction gives the emotional payoff, and the numbered series brings viewers back for the next episode (Zeigarnik 1927).

## Watch out

- Get the owner's written consent to film, to publish and to show their old brand.
- Respect the owner on camera. Show the before as a starting point, without mocking it.
- Show results the owner actually got. Never promise or imply sales they did not see.
