# One number, explained

## Shape

One statistic fills the screen in the first second. The speaker explains what it really means, why most people misread it, and what it changes for the viewer. One number per video, with the source visible.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the number on screen, large, and one line that frames it ("This is the craziest stat in sales I've seen").
2. 2 to 20 s: what it measures, in plain words, and where it comes from.
3. 20 to 45 s: the misreading. What people assume it means, and what it actually says.
4. Last seconds: what the viewer should do or think differently because of it.

## Make it yours

Collect numbers from your market that surprise even your customers. A payroll tool for small businesses explains the share of payroll errors caused by manual entry, from a public report. An AI customer success platform explains a churn figure from its own aggregated data. A recruiting tool explains how long a hiring manager spends on a résumé, with the study linked. The number opens the video, and your product stays in the caption or a closing sentence.

## Variations

- Humour version: the speaker reacts to the number with jokes before explaining it (see the example).
- Greenscreen version: the chart or report page behind you, with the number circled.
- Series: one number per week, same opening line.

## Why it works

A surprising number creates an instant gap between what the viewer expected and what they see, the curiosity driver described by Loewenstein 1994. A number explained clearly is easy to repeat at work, which gives it practical value and social currency (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- The number must have a source the viewer can open. Put it on screen or in the caption.
- Check the date and scope of the study before you generalize.
- Anonymize and aggregate any figure taken from customer data.
