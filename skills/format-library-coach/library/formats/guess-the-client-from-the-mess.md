# Guess the client from the mess

## Shape

Two team members look at what a customer left behind (a cabin, a desk, a cart, a workspace) and guess the type of customer from the clues. Quick rounds, one clue at a time, and a verdict at the end of each. The viewer plays along.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the question on screen and in voice ("old money or new money? Based on how they left it").
2. 2 to 15 s: first clue, first guess, quick back and forth.
3. 15 to 30 s: the decisive clue that settles it.
4. Last seconds: the verdict, then "what would you guess?" for the comments.

## Make it yours

Any business that sees traces of its customers can play. A coworking booking platform guesses the job of whoever booked a meeting room from what is left on the table. A cloud storage startup guesses the profession of a user from an invented, anonymized folder structure. A project management tool guesses the team type from a fake board full of overdue tasks. Build the clues yourself when the real traces are private, and keep the guessing light.

## Variations

- Screen version: guess the user from an invented desktop, inbox or dashboard.
- Viewer round: viewers guess in the comments before the reveal in a second video.
- Speed round: five messes in 30 seconds.

## Why it works

The question invites the viewer to guess before the team does, and the verdict closes that loop (Loewenstein 1994). Customer stereotypes everyone recognizes make people tag the friend who matches.

## Watch out

- Never reveal or hint at real customers. Stage the mess or remove every identifying detail.
- Keep the stereotypes playful. Mocking a group the product serves pushes buyers away.
- Real customer property or data never appears on camera.
