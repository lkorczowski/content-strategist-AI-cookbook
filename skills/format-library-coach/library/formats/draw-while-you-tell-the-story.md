# Draw while you tell the story

## Shape

The camera looks down on hands drawing on paper or a tablet. A voice tells a story or explains a mechanism, and the drawing builds at the same pace as the story. By the end, the finished drawing sums up the whole story in one image.

## Beats

1. 0 to 3 s: the first strokes and the first sentence, which states the stakes ("Two men knew the space shuttle was going to explode").
2. 3 to 90 s: the setup drawn piece by piece, each new element appearing as it is named.
3. 90 to 240 s: the turn or the mechanism, with the drawing connecting the pieces (arrows, labels, a key detail in color).
4. Last seconds: the finished drawing held on screen, and the final sentence.

## Make it yours

No face is needed, so the founder can draw or simply voice it. A data infrastructure startup draws how one request travels through its system. A climate tech company draws the story of the problem it attacks. An AI safety startup draws a famous failure and what it taught the field. Speed up the drawing in editing so the pace follows the voice. The final image can become your thumbnail or a post on its own.

## Variations

- Explainer mode: draw a mechanism (how your product works) instead of a story.
- Customer story: draw one customer's before and after.
- Whiteboard: the founder on camera at a board, for a facecam version.

## Why it works

Each new stroke is a small promise of what comes next, so the viewer waits to see the full picture. A true story with high stakes carries emotion, and the finished drawing gives the brain one image to remember it by.

## Watch out

- Speed matters: record in real time, then speed up the drawing to match the voice.
- For true stories, check the facts and name the source in the caption.
- Simple drawings work. Detailed art slows the story down.

2. Write the voice-over (60 to 300 seconds) with the first sentence stating the stakes.

4. Describe the final image that sums up the story.
Rules: drawing builds at the pace of the voice, simple drawings, true and sourced facts, no face required.
