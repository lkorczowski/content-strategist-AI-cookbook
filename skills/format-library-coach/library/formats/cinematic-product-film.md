# Cinematic product film

## Shape

A short film made with film-grade shots, lighting and music, with very few words. The product is revealed slowly, detail by detail, as if it were an event. It ends on the product in full and one line of text or voice.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: a striking image or one line of voice that sets the mood.
2. 2 to 30 s: details and textures, the product half seen, the music building.
3. 30 to 120 s: the reveal. The product in use, in its best light, at the peak of the music.
4. Last 5 to 10 s: the full product, the name and one line (launch date, tagline or call to action).

## Make it yours

Software is hard to film, so you film what it changes or the moment it is used. A robot or device can be filmed directly. For an app, you film the person, the place and the screen as one scene. Examples: an AI hardware startup filming its device at sunrise like a car launch, a design tool filming a designer's hands and the screen in a dark studio, a fintech app filming the one second a payment lands on a shop counter. The entry's note applies: this format is expensive, so test cheaper formats first and keep it for a launch.

## Variations

- Music edit: no words at all, only cuts on the beat (see the Mospoke example).
- Classic commercial: one demo idea filmed as a TV spot (see the Dyson example).
- Phone cinematic: the same shot list filmed with a phone, good light and a gimbal.

## Why it works

Film language tells the viewer that something important is happening, which creates awe and a sense of event. The slow reveal keeps a question open until the product is shown in full.

## Watch out

- Budget and time: crew, locations and music rights add up fast.
- License the music for commercial use on each platform.
- Show the real product. Renders presented as footage mislead buyers.

## For your AI

```
Write a treatment for a cinematic product film for [company], which makes [product] for [audience].
1. Propose 3 visual concepts: the mood, the place, the moment [product] is used, the music style.
2. For the best one, write the shot list by beat (opening image, details, reveal, final frame), with lighting and camera notes, 30 to 160 seconds.
3. Write the few words: opening line (under 10 words) and closing line.
4. Add a low-budget version of the same film shot on a phone.
Rules: real product footage only, music licensed for commercial use, no invented claims.
```
