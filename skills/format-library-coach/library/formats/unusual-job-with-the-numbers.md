# Unusual job, with the numbers

## Shape

The video shows an operation nobody usually sees, filmed where it happens. A few striking numbers carry it: weight, people, hours, distance. A pun or a light line closes it.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the first number, on screen and in voice, over the most impressive shot.
2. 2 to 40 s: the operation step by step, one number per step.
3. 40 to 100 s: the hardest moment, the one that shows the skill of the team.
4. Last 5 to 10 s: the result and the pun.

## Make it yours

Every company has an operation customers never see. You pick the one with the most impressive numbers and film it on site. For a software company, the "site" can be the data center, the support floor during an outage or the night a migration runs. Examples: an AI data labeling company showing how many people review one dataset and for how long, a logistics SaaS filming the warehouse that uses it at peak season, a cybersecurity startup showing the night of a large incident response. The format suits B2B companies and institutions.

## Variations

- Customer site: you film the operation at a customer's place, with their consent and their numbers.
- Series: one unusual job per episode inside your company.
- Voice-over version: no face, only the operation and the numbers.

## Why it works

Numbers make an invisible job concrete, and each new number resets attention. Viewers share the facts that surprised them, which gives them something interesting to say (Berger 2013, social currency).

## Watch out

- Check every number with the team that does the job.
- Ask before filming people at work, and respect safety rules on site.
- Keep confidential data and customer names off screen.

## For your AI

```
Plan an "unusual job, with the numbers" video for [company], which does [what the company does] for [audience].
1. List 5 operations inside [company] or at a customer's site that people never see.
2. For the best one, list the numbers we need (weight, people, hours, volume) and who can confirm each.
3. Write the opening line built on the most striking number, the step-by-step script with one number per step, the hardest moment, the ending and a light pun.
4. Add shot notes for filming on site, 45 to 120 seconds.
Rules: only numbers confirmed by the team, consent of everyone filmed, no confidential data on screen.
```
