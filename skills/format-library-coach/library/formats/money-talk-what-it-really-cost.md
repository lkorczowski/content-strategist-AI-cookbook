# Money talk: what it really cost

## Shape

The founder opens on a real amount ("330,000 euros is what we paid"). Then comes the breakdown, the part that blew up the budget, and what the viewer should plan for if they try the same thing. Filmed to camera, often where the money was spent.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the amount, said and shown on screen.
2. 2 to 30 s: the breakdown, three or four lines with the real numbers.
3. 30 to 70 s: the surprise. What cost more than planned, and why.
4. Last 10 s: what the viewer should budget for, in one or two sentences.

## Make it yours

Share only numbers you are comfortable making public, and make them useful. A founder of a B2B SaaS shows what the first 100 customers cost, by channel. A founder of an AI startup shows the monthly bill of their model and cloud stack. A founder of a developer tool shows the real cost of their first conference booth. Buyers and peers learn from it, and your transparency becomes part of the brand.

## Variations

- Tool stack: the monthly cost of every tool you pay for, line by line.
- Answer to the comments: the video opens on "You all asked how we...", as in the example.
- Series: one cost per episode (hiring, ads, legal, hosting).

## Why it works

A specific amount in the first second is a strong surprise, and people stay to see how it breaks down. Real numbers are rare and useful, which gives the video practical value and makes the sharer look in the know (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- Only numbers you want public. Investors, partners and employees may be affected.
- Leave out salaries of identifiable people and details covered by contracts.
- Keep the numbers exact. A rounded or inflated figure will be called out.
