# Blind test

## Shape

Two things are compared without saying which is which. A person tries both, judges them out loud and picks one. The reveal shows which was which, and the person's reaction closes the video.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the two options, labelled A and B, and the challenge in a few words ("cheese or not cheese?").
2. 2 to 20 s: the person tries A, then B, and comments on each.
3. 20 to 45 s: the person hesitates, then commits to a choice with one reason.
4. Last 5 to 15 s: the reveal, the reaction, and the result on screen.

## Make it yours

For software, hide the labels and let a real user judge the output. An AI writing tool shows two emails, one written by a person and one by the tool, and asks a sales manager which one they would send. An AI image upscaler shows two photos and asks a photographer which is the original. A translation startup plays two versions of the same sentence to a native speaker. You can be the host or stay off camera. The person judging should be someone your buyers trust, like a professional of the field.

## Variations

- Series of judges: the same two options, five people, one tally at the end.
- Viewer vote: show both options, ask viewers to comment A or B, reveal in a follow-up video.
- Three-way blind: add a third option to make the guess harder.

## Why it works

Viewers make their own guess before the reveal, so they stay to see if they were right (Loewenstein 1994). The reveal is a fair surprise: nobody knew the answer, including the judge.

## Watch out

- The test must be truly blind. Hide labels, logos and any clue in the setup.
- Keep every result, even the ones where your product loses, or the series loses credibility.
- Get consent from the judges and disclose that the account belongs to the company.
