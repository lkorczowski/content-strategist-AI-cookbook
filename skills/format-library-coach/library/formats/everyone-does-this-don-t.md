# Everyone does this, don't

## Shape

The expert points at a common setup, the way almost everyone does it. They say it is exactly what to avoid and explain why in one sentence. Then they show the better way on site, with the result visible.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the common setup on screen and the line "everyone does this".
2. 2 to 20 s: why it is a problem, with one concrete consequence the viewer has felt.
3. 20 to 55 s: the better way, shown step by step where the work happens.
4. Last 10 s: the result side by side with the common setup, and a closing line.

## Make it yours

Your buyers have a default setup in their tools or their workflow that costs them time or money. You film it as it is in real offices, then show the better way, ideally with your expertise and possibly your product. Examples: a CRM founder on the pipeline stages almost every sales team gets wrong, an analytics tool founder on the dashboard everyone builds first, an AI support tool founder on the help center structure that makes bots answer badly.

## Variations

- Screen version: the "site" is a software screen, recorded as a screen capture.
- Series: one common setup per episode, same opening line.
- Viewer setup: a follower sends a photo or recording of their setup and you fix it.

## Why it works

Seeing your own setup called a mistake creates instant tension, and the fix gives practical value people save and send to colleagues (Berger 2013). The concrete consequence makes the advice feel personal.

## Watch out

- Be sure the better way is better for most people. Name the case where the common setup is fine.
- Film your own setups or ask permission before filming someone's office or home.
- Keep the tone helpful. Mocking viewers for their setup drives them away.
