# Public spectacle stunt

## Shape

A spectacle happens in a public place: a flash mob, a machine in a stadium, an event announced out of nowhere. Several cameras film the action and the crowd's reaction. The scale of the moment is the message, and the brand appears at the end or in the caption.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the place and the first sign that something unusual is happening.
2. 2 to 20 s: the spectacle starts, and more people join or notice.
3. 20 to 150 s: the peak, with wide shots for the scale and close-ups on the crowd's faces.
4. Last 5 to 15 s: the end of the moment, the crowd's reaction, and who made it happen.

## Make it yours

The stunt makes your product visible in a place where your audience already is. A robotics startup brings its machine to a sports event and films the reactions. An AI music startup gathers musicians to play a piece the product composed, in a train station. A startup for remote teams announces a public meetup in a city and films who shows up. The stunt must say something about the product: the scale or the surprise should mirror what the product does.

## Variations

- Machine in the crowd: a robot or a device in a crowded place, filmed with the reactions.
- Outrageous announcement: an employee announces an over-the-top public meetup, and the video is the announcement itself.
- Small scale: one surprising act in one street, filmed with three cameras.

## Why it works

A crowd reacting tells the viewer that something worth watching is happening, and strong emotions like awe and joy are the ones people share (Berger 2013). The surprise in a familiar place resets attention.

## Watch out

- Get permits for public spaces and private venues, and check the rules of the event or stadium.
- Music, logos and broadcast footage carry rights. Clear them before posting.
- Get releases for recognizable people in close-ups, and keep the stunt safe for the crowd.
