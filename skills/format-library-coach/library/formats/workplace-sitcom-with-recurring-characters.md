# Workplace sitcom with recurring characters

## Shape

The real team plays itself in short episodes shot in the real office. Each episode has one situation from the job and one punchline, usually between two people in frame. The same characters return, so viewers start following them like a show.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: two characters in frame, and a line that sets the situation (a promotion, a deal, a complaint).
2. 2 to 30 s: the situation plays out, true to how the job really works.
3. 30 to 70 s: the twist, often a misunderstanding or a role reversal.
4. Last seconds: the punchline from the character viewers already know.

## Make it yours

Your team, your office and your trade are the set. Cast the real roles: the founder, the salesperson, the support lead, the engineer. A B2B payments startup runs a series about the sales rep who celebrates every closed deal too loudly. An AI customer support company films the support lead versus the angry-customer simulator. A logistics software team plays the ops manager who sees every problem before it happens. Each episode shows how the business works, so the comedy also teaches what you sell.

## Variations

- Real metric as plot: an episode built around a real milestone the team just hit.
- Guest character: a customer or partner joins for one episode, with their consent.
- Role swap: two characters trade jobs for a day.

## Why it works

Recurring characters create a reason to come back, because each episode feels like an open chapter (Zeigarnik 1927). Viewers who work in similar teams recognize the situations and share them with colleagues.

## Watch out

- Every team member who appears must agree, and must be able to stop appearing without consequences.
- Keep real customers, deals and numbers anonymous unless they agreed to be shown.
- Do not let the comedy make your service look careless or unreliable.

1. Define 3 to 4 recurring characters based on those roles: one trait each, one catchphrase.
2. Write 5 episode ideas, each with one real situation from our job and one punchline.
3. Script the best episode: setup line, situation, twist, punchline, with two-shot camera notes and office locations.
Rules: every person on camera consents, real customers and numbers stay anonymous unless they agreed, the comedy must not make our service look unreliable.
