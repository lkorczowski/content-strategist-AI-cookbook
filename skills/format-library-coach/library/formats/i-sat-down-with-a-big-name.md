# I sat down with a big name

## Shape

The video opens with "I sat down with [famous person]" and drops the most surprising claim of the interview right away. Then it gives the context: who the guest is and why their opinion matters. It ends on one follow-up question and the guest's answer.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the guest on screen, the line "I sat down with...", then the guest's boldest claim.
2. 2 to 20 s: the context in one or two sentences: who they are, what they built.
3. 20 to 90 s: the follow-up question and the guest's reasoning.
4. Last 10 s: a closing line that sends to the full interview or opens a question for the comments.

## Make it yours

You interview people your buyers want to hear from, and you cut the strongest moment as a short. The guest does not have to be famous to everyone, only to your market. Examples: an AI security founder interviewing a well-known CISO, a fintech founder sitting with an investor known in the field, a vertical SaaS founder interviewing the most respected expert of the industry they serve. The entry also suggests interviewing a customer or an investor.

## Variations

- Customer guest: a well-known customer tells their view on the market.
- Audio clip: a podcast recording with a still image and captions.
- Series: one guest per week, same opening line.

## Why it works

A big name gives viewers a reason to stop and a reason to share ("look who said this"), which is social currency (Berger 2013). The surprising claim first opens a question the context then answers.

## Watch out

- Quote the guest accurately and keep the claim in its context. Cutting a sentence to change its meaning breaks trust and may break the guest relationship.
- Get the guest's agreement to publish clips on each platform.
- Fact-check bold claims, or frame them clearly as the guest's opinion.
