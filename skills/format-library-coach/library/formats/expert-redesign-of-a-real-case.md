# Expert redesign of a real case

## Shape

The video opens on a real person's problem in one sentence. The expert shows the current state (a plan, a screen, a room), diagnoses what goes wrong, then builds the redesign step by step. It ends on the before and after side by side.

## Beats

1. 0 to 3 s: the problem stated with its paradox ("120 square meters and nothing works") over the current state.
2. 3 to 60 s: who asked for help and the diagnosis: three things that break, pointed at on the plan or the screen.
3. 60 to 200 s: the redesign, one decision at a time, each with its reason.
4. Last 10 to 20 s: before and after side by side, and the one principle the viewer can apply at home.

## Make it yours

Audit a user's real setup or workflow, with their permission. A CRM for agencies redesigns a messy pipeline a user shared. A website builder for restaurants redoes a menu page live. An analytics tool rebuilds a founder's dashboard from scratch. You can do it with your product or without it: the diagnosis proves your expertise, and the redesign shows what good looks like in your category. This sits close to a demo, so it works for people already considering you.

## Variations

- Constraint redesign: improve it without replacing anything (see the kitchen example).
- Speed edition: diagnosis and redesign in 60 seconds, for a shorter cut.
- Viewer submission: people send their case in the comments, you pick one per week.

## Why it works

The before and after promises a visible payoff, so people stay through the diagnosis to see it. Each design decision comes with a reason, which gives the viewer practical value they can reuse on their own case (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- Get written permission from the person whose case you show, and hide names, emails and private data.
- Respect the original: diagnose the setup, never mock the person who built it.
- If the person is a customer of yours, say so on screen.

## For your AI

```
Plan an "Expert redesign of a real case" video for [company], which helps [audience] with [problem].
1. Describe the case: [the user's setup, workflow or page, shared with permission].
2. Write the opening line as a paradox (what they have vs what goes wrong, under 12 words).
3. List the 3 main problems of the current state, each with what to point at on screen.
4. Write the redesign as 4 to 6 decisions, each with one sentence of reason, and the before and after shot.
5. End with one principle viewers can apply without [product].
Rules: written permission from the person, hide private data, no mocking, say if they are a customer, 90 to 240 seconds.
```
