# Read the label

## Shape

The expert holds a product or shows its label, terms or spec sheet, and reads it line by line. Each line gets a short deadpan comment, often mocking the fear people attach to harmless items. The expert separates real issues from fear-mongering and ends with a calm verdict.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the product in hand and "Let's see what's inside".
2. 2 to 30 s: the first lines, with deadpan comments on the harmless ones ("Water. Spooky.").
3. 30 to 90 s: the line that actually matters, explained seriously, with why it matters.
4. Last seconds: the verdict in one sentence.

## Make it yours

Your category has its own labels: pricing pages, terms of service, data policies, spec sheets. You read them with an expert eye. A privacy tool founder reads a typical app's data policy line by line. A payroll software founder reads the fine print of a standard contractor agreement. An AI model evaluation startup reads a model's spec sheet and explains which numbers matter. Read generic or anonymized documents, or your own, and teach viewers what to look for.

## Variations

- Your own label: read your product's terms or pricing page and explain every line.
- Viewer request: viewers send a label in the comments, you read it in the next video.
- Speed run: ten lines in thirty seconds, one word of verdict each.

## Why it works

The deadpan comments make the viewer laugh at the panic around harmless items, and the serious line builds trust because the expert clearly separates fear from fact. Viewers save the video as a guide for the next label they read (Berger 2013, practical value).

## Watch out

- Never defame a named competitor. Read a generic example or your own product, or stick to verifiable facts.
- Stay accurate on health, finance or legal claims, and point to a professional for personal advice.
- Show documents within copyright rules, and hide any personal data.
