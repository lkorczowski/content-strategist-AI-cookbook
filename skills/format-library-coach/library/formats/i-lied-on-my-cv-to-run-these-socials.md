# I lied on my CV to run these socials

## Shape

A long textbox sits over existing footage of the company: the person behind the account confesses they lied on their CV to get the job and now must run the social media of an unlikely boss or business. The footage shows that boss or business doing its thing. The text ends with a plea: "help me keep my job".

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the first line of the confession on screen, over the most striking shot.
2. 2 to 10 s: the rest of the text, describing the boss or the business with affection and exaggeration.
3. 10 to 25 s: shots that prove the description: the boss in action, the product, the team.
4. Last seconds: the plea to like, follow or comment, on the funniest shot.

## Make it yours

The joke works when the business looks hard to market at first sight. A tax software company writes "I lied on my CV and now I run the socials of a tax engine. Make taxes fun or I get fired." A deep tech startup writes it about a founder who only talks in equations. A B2B logistics platform writes it about a warehouse robot. No shoot is needed: you pick footage you already have and write the text. This shape was shot in a real program, as an edit of existing rushes.

## Variations

- Series: each post is a new attempt to "save the job", with a new idea.
- Boss reply: the boss answers in the next video, deadpan.
- Comment rescue: viewers suggest ideas, and the best one is shot next.

## Why it works

The confession is self-deprecating and puts the viewer in the role of helper, which invites likes and comments. Many people know the feeling of faking it at a new job, so the text feels relatable and gets shared.

## Watch out

- Keep the tone affectionate toward the boss and the product.
- The text is the hook: write it short enough to read in the time it stays on screen.
- Use a sound from the platform library.
