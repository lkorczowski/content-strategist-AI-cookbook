# Hey Boss: the comment question, asked on the shop floor

## Shape

An employee walks up to the boss in the middle of work, phone in hand, and reads a question from the comments out loud ("Hey boss, someone asks..."). The boss answers on the spot, often by showing the product or the machine. The comment appears on screen while it is read.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the employee interrupts the boss mid-task with "Hey boss", the comment shown on screen.
2. 2 to 15 s: the question read out loud, the boss's first reaction.
3. 15 to 45 s: the answer, shown with the product, the machine or the process.
4. Last seconds: a short closing line from the boss, and the employee already looking for the next comment.

## Make it yours

This format was created by Dr. Louis Korczowski and tested in a real program. It fits any company where work is visible: factories, workshops, kitchens, labs, hardware teams. A robotics startup answers "can it pick up an egg?" next to the arm. An AI quality-inspection company answers "how does it spot a scratch?" on the line. A 3D printing service answers "what is the biggest part you can print?" in front of the printer. Every comment gives you a new episode, so the series never runs out.

## Variations

- Hard question: the employee picks the most skeptical comment, and the boss answers it with a demo.
- Reverse: the boss reads a comment to an employee who is the real expert.
- Office version: a software founder answers at a screen, with the team around.

## Why it works

Reading a real comment proves the brand listens, and commenters return to see if their question gets picked. The interruption mid-work feels candid, and the answer shown on the machine gives practical value people save (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- Read real comments only, and blur usernames unless the commenter agreed.
- Keep safety rules visible on the shop floor: protective gear, no distraction near running machines.
- Everyone on camera agrees to appear.
