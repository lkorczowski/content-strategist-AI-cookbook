# Tier list of the options in your market

## Shape

The creator ranks the tools, methods or options of a market from best to worst, one item at a time, with one reason each. The tier board stays on screen, behind the speaker or as a carousel. The founder's own product stays out of the ranking, so the video reads as expertise.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the board and the topic ("I ranked every way to do X").
2. 2 to 30 s: the first items, placed fast, one reason each.
3. 30 to 100 s: the surprising placements: a popular option ranked low, an unknown one ranked high, with the reason.
4. Last 5 to 15 s: the top tier, and a question to viewers about what you missed.

## Make it yours

Rank what your buyers compare before they buy, and leave your own product off the board. A founder of an AI analytics startup ranks ways to track marketing results, from spreadsheets to full data stacks. A founder of a scheduling tool ranks meeting habits from best to worst. A founder of a security startup ranks common password practices. Your expertise is the product here: the reasons show how you think. You can batch-shoot several lists in one session, one topic per video.

## Variations

- Batch shoot: film several tier lists in one morning, each on a different topic.
- Carousel: a photo carousel with one tier per slide and no voice.
- Pop culture: rank something famous in your field (iconic launches, famous ads) to reach a wider audience.

## Why it works

Viewers check whether their own choice ranks high, and the surprising placements reset attention (Loewenstein 1994). Sharing a list that confirms your choice, or arguing with it, makes the sharer look informed (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- Give a reason for every rank. A list without reasons reads as opinion and invites rage.
- Keep claims about named products factual and current. Opinions labelled as opinions.
- Keep your own product off the board. Ranking yourself belongs in F114.
