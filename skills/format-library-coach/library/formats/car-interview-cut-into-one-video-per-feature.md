# Car interview cut into one video per feature

## Shape

A teammate in the passenger seat asks the founder questions during a drive, with the phone on a mount. Each answer about one feature becomes its own video, with a claim textbox on top. The answer cuts to a screen recording of that feature in action, then back to the car.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the founder talking in the car, with a claim textbox ("She automated the job she hated most").
2. 2 to 20 s: the problem the feature solves, in the founder's words. The question is cut unless it is needed.
3. 20 to 45 s: the screen recording of the feature, at most a minute, fluid and fast.
4. Last seconds: one closing sentence from the car.

## Make it yours

One drive gives you a week of videos. You list the features you want to explain, ask about one per stop or per stretch of road, and cut afterwards. A field service app gets "it works without signal", "it fills the report from a photo", "it books the next visit". A CRM for agencies gets one video per automation. An AI writing tool gets one video per use case. The car keeps the tone relaxed, so the founder sounds like a person explaining their work. This shape was shot in a real program, built from an earlier car format that had become a template.

## Variations

- Personal drive: the questions are about the founder's story, one memory per video.
- Walk version: the same cut, filmed while walking.
- Customer in the car: a user answers instead of the founder.

## Why it works

The car feels candid, so viewers lower their guard and listen to a demo they would skip as an ad. The claim on screen sets a promise, and the screen recording pays it off within seconds (Loewenstein 1994).

## Watch out

- The driver never holds the phone. Film from the passenger seat or park.
- Cut technical overload: one feature, one benefit per video.
- Borrowed b-roll needs the right to use it.

## For your AI

```
Plan a car interview session for [company], which makes [product] for [audience]. Features to cover: [list].
1. Write one question per feature that makes [founder] tell a real situation, then name the feature.
2. For each feature, write the claim textbox (under 10 words, true), the 2 to 4 sentences to keep from a typical answer, the screen recording to insert (what to click, under 60 seconds) and the closing line.
3. Order the videos for a week of posting.
Rules: claims match what the product does today, no invented numbers, the driver never handles the phone.
```
