# The quiet demo: the tool appears, nobody names it

## Shape

You post a useful video on a topic your buyers care about, and you use your product on screen to get the result. You never name it or point at it. Viewers who notice ask "what tool is that?" in the comments, and the answer goes there.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the useful promise ("how I turn a sales call into a client email" or your own version), face or screen.
2. 2 to 30 s: the steps. The product does the key step on screen, clearly visible, with no comment on it.
3. 30 to 50 s: the result, shown in full.
4. Last seconds: a closing line about the method, with no call to action toward the tool.

## Make it yours

You pick a task your buyers do every week and teach it well. Your product sits inside the workflow where it naturally belongs. The demo works because the tool is visible doing the hard part. Examples: an AI slide tool used while teaching how to structure a pitch, a transcription app used during a video on how to repurpose a podcast, a spreadsheet AI add-on used in a tutorial on cleaning a lead list. The [comment-section-coach](../../../comment-section-coach/) skill covers the demo trick and how to answer in the comments.

## Variations

- Facecam with a screen in the corner: you explain the method, the screen shows the tool.
- Series: same task family, one video a week, the tool always present.
- Pinned answer: once someone asks, you pin your reply.

## Why it works

The unnamed tool creates a small information gap, and curious viewers close it by asking (Loewenstein 1994). The question and its answer in the comments become social proof that other viewers read.

## Watch out

- Disclose your role as founder when you answer in the comments ("I built it").
- Make the video useful without the tool. If the method only works with your product, it reads as a hidden ad.
- Keep the product visible long enough to be noticed.
