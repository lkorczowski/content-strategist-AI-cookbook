# Yes or no, no debate

## Shape

Someone fires short questions about one topic, and the expert answers each one with a single word: yes or no. The questions come fast, one per second or two, and some answers go against what most people believe. The video ends on the last answer, with zero explanation.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the topic and the rule on screen ("AI note-takers, yes or no, no debate") and the first question already asked.
2. 2 to 20 s: rapid questions, one-word answers. Start with easy ones so the viewer gets the rhythm.
3. 20 to 35 s: the surprising answers. Place two or three questions where the honest answer breaks a common belief.
4. Last 5 s: one final question that is funny or bold, answered with a straight face, then a hard cut.

## Make it yours

Pick the topic your buyers argue about and list the questions they ask on sales calls. You answer, a teammate or a friend asks off camera or in a two-shot. An AI bookkeeping tool for freelancers runs "Taxes for freelancers, yes or no". A cybersecurity startup runs "Passwords, yes or no". A no-code app builder runs "Can you build it without a developer, yes or no". The answers you leave unexplained become the comments you reply to next.

## Variations

- Role swap: the founder asks, a customer or an engineer answers.
- Two-shot duel: two experts answer at the same time and the disagreements are the highlight.
- Product edition: every question is about what your product does, for a bottom-of-funnel cut.

## Why it works

Each answer opens a small gap ("why no?") that the video refuses to close, which is the curiosity mechanism Loewenstein 1994 describes. The speed makes the video short enough to rewatch, and the unexplained answers push people to argue in the comments.

## Watch out

- Every one-word answer must be true. A wrong yes or no, even for speed, damages your credibility.
- Avoid health, legal or financial claims that need nuance in one word.
- If you use the trend sound, check it is cleared for business accounts.

## For your AI

```
Write a "Yes or no, no debate" video for [company], which sells [product] to [audience].
1. List 15 questions [audience] really asks about [topic], each answerable with yes or no. Mark the 3 whose honest answer surprises most people.
2. Order 10 of them: 4 easy, 3 surprising, 2 fast, 1 bold closer.
3. Give the opening on-screen text (under 8 words), who asks and who answers, and shot notes (facecam or two-shot, hard cuts, no pauses).
4. Draft one follow-up reply video for the answer most likely to start an argument.
Rules: every answer must be true and defensible, no health, legal or financial advice in one word, no explanations inside the video.
```
