# Photo carousel story

## Shape

A swipeable set of photos, with one line of text per slide. The slides tell a story, a timeline or a list, one step at a time. The last slide is the payoff: the result, the answer or the lesson.

## Beats

1. Slide 1: the hook. A striking image and one line that promises the payoff ("I did X for 40 days" or "how X works in 7 slides").
2. Slides 2 to 4: the setup. Context and the first steps, one idea per slide.
3. Slides 5 to 8: the turn. The part that changes things, or the strongest items of the list.
4. Last slide: the payoff, then an invitation to comment or save.

## Make it yours

You turn a story, a process or a lesson into slides. A carousel needs no filming, so you can make it from screenshots, product photos or your own pictures. Examples: an AI fitness app sharing one user's 30 days in photos, with their consent, a data tool explaining a complex concept in 7 slides with simple charts, a SaaS founder sharing the lessons from their first year, one per slide.

## Variations

- Timeline: one photo per day or week, the change visible at the end (see the face scan example).
- Explainer: a topic explained in a few slides (see the eclipse example).
- Long list: many lessons, one per slide (see the business lessons example).

## Why it works

Each slide is a small open loop, and the promise on slide one keeps people swiping to reach the payoff (Loewenstein 1994). Useful lists get saved and sent, which is practical value (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- Use photos you own or licensed images, and get consent from anyone in them.
- One idea per slide, with text short enough to read in two seconds.
- Make the last slide worth the swipe. A weak payoff makes people stop before the end next time.

## For your AI

```
Write a photo carousel for [company], which serves [audience], about [story, process or lesson].
1. Choose the shape: timeline, explainer or list. Say why.
2. Write the hook line for slide 1 (under 12 words) that promises the payoff.
3. Write one line per slide for 5 to 10 slides, with a note on the photo or screenshot for each.
4. Write the payoff slide and the caption, ending with a question or an invitation to save.
Rules: one idea per slide, text readable in 2 seconds, only images we own or license, consent from anyone pictured, no invented numbers.
```
