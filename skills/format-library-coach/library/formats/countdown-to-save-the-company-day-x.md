# Countdown to save the company (day X)

## Shape

A real deadline the business must beat (a debt, a stock to sell, a closure date) becomes a numbered series. Each episode gives the count, the progress since last time, and one thing viewers can do to help. The series ends when the deadline passes, whatever the outcome.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the stake and the count ("In 30 days, we lose the company", "Part 3 of trying to save...").
2. 2 to 20 s: why it is happening, in two sentences, for new viewers.
3. 20 to 45 s: the progress since the last episode, with the real number.
4. Last 10 s: one action for the viewer (share, buy, tell a friend) and the next episode's day.

## Make it yours

This format only works with a real stake, so keep it for the moment you face one. A bootstrapped SaaS for schools with a runway ending counts down to the date it must reach a revenue target. A small AI hardware company with unsold inventory counts down the units left before a supplier deadline. A family business turned software startup counts down the days to a loan payment. Show the real numbers and the real effort, and say what happens if you miss.

## Variations

- One-off plea: a single video when a sudden threat hits the business (see the third example).
- Sales target: the count is units sold instead of days left (see the second example).
- Family angle: a child or partner tells the story of a parent's company (see the first example).

## Why it works

A real deadline with a visible count creates suspense across episodes, and viewers come back to see if the business makes it (Zeigarnik 1927). The emotion of helping a real person survive pushes people to share and act (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- The stake must be true. A fake countdown destroys trust and can break consumer law.
- Report the outcome, even if you miss the target.
- Asking for money can trigger platform rules on fundraising: check them before you post.
