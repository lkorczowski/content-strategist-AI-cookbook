# Name the behavior nobody named

## Shape

The speaker describes a behavior almost everyone has, in enough detail that the viewer recognizes themselves. Then they give it a name, explain why it happens, and say what to do about it. The name is the part people repeat, comment and share.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the name on screen or the description that makes viewers think "that's me".
2. 2 to 15 s: the behavior described in concrete scenes.
3. 15 to 45 s: the name, then why it happens, in plain words.
4. Last seconds: one thing to do about it, and the name repeated.

## Make it yours

Every market has a behavior nobody has named yet. You name it, and you become the person who owns the word. An AI meeting summary tool names the habit of saying "let's circle back" with no owner. A personal finance app for freelancers names the urge to spend right after a big invoice is paid. A browser extension for focus names the reflex of opening a new tab the moment a task gets hard. Used well, this is a category-creation tool: the problem gets a name, and your product is the answer to it.

## Variations

- Existing term: explain a term psychologists or researchers already use, and credit where it comes from.
- Tag a friend: end with "send this to the person who does this".
- Series: one named behavior per week from your market.

## Why it works

Viewers recognize themselves or someone they know and tag that person, so the video spreads through comments. A name is easy to repeat, and using it makes the sharer look in the know (Berger 2013, social currency).

## Watch out

- The behavior must be common. If only a few viewers recognize it, the name does not stick.
- If you use a term someone else coined, say so. Claiming it as yours damages trust.
- Avoid diagnosing medical or mental health conditions.

```
Help me write a "name the behavior nobody named" video for [company], which makes [product] for [audience].
1. List 10 behaviors my audience shares around [problem area] that are common, specific and slightly embarrassing.
2. For the 3 best, propose a short, memorable name (2 to 4 words) and check whether a term already exists; if it does, tell me who uses it.
3. For the best one, write a 30 to 60 second script: the opening, the behavior in 2 concrete scenes, the name, why it happens, one thing to do, the name repeated.
4. Add shot notes: facecam framing, text on screen for the name.
Rules: credit existing terms, no medical diagnosis, no invented studies or numbers, the product appears at most once at the end.
```
