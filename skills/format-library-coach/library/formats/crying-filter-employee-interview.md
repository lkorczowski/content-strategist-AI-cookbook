# Crying-filter employee interview

## Shape

Someone off camera asks employees, one after another, "do you like working here?". Each answers with enthusiasm while a crying filter on their face says the opposite. The gap between the words and the filter is the joke, and the clip works as light, self-aware employer branding.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the question on screen, first employee already answering with a big smile and the crying filter on.
2. 2 to 20 s: quick cuts, one employee per answer, each more enthusiastic than the last.
3. 20 to 40 s: the funniest answer, from the person viewers least expect (the founder, the new hire).
4. Last seconds: one last "I love it" and cut, or a caption that winks at the joke.

## Make it yours

A startup team can ride this trend in an afternoon. An AI startup in a launch week asks the engineers, each one answering "I love it" with tears streaming. A customer support platform asks its own support team on the busiest day of the year. A fintech scale-up asks the finance team during quarter close. The joke shows a team that does not take itself too seriously, which helps hiring and makes the brand feel human.

## Variations

- Customer version: customers answer "do you like our product?" with the filter, only if they volunteer.
- Founder last: end on the founder, for the self-deprecating punchline.
- Situation version: ask on a specific hard day (launch, audit, move).

## Why it works

The contradiction between the words and the face surprises, and viewers who know that feeling at work recognize themselves and tag colleagues. A known trend shape also tells the viewer instantly how to read the joke.

## Watch out

- Trends expire: check that the sound and filter are still active before shooting.
- Every employee must volunteer. Nobody should feel the joke is about their real working conditions.
- On a business account, check that the sound is cleared for commercial use.
