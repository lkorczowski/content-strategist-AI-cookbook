# The impossible client request (phone call)

## Shape

A client calls with an extreme request, and the call plays on speaker in the office. The team listens, reacts, and works out the solution live, on whiteboards, screens or phones. The video ends on the answer to the client, which shows how the service handles the impossible.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the request, in the client's voice, as the first line ("I need to move 100 tons in one flight").
2. 2 to 20 s: the team's reaction and the constraints said out loud.
3. 20 to 60 s: the solution built step by step: who is called, what is checked, what is booked.
4. Last seconds: the call back to the client with the answer, and the result if you can show it.

## Make it yours

Every B2B service gets wild requests. Reenact one and show how your team solves it. A cloud infrastructure startup plays the call where a client needs to handle a tenfold traffic spike by tomorrow. An AI translation service plays the request to localize a product into twelve languages before a launch. A data migration company plays the client who wants ten years of records moved over a weekend. The solution steps are your product demo, told as a rescue.

## Variations

- Real voicemail: a real request read aloud with the client's permission, details removed.
- Fail and recover: the first idea fails on camera, the second one works.
- Viewer request: viewers comment their wildest request and the team answers it.

## Why it works

An extreme request opens a question, "can they do it?", and the viewer stays to find out (Loewenstein 1994). Watching the steps teaches how the service works, which builds trust before a buyer ever calls.

## Watch out

- Anonymize real requests or reenact them. Get the client's consent before showing anything recognizable.
- Show only solutions you really deliver. An impossible promise on camera becomes a support problem later.
- Keep pricing and contract details out of the call.
