# The dated bet: mark my words

## Shape

The founder opens with a bold prediction about their category, with a date attached ("X won't exist within 20 years"). They name the experience behind the bet, then list the signals that support it. The video closes on what would prove them wrong.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: "Mark my words" and the dated prediction in one sentence.
2. 2 to 10 s: the experience behind it ("after five years of studying...").
3. 10 to 70 s: three or four signals, numbered, each one sentence with what it shows.
4. Last seconds: what would prove the bet wrong, and an invitation to come back on that date.

## Make it yours

Make a bet your product is built on. If you are right, your company wins. An AI legal drafting tool bets that most first drafts of contracts will be written by software within five years. A voice interface startup bets that keyboards will leave most cars within ten years. A developer platform bets that a whole job title disappears by a given year. Your signals come from what you see in your market every week. Save the video and check it on the date.

## Variations

- Skin in the game: the founder says what they put on the line ("I just put my money on it").
- Short horizon: a bet for next year, revisited in a follow-up video.
- Surprises list: after the bet, "four things that surprised me" replace the signals (see the example).

## Why it works

A dated bet is a claim the viewer can hold you to, which makes it feel braver than an opinion. The open question (will it happen?) and the surprising signals pull people into the comments to agree or argue, and sharing a bold prediction makes the sharer look early.

## Watch out

- Bets about stocks or assets can be read as financial advice. Add a clear disclaimer or avoid them.
- Signals must be real and sourced.
- Avoid bets that mock a competitor by name.
