# Two experts rate the hacks (and disagree)

## Shape

Two experts sit side by side facing the camera. A popular hack from their field appears, and both say their score from 0 to 10 at the same moment. When the scores clash, each defends theirs in a sentence, and the disagreements carry the fun.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: both experts in frame and a title that promises friction ("AI coding hacks, rated by two engineers. We didn't agree").
2. 2 to 20 s: first hack, simultaneous scores, one sentence each.
3. 20 to 60 s: three or four more hacks, with one big disagreement where both argue briefly.
4. Last seconds: the hack they both rate highest, or a question asking viewers who was right.

## Make it yours

Two cofounders make the ideal pair: they know the field and they already argue. A two-founder AI coding assistant rates prompt tricks developers share. A founding pair behind a productivity app rates time-management hacks. A clinical founder and a technical founder of a health AI rate wellness hacks from their two angles. The disagreement shows two minds behind the product, and viewers pick a side in the comments.

## Variations

- Founder versus customer: one expert and one user rate the same hacks.
- Blind scores: each writes the score on a card and both flip at once.
- Series: one field or one hack theme per episode, same title format.

## Why it works

Saying scores at the same moment creates a tiny suspense before every reveal, and visible disagreement invites viewers to vote in the comments. The pair gets laughs from real friction, and the useful scores give practical value (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- Staged disagreement shows. Keep the real scores, even when you agree.
- Health and finance hacks need care: say clearly which ones are unsafe.
- Credit the creator if a hack comes from a specific video.
