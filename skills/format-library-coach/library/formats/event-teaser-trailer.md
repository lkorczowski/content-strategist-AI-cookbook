# Event teaser trailer

## Shape

A trailer cut like a movie preview, with epic music and fast shots from a past edition of the event. The energy builds with each cut: crowd, speakers, details, laughter. It ends on the date, the place and the call to book.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: a strong image from the last edition and the first beat of the music.
2. 2 to 20 s: fast cuts of the best moments, faces and details, rhythm on the music.
3. 20 to 45 s: the peak. The biggest moment of the past edition, slowed down or held.
4. Last 5 to 10 s: black screen, date, place, "book your seat" and the link.

## Make it yours

Founders run events too: a launch event, a customer meetup, a user conference, a demo day. You film your first edition with this trailer in mind, then you cut it to sell the next one. Examples: a developer platform announcing its second user conference with clips of the first, an AI community startup teasing its monthly founder dinner, a B2B SaaS cutting a trailer for its customer summit from last year's keynote and hallway shots.

## Variations

- First edition: no past footage yet, so you cut the trailer from the venue, the speakers and the preparation.
- Countdown series: one short trailer per week before the event, each revealing one speaker.
- Aftermovie: the same cut published after the event, to sell the next one.

## Why it works

The music and the crowd shots let viewers feel the event before they attend, and people who were there share it to show they belonged (Berger 2013, social currency). The date at the end turns that feeling into a clear next step.

## Watch out

- Get consent from attendees who appear on screen, ideally at registration.
- License the music for commercial use on each platform.
- Plan the shots during the event. A trailer needs wide crowd shots, close details and reactions.
