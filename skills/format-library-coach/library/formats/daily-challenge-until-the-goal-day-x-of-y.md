# Daily challenge until the goal (day X of Y)

## Shape

The creator repeats the same act every day and posts it with a visible counter ("Day 214 of 365"). Each video is short and looks almost the same, with one small change: a little progress, a new obstacle, a different place. The counter and the stubbornness are the story.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the counter on screen and the rule said in one line ("Day 21 of doing this every day").
2. 2 to 15 s: the act of the day, done in full.
3. 15 to 45 s: what is different today: the progress, a setback, a comment from a viewer.
4. Last seconds: the result of the day and the counter for tomorrow.

## Make it yours

The goal is a business goal you can show progress on every day. "Day 12 of getting our first 100 customers" works for any early-stage founder: show the count, the action of the day and what you learned. A founder of an AI cold-email tool sends one personal message a day to a dream customer and shows the reply rate. A founder of a no-code app builder ships one tiny app a day. A founder of a language learning app speaks one minute a day in a new language. The counter goes on screen every time, in the same place.

## Variations

- Team series: an intern or a new hire builds something from zero, one episode per day.
- Shorter run: a 30-day sprint with one clear finish line.
- Viewer rule: viewers add a new rule every week.

## Why it works

An unfinished count stays in the viewer's mind, and each day pulls them back to see the next number (Zeigarnik 1927). Repetition also makes the format easy to recognize in the feed after a few days.

## Watch out

- Keep the promise: missing days breaks the series. Pick a goal you can keep up.
- Share real numbers only. Round nothing up.
- Avoid anything dangerous or illegal for the sake of the streak, and respect place rules where you film.
