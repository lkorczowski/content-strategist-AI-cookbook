# Movie scene remake with your product

## Shape

The team reenacts a famous film scene line for line, in their own workplace. The product takes the place of the iconic object at the center of the scene. Viewers recognize the scene in seconds and wait for the product's moment.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the opening line of the scene, delivered exactly, so viewers recognize it.
2. 2 to 25 s: the scene plays with the same rhythm and framing as the original.
3. 25 to 50 s: the iconic object moment, replaced by your product.
4. Last seconds: the scene's famous reaction, and a caption naming the product.

## Make it yours

Find a famous scene built around an object, a reveal or a comparison, and swap in your product. A dashboard tool remakes a famous comparison scene where characters compare their reports instead of the original object. An AI voice startup remakes a famous phone scene where the voice on the line is the product. A security key maker remakes a famous heist scene where the key opens everything. The closer you follow the original rhythm, the stronger the recognition.

## Variations

- One-person remake: the founder plays every role.
- Customer version: the scene plays out with the customer's problem as the plot.
- Trailer style: a fake movie trailer starring the product.

## Why it works

Recognition is instant, and the swap of the iconic object is a surprise viewers enjoy spotting. Film fans share remakes of scenes they love because the reference makes them part of the joke (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- Reenact the scene with your own footage. Using the original clip raises rights problems on brand accounts.
- Avoid copying logos, posters or music from the film without a license.
- Pick scenes whose tone fits your brand; violent or explicit scenes backfire.

2. For the best one, write the remake script: lines adapted to our product, roles, location in our workplace, framing notes that echo the original, and the closing caption.
Rules: reenact with our own footage, no original clips, logos or music without a license, scenes that fit a brand tone.
