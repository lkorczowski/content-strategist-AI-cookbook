# Industry insider alert: what nobody tells you

## Shape

An insider reveals a practice of their own industry that works against customers. They explain how it works with one concrete example. The video ends with what viewers can do to protect themselves.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the warning, as an insider ("Most people in my industry won't tell you this").
2. 2 to 30 s: the practice, named and explained in plain words.
3. 30 to 90 s: one example that shows it happening, step by step.
4. Last 15 s: how to protect yourself, two or three actions, then a short close.

## Make it yours

Your product probably exists because of a practice you saw from inside. Say it out loud, and give the protection steps for free. A founder of a transparent pricing tool for SaaS buyers explains how vendors raise prices at renewal. A founder of an AI contract reviewer explains the auto-renewal clauses hidden in software agreements. A founder of a hiring platform explains how some recruiters filter résumés before a human sees them. The protection steps work without your product, and the product appears as one option.

## Variations

- Yapping version: told raw in the car, as if warning a friend.
- Greenscreen version: the contract clause, pricing page or email behind you.
- Series: one practice per episode, same opening warning.

## Why it works

A secret from inside a field opens a strong information gap (Loewenstein 1994), and the viewer feels protected by the end. Sharing the warning makes the sharer look informed and helpful, the social currency and practical value drivers of Berger 2013.

## Watch out

- Name practices and keep people and companies out of it. Defamation risk is real.
- Every fact must be sourced or come from your direct experience.
- Fear without a protection step reads as clickbait.
