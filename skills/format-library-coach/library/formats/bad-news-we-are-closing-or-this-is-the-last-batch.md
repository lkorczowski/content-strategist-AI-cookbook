# Bad news: we are closing (or this is the last batch)

## Shape

The founder opens with "we have bad news" and announces an end: the company closes, a product is discontinued, or the last stock is going. They say what they tried before deciding. The video ends with what happens now for customers and followers.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: "We have bad news" on screen and in the voice.
2. 2 to 10 s: the end, said plainly (closing, product discontinued, last batch).
3. 10 to 30 s: what was tried, and why it did not work, in two or three sentences.
4. Last 10 s: what happens now: last day to buy, how to export data, refunds, what comes next.

## Make it yours

Keep this format for a real end, and use it to treat customers well on the way out. A SaaS sunsetting a legacy plan explains the date, the reason and how to migrate. A small AI startup shutting down a side product tells users how to export their data. A hardware company ending a product line announces the last batch and the support period. A clear, honest announcement protects your reputation for the next thing you build.

## Variations

- Last batch: the product continues, this version or run ends.
- Pivot: the company closes one business to focus on another, said in the same structure.
- Thank-you version: the end announced with a montage of customers and moments.

## Why it works

"We have bad news" is a line people stop for, because it promises a change they want to understand. A real ending triggers emotion and urgency, and customers share it to tell others before the date (Berger 2013, emotion).

## Watch out

- Only true news. A fake closing is deceptive marketing under consumer law and platform rules.
- Give customers the practical details: dates, refunds, data export.
- Check with your lawyer and your team before announcing a closure publicly.
