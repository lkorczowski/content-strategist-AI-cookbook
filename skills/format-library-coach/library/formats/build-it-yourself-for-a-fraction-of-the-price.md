# Build it yourself (for a fraction of the price)

## Shape

The maker shows an expensive product and its price, then says they built their own for much less. The video follows the build in fast steps. It ends with the home-made version working and one reason it suits them better.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the expensive thing and the claim in one line ("This cost a lot, so I built my own").
2. 2 to 15 s: why the expensive option did not fit: price, missing feature, lock-in.
3. 15 to 90 s: the build in quick steps, with the hard part kept in.
4. Last 10 to 20 s: the result working, the real cost, and what you would change next time.

## Make it yours

For AI founders, the angle is "I built it in a weekend with AI": you replace an expensive tool or service with something you built with AI, and show how. A founder of an AI coding tool rebuilds a paid internal dashboard in one afternoon and shows the prompts. A founder of a hardware startup shows the prototype they built on a budget before raising money. A founder of an automation startup replaces a costly reporting service with a small workflow. The story is the founder's own: why you built it, what broke, what you learned.

## Variations

- Assistant build: you build with an AI assistant guiding each step, like the guitar pedal example.
- Repost credit: share an open hardware maker's build with full credit and your comment.
- Customer build: a customer shows what they built with your product.

## Why it works

The price gap creates a surprise the viewer wants explained, and the build answers "how?" step by step (Loewenstein 1994). Saving money is practical value people pass on to friends with the same need (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- Real prices only, with the date. State what the home-made version lacks.
- Safety first for hardware and electrical builds: add warnings and skip risky shortcuts.
- Disclose paid partnerships, as in the second example, and credit the original maker of any build you repost.
