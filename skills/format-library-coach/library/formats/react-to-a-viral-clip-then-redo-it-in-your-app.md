# React to a viral clip, then redo it in your app

## Shape

A viral clip from someone else plays with a short, funny reaction: a trip, a workout, a recipe, a room makeover. Then the screen switches to the app, where the same thing is rebuilt: the route traced, the plan generated, the budget computed. The video ends on the result in the app, next to a frame of the original clip.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the viral clip with a textbox that reacts to it ("The ultimate weekend plan, apparently").
2. 2 to 12 s: a few seconds of the clip and one reaction line.
3. 12 to 35 s: the screen recording: the same thing rebuilt in the app, step by step.
4. Last seconds: the result, and a line inviting viewers to send the next clip.

## Make it yours

Pick clips your buyers already watch, then show what your app does with that exact content. A cycling maps app rebuilds the ride from a viral cycling reel and shows the climb. An AI meal planner turns a viral recipe into a shopping list for four. An interior design app rebuilds a viral room in 3D and lists the furniture. A budgeting app prices the "day in my life" of a creator. The reaction brings the reach of the original clip, and the screen recording is the demo. This shape was shot in a real program, as a batch of about ten videos.

## Variations

- Comment request: viewers send the clip, and each reply video rebuilds one.
- Correction: the app shows what the clip got wrong (the real distance, the real cost).
- Series: one creator or one place per week, same textbox pattern.

## Why it works

The original clip already proved it can hold attention, so the opening borrows that pull. Rebuilding it gives the viewer a practical takeaway they can use or send to a friend, which is practical value (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- Credit and tag the original creator, and keep the extract short. Use the platform's duet or stitch when you can.
- Only rebuild what the app really does on that content; no staged screens.
- Ask before using a clip that shows private people.
