# The new hire learns one task (numbered episodes)

## Shape

A new team member tries one task of the job and gets it wrong in a funny way. The boss or a senior colleague corrects them and shows the right way. Each episode has a number and the same textbox pattern ("How to do X: the new hire, ep 3").

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the textbox with the task and the episode number, the new hire already struggling.
2. 2 to 15 s: the attempt and the mistake.
3. 15 to 35 s: the correction: the senior shows the right way, with one useful detail.
4. Last seconds: the new hire tries again, or a hint of the next episode's task.

## Make it yours

You list the tasks a new person learns in their first weeks, one per episode. A design tool startup films the new hire exporting a file in the wrong format, then the designer shows the right setting. An AI support platform films the new hire answering a ticket by hand while the agent already solved it. A logistics software company films the new hire planning a route on paper. The task can be your own product, or a moment of company life that buyers recognize. This shape was shot in a real program.

## Variations

- Reverse: the new hire teaches the boss a tool the boss never used.
- Exam episode: the last episode tests everything the new hire learned.
- Customer view: the new hire is a new customer learning the product.

## Why it works

The numbered series makes viewers look for the next episode and the earlier ones, which keeps an open loop across videos (Zeigarnik 1927). Everyone remembers their first weeks in a job, so the mistakes feel familiar and the fix gives practical value.

## Watch out

- The new hire must be in on the joke and agree to every episode. Humiliation kills the format and the team mood.
- Drop an episode when the task gives no clear mistake to show.
- Keep the correction accurate: viewers will copy it.
