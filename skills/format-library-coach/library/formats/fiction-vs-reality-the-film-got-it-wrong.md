# Fiction vs reality (the film got it wrong)

## Shape

The speaker shows how a game, a film or a cliché depicts something. Then they show what it really looks like, or how it really happened, side by side or one after the other. Each pair repeats the same rhythm: the fiction, then the reality, then one line on the gap.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the fiction on screen and the question ("what does this look like in real life?").
2. 2 to 20 s: the first reality reveal, the most surprising one.
3. 20 s to the last 15 s: more pairs, or the full real story behind one scene, with documents or photos.
4. Last seconds: the biggest gap, or why the fiction chose to show it that way.

## Make it yours

Films and series have shaped what people imagine about your field, and you know the reality. A founder of an AI security startup compares hacker scenes from films with what an attack really looks like. A fundraising tool compares the startup pitch in a famous series with a real pitch meeting. A robotics software founder compares film robots with what robots in warehouses actually do. Each pair shows your expertise without a pitch.

## Variations

- Game version: objects or places from a video game shown in real life (see the first example).
- Real story behind the scene: one film scene, then the history it was based on (see the second example).
- Cliché version: a common image of your job (stock photos, TV tropes) against a real day.

## Why it works

The fiction sets an expectation, and the reality breaks it, which resets attention at every pair. Knowing what is real behind a famous scene gives viewers a fact to retell (Berger 2013, social currency).

## Watch out

- Use short film or game clips within fair use and platform rules, and add your commentary over them.
- Check the real story against solid sources, and say when it is disputed.
- Keep the tone curious about the film. Viewers who love it will defend it in the comments.

## For your AI

```
Script a "fiction vs reality" video for me, founder of [company] in [field], for [audience].
1. List 5 films, series, games or clichés that show [field] in a way my audience knows.
2. For the best one, write 3 pairs: what the fiction shows, what reality looks like, one line on the gap. Give a source for each reality claim; mark [check] where unsure.
3. Write the opening question (under 12 words) and the closing line.
4. Add shot notes: which clip or still for the fiction, which photo, document or footage for the reality.
Rules: short clips within fair use, sourced facts, curious tone, no product pitch.
```
