# Roleplay: wrong answer vs right answer

## Shape

A real-life scene (a job interview, a sales call, a negotiation) is played twice. First comes the answer most people give and its weak result. Then the same scene replays with the answer that works and its better result.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the situation on screen ("salary negotiation, round one") and the first question asked.
2. 2 to 30 s: the common answer, and the other side's reaction that shows it failed.
3. 30 to 80 s: rewind, same question, the strong answer, and the better outcome.
4. Last seconds: the principle behind the right answer, in one sentence.

## Make it yours

Play the conversation your buyers struggle with. A sales coaching AI plays a discovery call twice: the rep who pitches too early, then the rep who asks one more question. A customer support platform plays an angry-customer call twice. A hiring tool plays a candidate answering "tell me about yourself" twice. You can play both roles with a costume change, or film with a colleague.

## Variations

- Viewer vote: show both answers and ask which one works before the reveal.
- Three versions: bad, average, great.
- Demo call: the right answer uses your product live in the conversation.

## Why it works

The contrast makes the lesson obvious without a lecture, and viewers save the right answer to use later, which is practical value (Berger 2013). Many recognize their own wrong answer, which keeps them watching for the fix.

## Watch out

- Keep the outcomes realistic. An exaggerated win makes the advice feel fake.
- Never promise a specific result (a salary, a closed deal) from one line.
- If you show a real call, get consent from everyone on it.
