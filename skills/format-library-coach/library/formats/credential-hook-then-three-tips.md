# Credential hook, then three tips

## Shape

The first sentence promises how to get a result. The second gives the one credential that lets the speaker say it. Then come three numbered tips, one sentence each, delivered fast to camera.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the promise ("Here's how to get your first ten paying customers").
2. 2 to 6 s: one credential, one line, true and checkable.
3. 6 to 35 s: tip one, tip two, tip three. A number on screen for each, one sentence of explanation.
4. Last seconds: a short close, either the strongest tip repeated or a question about which tip the viewer will try.

## Make it yours

Choose a result your buyers want and that your experience proves. Your credential is the line that makes them listen. A founder of an AI sales assistant gives three tips to book demos, after running outbound at a fast-growing startup. A founder of a hiring platform for engineers gives three interview tips, after reviewing thousands of candidates. A founder of a pricing tool for SaaS gives three pricing page fixes, after testing their own pricing in public. The tips stand on their own; the product can appear in a caption.

## Variations

- Learned list: drop the promise, open on "Things I've learned about X" and deliver the list fast (see the second example).
- Five tips: same shape, faster delivery, for a topic with more steps.
- Hook stacking: stack two hooks in the opening (the result and the credential in one breath) to tighten the first seconds.

## Why it works

The credential answers the viewer's first question (why listen to you?) before they can scroll. Useful tips that make the sharer look informed match the practical value and social currency drivers in Berger 2013.

## Watch out

- The credential must be one line and true. A vague or inflated claim kills trust.
- Three weak tips lose the viewer by tip two. Lead with your strongest.
- Low views on a good shape happen: judge the shape by many posts.
