# Open product decision: option A or option B

## Shape

The founder explains a real product or launch decision as a choice between two options. They lay out the trade-off of each, in plain words. The video ends with the option the team picked, or with the decision still open and a question to viewers.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the decision as a hook ("Why we are waiting to launch").
2. 2 to 20 s: the context. What happened in the market that forced a choice.
3. 20 to 55 s: option A and its trade-off, then option B and its trade-off, each with a label on screen.
4. Last 10 s: what we picked and why, or "we haven't decided, which would you choose?"

## Make it yours

Film the decision while it is still open, and your viewers become part of it. An AI transcription tool explains whether to launch a cheap plan now or wait for the accurate model. A design collaboration startup explains whether to build a plugin for a big platform or keep its own editor. A security product for startups explains whether to go open source or stay closed. Buyers see how you think about quality and trade-offs, which is the real sales argument behind your product.

## Variations

- Still open: the video ends on the question, and a follow-up announces the choice and credits the comments.
- Looking back: a past decision explained with what happened after.
- Three options: option C added when it is real, with the same structure.

## Why it works

A choice between two options invites the viewer to pick before the founder does, which keeps them watching for the answer. An open decision leaves a loop the audience wants closed (Zeigarnik 1927), so they return for the follow-up.

## Watch out

- Keep it a real decision. A fake dilemma with an obvious answer reads as an ad.
- Leave out confidential details on suppliers, partners or contracts.
- If you promise to follow viewer votes, honor the result or explain why you did otherwise.
