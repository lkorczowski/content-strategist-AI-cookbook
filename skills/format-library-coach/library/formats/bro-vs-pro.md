# Bro vs pro

## Shape

The same person plays two characters side by side: the amateur ("bro") and the expert ("pro"). The bro says one careless line, the pro answers with what they do instead and the reason behind it. Five or six exchanges follow, and the last one lands the biggest gap.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: split screen, labels "bro" and "pro" on top, and the bro's first line already playing.
2. 2 to 25 s: three or four exchanges. Bro line, pro answer with a reason ("because research shows...").
3. 25 to 40 s: the exchange where the pro's habit sounds extreme or surprising.
4. Last seconds: a final bro line that is funny, and the pro's shortest answer.

## Make it yours

Pick a task your buyers do every week and contrast the careless way with the expert way. A sales engagement tool runs "bro vs pro cold email". A data platform runs "bro vs pro dashboards". An AI meeting assistant runs "bro vs pro meeting notes". You play both roles with one costume change (a cap, glasses). Your product can appear in the pro's hands for one exchange, as a habit among others.

## Variations

- Two people: a teammate plays the bro, you play the pro.
- Customer edition: before using your category (bro) and after (pro).
- Pro vs legend: a third column for the top expert, for a longer version.

## Why it works

The contrast teaches one habit per line, and the bro makes viewers laugh at a version of themselves they recognize. Sharing it to a friend ("this is you") is easy and harmless.

## Watch out

- The pro's reasons must be correct. A wrong "research shows" gets called out fast; keep a source for each claim.
- Make the bro likable. A bro written as stupid turns the humour into contempt.
- Keep the split screen readable on a phone: large labels, one line of text at a time.
