# Verdict-first monologue

## Shape

A talking-head script that gives the verdict in the very first line. Next, the speaker says out loud the sensible first reading a viewer would have, then tops it with one plain fact about a third of the way in. From there the script escalates, pushing the key word of the hook to its most extreme meaning by the end.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the verdict, one sentence, built around one strong hook word.
2. 2 to 35 s: the viewer's sensible first reading, said out loud and taken seriously.
3. 35 to 60 s: one plain fact that changes the picture. No chart, no jargon, a fact anyone can check.
4. 60 s to the end: the escalation. Each step pushes the hook word further, until it means more than the viewer thought at the start.

## Make it yours

This is a script structure for founders who talk to camera. You start from a verdict about your market, then build the escalation around one word. An AI hiring platform founder: "Most job descriptions are fiction." A cybersecurity startup founder: "Your password policy is theatre." A developer tools founder: "Your staging environment is lying to you." Write the sensible reply a smart viewer would give, then the fact that tops it. To study the structure behind this format, use [short-story-replication](../../../short-story-replication/) and [short-video-author-analysis](../../../short-video-author-analysis/).

## Variations

- Shorter cut: verdict, first reading, fact, one escalation step, under 60 seconds.
- Walk and talk: the same script delivered while walking, for more energy.
- Comment-led: the sensible first reading is an actual comment from your previous video.

## Why it works

The verdict upfront gives the viewer a reason to stay: they want to see whether it holds. Voicing their own objection keeps them nodding, and the escalation keeps raising the stakes so the question stays open until the end (Zeigarnik 1927).

## Watch out

- The plain fact must be true and checkable. One wrong fact breaks the whole escalation.
- The hook word must carry the ending. If the last line drops it, the structure falls flat.
- Past 90 seconds, every sentence must earn its place. Cut anything that repeats.
