# Expert reacts to a fail

## Shape

A fail clip from your field plays on screen. The expert pauses it at the key moment, points at what went wrong, and says what a pro would do instead. The clip resumes so the viewer sees the consequence.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the fail clip starts and the expert's first line lands on the frame that matters ("look at where he's placing the bar").
2. 2 to 30 s: freeze frame, circle or arrow on the mistake, one plain explanation of why it fails.
3. 30 to 70 s: the fix. Show the right move, a quick sketch or your own short demo.
4. Last seconds: the clip plays to the end, then one line the viewer can reuse next time.

## Make it yours

Your field has its own fails: broken workflows, bad setups, risky shortcuts. Pick a public clip of that mistake and give your expert verdict. A security tool for small teams reacts to a video of someone sharing a password on a shared screen. An AI bookkeeping tool for freelancers reacts to a messy receipt shoebox. A no-code automation platform reacts to an operator copying data between ten tabs. You can stay off camera and let your voice and annotations carry it, or appear in the corner.

## Variations

- Near miss: react to a clip where disaster was avoided by luck, and explain what normally happens ("Normally this is the crash").
- Series: one fail per day from the same field, same opening line.
- Self-fail: react to your own early mistakes, which removes any rights question.

## Why it works

The fail gives an instant emotional hook, and the pause opens a gap: the viewer wants to know what the expert sees that they missed (Loewenstein 1994). The fix gives practical value people save and send to the friend who makes the same mistake (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- Use the platform's stitch or duet tools so the original creator is credited, and avoid mocking private people.
- Explain the mistake with respect. Contempt turns an expert into a bully in the comments.
- Never give advice that is unsafe without stating the limits (health, driving, money).
