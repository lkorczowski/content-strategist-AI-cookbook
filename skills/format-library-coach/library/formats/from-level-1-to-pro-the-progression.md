# From level 1 to pro: the progression

## Shape

The video walks through the levels of a practice in order, from the very first day to the top. Each level gets a name and a short picture of what that person looks like: their gear, their habits, their mistakes. Viewers place themselves on the ladder as it climbs.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: level 1 on screen and its most recognizable detail ("Level 1: you track everything in a notes app").
2. 2 to 60 s: the middle levels, one every 10 to 15 seconds, each with one visual detail and one habit, shown in b-roll or acted to camera.
3. 60 to 120 s: the top levels, where the habits change in a surprising way (often simpler, sometimes absurd).
4. Last seconds: the final level, then a question: "Which level are you?"

## Make it yours

Map the levels of a user of your category, from the crude solution to the expert one. A project management SaaS goes from sticky notes to spreadsheet to a tool to a fully automated workflow. An AI coding assistant goes from copying answers from a forum to writing scripts to building agents. A customer support platform goes from a shared inbox to macros to AI triage. Your product sits at one level; you can show it there without selling it, and the top level can make fun of over-engineering.

## Variations

- Gear only: show what each level owns, with b-roll of the setup.
- Mistakes only: the typical mistake at each level.
- Reverse ladder: start from the pro and go down to the beginner.

## Why it works

Viewers recognize their current level and the level of people they know, which makes the video easy to tag and share. The climb also leaves the top level unknown until the end, an open question that holds attention (Loewenstein 1994).

## Watch out

- Mock the habits, never the people at the low levels: they are your future users.
- Keep every level concrete. A level described in vague words breaks the recognition.
- Using another creator's level names or footage needs credit and rights.

```
Write a "From level 1 to pro: the progression" video for [company], which sells [product] to [audience].
1. Define 6 to 8 levels of someone doing [task or practice], from first day to top expert. For each: a name, one visual detail (tool, gear, screen), one habit, one typical mistake.
2. Place [product] at the right level without a sales pitch.
3. Write the opening line (level 1 and its most recognizable detail), the script per level, and shot notes (facecam or b-roll per level).
4. End with a question that invites viewers to comment their level.
Rules: make fun of habits, never of people; every level must be concrete and true to life; 60 to 150 seconds.
```
