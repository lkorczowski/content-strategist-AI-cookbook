# Everyday analogy for a hard concept

## Shape

The speaker starts with an everyday situation everyone has lived, such as a burger whose price jumps between the menu and the bill. They walk through it in a few seconds, then map it step by step onto a hard concept from their field. The video ends when the viewer can explain the concept with the everyday words.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the everyday situation as a question ("Ever eaten a burger in the US?").
2. 2 to 15 s: the situation in two or three concrete steps, with the surprise in it.
3. 15 to 45 s: the switch to the hard concept, each part of the analogy matched to one part of the concept.
4. Last seconds: the one-line takeaway, in the everyday words.

## Make it yours

Your expertise is full of concepts your buyers half understand. You pick one, and you find the everyday situation that has the same shape. A cap table tool explains dilution with a restaurant bill. An AI infrastructure startup explains latency with a queue at a coffee shop. A cybersecurity founder explains phishing with a fake delivery notice at the door. The analogy is yours, which makes the format hard to copy: test it on someone outside your field before you film.

## Variations

- Props version: use the real object (the receipt, the coffee cup) on camera.
- Series: one concept per episode, always the same everyday world (the kitchen, the supermarket).
- Reverse: start with the jargon, look confused, then switch to the analogy.

## Why it works

The everyday situation is instantly familiar, so the viewer follows without effort, and the surprise inside it keeps them watching. A clear analogy is easy to repeat, which makes it practical to share with a colleague (Berger 2013, practical value).

## Watch out

- Check where the analogy breaks, and say it in one sentence if it matters.
- Keep one analogy per video. Mixing two confuses more than it explains.
- On financial or legal concepts, stay general and avoid personal advice.

```
Help me write an "everyday analogy" video for [company], which sells [product] to [audience].
The hard concept: [concept].
1. Propose 5 everyday situations with the same structure as the concept, each with a built-in surprise.
2. For the best one, map each part of the situation to one part of the concept in a 2-column list.
3. Write a 30 to 60 second script: the opening question, the situation in 2 or 3 steps, the switch, the takeaway in everyday words.
4. Say where the analogy breaks, in one sentence.
5. Add shot notes: facecam, props, text on screen.
Rules: one analogy only, no invented numbers about my product, no personal financial or legal advice.
```
