# I let my Gen Z intern edit this video

## Shape

An ordinary company video, re-edited in a deliberately chaotic style: sudden zooms, meme sounds, captions with slang, random effects. A textbox at the start says the intern was given the edit. The contrast between a serious brand and the wild edit carries the joke.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the textbox ("I let our Gen Z intern edit this video") over a calm first shot.
2. 2 to 15 s: the first chaotic edits: zoom on a face, a meme sound, a slang caption.
3. 15 to 30 s: the escalation: effects pile up on the product shots.
4. Last seconds: a final absurd edit, or the boss staring at the screen.

## Make it yours

You start from footage that is almost boring: a product demo, an office tour, a founder update. A payroll software company lets the intern edit its compliance update. An AI analytics startup lets the intern add memes to its dashboard demo. A B2B security vendor lets the intern cut its trust page video. The product stays visible the whole time, so the joke still shows what you sell. This shape was shot in a real program, as an edit of existing rushes.

## Variations

- Real intern: the actual youngest person on the team does the edit and appears at the end.
- Before and after: the boring original first, then the intern version.
- Boss reaction: the founder watches the edit on a laptop, filmed in a corner.

## Why it works

Brands that laugh at themselves surprise viewers who expect polish. The generation gap is a situation many workplaces know, so people tag colleagues, which spreads the video.

## Watch out

- Use sounds from the platform library; meme sounds are often unlicensed for brand accounts.
- Keep claims about the product accurate even inside the jokes.
- If a real intern is named or shown, get their agreement.
