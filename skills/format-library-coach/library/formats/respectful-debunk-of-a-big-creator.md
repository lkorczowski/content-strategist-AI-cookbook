# Respectful debunk of a big creator

## Shape

A short excerpt of a bigger creator or coach making a claim plays first. The expert then checks it point by point, with a source, a chart or a demo on screen for each point. The tone stays calm and the video ends on what is actually true.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the claim, in the original creator's words, with on-screen text "Let's check this".
2. 2 to 60 s: point one and its evidence (study, document, screen recording), then point two.
3. 60 to 150 s: the strongest correction, the one that changes what the viewer should do.
4. Last seconds: the accurate version in one sentence, and where to check it yourself.

## Make it yours

Every market has loud claims that mislead your buyers. Pick one that is popular and wrong, and answer it with your expertise. An AI hiring tool checks a viral claim that "recruiters read every CV in six seconds" by showing how screening really works. A payroll platform for small businesses checks a coach's tax shortcut against the official rules. A data security startup checks a creator's "this setting protects you" tip with a live test. Your product can appear as the tool you used to check, or stay out entirely.

## Variations

- Partial agreement: say what the creator got right before the correction, which keeps fans listening.
- Live test: replace documents with a real experiment on screen.
- Ask for an answer: invite the creator to respond in a duet.

## Why it works

Seeing a known voice challenged creates tension, and the point-by-point check opens a gap the viewer wants to close (Loewenstein 1994). Sharing a correction makes the sharer look informed, which is social currency (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- Attack the claim and leave the person out of it. No insults, no guessing at motives.
- Every correction needs a source you can show. One weak point sinks the whole video.
- Use stitch or duet, or a short excerpt for commentary, and credit the original creator.
