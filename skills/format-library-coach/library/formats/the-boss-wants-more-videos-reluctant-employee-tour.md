# The boss wants more videos (reluctant employee tour)

## Shape

An employee films the workplace with deadpan reluctance and explains they are only doing it because "the boss wants more videos". They walk through the place and list the products in the flattest voice possible. The anti-ad tone is the ad.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the employee to camera, unexcited, with the reason ("the boss wants us to do more videos").
2. 2 to 25 s: the tour starts, products named flatly, one per shot.
3. 25 to 50 s: one tiny moment where real pride slips through, quickly hidden.
4. Last seconds: a flat sign-off ("so, that's it, come visit") and the camera stops.

## Make it yours

The format comes from shops, and it adapts to software teams that have something to show. A support engineer at a helpdesk startup tours the product features with zero enthusiasm because "marketing asked". An intern at an AI analytics company lists the dashboards in a monotone. A developer at an API platform reads the changelog aloud, eyes half closed. The founder stays off camera: the joke works because the boss is offscreen.

## Variations

- Weekly episode: the same employee, a new "assignment" from the boss each week.
- Boss cameo: the boss walks in at the end and the employee instantly fakes enthusiasm.
- Two employees: one overexcited, one deadpan.

## Why it works

The reluctant tone breaks the polished ad pattern viewers scroll past, so it stands out. Everyone has been asked to do a task they did not choose, and they recognize themselves in the employee.

## Watch out

- The employee must be a willing performer. A truly unhappy employee on camera hurts your employer brand.
- Keep the products accurate even when the tone is flat.
- On a business account, use sounds cleared for commercial use.
