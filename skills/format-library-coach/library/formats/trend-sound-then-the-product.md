# Trend sound, then the product

## Shape

The first seconds ride a trending sound or a short sketch everyone recognizes. Then the video switches to the product or the workplace: how it is made, packed or used. The trend buys the attention, the product keeps it.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the trend moment, reproduced faithfully so viewers recognize it.
2. 2 to 5 s: the hard cut or the transition into your setting.
3. 5 to 25 s: the product in action, a satisfying process or a quick demo.
4. Last seconds: the result or the finished product, held on screen.

## Make it yours

Open on the trend, then show your product's most satisfying moment. A hardware startup opens on a trending sound, then shows a device assembled in one smooth take. An AI image tool opens on a famous sketch, then shows a photo transformed in seconds. A screen recording tool opens on a trending meme, then shows a messy video edited into a clean demo. Keep the product part short and visual so the transition feels earned.

## Variations

- Sketch intro: the opening reuses a well-known short sketch line (see the second example).
- Team version: the team performs the trend, then goes back to work.
- Loop: the last frame matches the first, so the video replays.

## Why it works

A familiar sound or sketch signals entertainment, so viewers give it a second look. The switch to a real product moment is a small surprise, and a satisfying process keeps people watching to the end.

## Watch out

- Check the sound license: many trending sounds are blocked for business accounts. Use the commercial library.
- Trends expire fast; check the sound is still active before shooting.
- The trend and the product need a link, even a loose one, or viewers feel tricked.
