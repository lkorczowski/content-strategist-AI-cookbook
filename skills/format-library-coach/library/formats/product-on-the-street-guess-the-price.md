# Product on the street: guess the price

## Shape

The interviewer hands the product, or shows it on a phone, to someone on the street and asks how much it costs. The person guesses, and the real answer reveals a business model they did not expect. Their next question becomes the objection the video answers.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the product in the stranger's hand and the question "How much does this cost?".
2. 2 to 12 s: the guess, then the real answer, and the stranger's reaction.
3. 12 to 35 s: the stranger's doubt ("Is it bad quality?", "What's the catch?") and a short, plain answer that explains how the model works.
4. Last 5 s: the stranger's final verdict, then the product name on screen.

## Make it yours

This shape suits any product whose price or model surprises people. You show the product to a stranger and let their guess set up the reveal. An AI note-taker for doctors with a free tier asks people what they think a medical scribe costs per year. A usage-based design tool asks freelancers to guess the monthly bill for one logo. A tool that pays users for their data asks how much a stranger would pay for it. The stranger's objection is the most useful part: write down the questions people ask and answer them in the next videos.

## Variations

- Two-shot at a stand: film at your booth at an event, with two people guessing together.
- Guess the time: ask how long a task takes by hand, then show the product doing it.
- Guess the maker: ask who made the product, and reveal a small team or a founder.

## Why it works

The guess creates a gap between expectation and reality, and the viewer makes their own guess before the reveal (Loewenstein 1994). A surprising price or model is also easy to retell, which gives people a reason to share (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- The price or model must be true on the day you post. Update or archive the video when it changes.
- Get consent and a release from every person shown.
- Disclose that the account belongs to the company.
