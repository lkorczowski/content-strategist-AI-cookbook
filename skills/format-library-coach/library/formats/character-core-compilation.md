# Character core compilation

## Shape

A fast montage of short moments that show one person's personality: they get annoyed, laugh, help someone, look exhausted, celebrate. The clips come from behind-the-scenes footage, cut to music in the style of celebrity fan edits. A textbox names the person as a "core" ("POV: [name] core").

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the most recognizable moment of the person, with the "core" textbox.
2. 2 to 12 s: three or four short clips of one trait (the jokes, the anger, the kindness).
3. 12 to 25 s: the contrast: an opposite trait, then a tender or funny moment.
4. Last seconds: a final close-up that sums up the character, ending on the beat.

## Make it yours

The format needs a person viewers already know a little, and a folder of outtakes. A founder who appears in every video gets "CEO core": the late-night commits, the coffee, the demo that crashes. A support lead becomes "support hero core": calm on the phone, chaos off camera. A hardware cofounder becomes "lab core": safety goggles, soldering, a small victory dance. It costs no shoot: you edit footage you already have. This shape was shot in a real program, built from backstage rushes.

## Variations

- Team core: one trait per person, a few seconds each.
- Customer core: moments from a community event, with consent.
- Then and now: early clips of the founder, then recent ones.

## Why it works

Short moments of real personality build a bond faster than a scripted video, and the "core" label borrows the language of fan culture. Followers who know the person share it with friends who know them too, which is social currency for the group (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- Use only moments the person agreed to publish, especially the tired or angry ones.
- Use music from the platform library or with the rights; fan edits often use tracks that brands cannot use.
- Keep it short: the format works when each clip lasts a second or two.
