# How I make money, explained at my stand

## Shape

The founder opens with their age and "here's how I make money", then explains the business model in plain words. They stand where the business happens (a stall, a workshop, a desk) with the product in hand. The viewer leaves understanding exactly who pays and for what.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: "I'm [age] and here's how I make money", with the place visible.
2. 2 to 15 s: what the product is, shown in hand or on screen, with a question if it is unusual ("You don't know what that is?").
3. 15 to 45 s: the model. Who pays, how much effort each sale takes, where the margin comes from.
4. Last seconds: one surprising detail or the next step for the business.

## Make it yours

Say your model as simply as you would to a curious neighbor. A founder of a compliance SaaS for dentists explains that clinics pay a monthly fee because inspections changed. A founder of an AI data labeling service explains they sell to research teams who need clean data fast. A founder of a Chrome extension for recruiters explains the free version and the paid team plan, at their desk with the extension open. B2B models work in this format as long as the explanation stays plain.

## Variations

- One decision: the owner explains a single supplier or pricing decision and its effect on margin (see the second example).
- Laptop stand: for software, the "stand" is the desk and the screen shows the product.
- Series: one part of the model per episode (acquisition, pricing, costs).

## Why it works

People are curious about how others earn a living, and the opening line promises a direct answer. An unusual product raises a quick question (what is that?), and the plain explanation rewards it (Loewenstein 1994).

## Watch out

- Say only the numbers you want public.
- Keep the model simple. Jargon loses the viewer in the first seconds.
- Filming in a market or shared space may need permission from the organizer.
