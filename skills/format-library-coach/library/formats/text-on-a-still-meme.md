# Text on a still (meme)

## Shape

One image or a very short clip fills the screen, with one line of text on top. The text says something your audience recognizes from their own life. There is no voice: the image, the line and often a trending sound do the work.

## Beats

1. 0 to 1 s: the image and the line appear together. Viewers must read it in one glance.
2. 1 to 5 s: the image holds, or a tiny movement happens, while the joke lands.
3. 5 to 10 s: optional second line, the twist that makes it funnier.
4. End: the video loops, so a viewer watches it twice.

## Make it yours

You write the thoughts your buyers have every day and never say out loud, and you pair each one with an image that fits. Your product is rarely in the frame: the meme shows that you know their daily life. Examples: an AI meeting notes tool posting about the call that could have been an email, a payroll SaaS posting about the last day of the month, a developer tool posting about the bug that only appears during a demo.

## Variations

- Clip version: a two second clip instead of a still.
- Point of view: the line starts with "when you..." over a reaction image.
- Series: the same image template with a new line each week.

## Why it works

Recognizing yourself in a line takes one second, and people tag the friend or colleague who lives it too. The short length and the loop invite a second watch.

## Watch out

- Use images and sounds you have the right to use: your own photos, licensed stock, or the platform's library for sounds.
- Keep the line short enough to read before the loop.
- Avoid jokes at your customers' expense. Laugh with them about the situation.

2. For each, write one line under 12 words and describe the image or 2 second clip to pair it with.
3. Mark the 3 strongest and add an optional second line for each.
Rules: no voice, the line must read in one glance, images and sounds we have the right to use, laugh with [audience] and never at them.
