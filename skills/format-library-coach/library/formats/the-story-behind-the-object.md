# The story behind the object

## Shape

The video shows one object up close, then tells the unlikely story of how it came to exist or came to be used. The story has a setup (who needed it and why), a turn (the strange request or accident) and a payoff (where the object ended up). The object stays on screen or comes back at the end.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the object in close-up and the first line of the story, often a date and a strange request.
2. 2 to 40 s: the setup: who, where, what problem they had.
3. 40 to 90 s: the turn, the moment the object was invented, adapted or used in an unexpected way.
4. Last seconds: back to the object today, and one sentence linking the story to it.

## Make it yours

Tell the story behind one component of your product: an algorithm, a design choice, a dataset, a piece of hardware. A speech-to-text startup tells where the model behind its noise filtering comes from. A hardware AI company tells the history of the sensor it uses. A payments SaaS tells why a card number has the shape it has. You can also tell the story of a famous object in your category. Either way, the object becomes the hook and your product sits at the end of the story.

## Variations

- Product launch: introduce a new feature through the idea that inspired it (see the chocolate example).
- Founder object: the story of one object on your desk that shaped the company.
- Series: one object per episode, same opening shot.

## Why it works

A familiar object paired with an unlikely origin creates a gap between what the viewer sees and what they know, and the story closes it (Loewenstein 1994). A surprising story about an everyday thing is easy to retell, which makes it shareable (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- Check every fact of the story against a reliable source. Popular object stories often carry myths.
- Use archive images only within their license or platform rules.
- Keep the link to your product to one sentence at the end.

## For your AI

```
Write a "Story behind the object" video for [company], which makes [product] for [audience].
1. List 5 objects or components linked to [product or category] with a surprising origin. For each, give the source to check the facts.
2. For the best one, write the opening line (a date or a strange request, under 15 words), the setup, the turn and the payoff.
3. Close with one sentence linking the object to [product].
4. Shot notes: close-up of the object, b-roll or greenscreen with archive images, return to the object at the end.
Rules: every fact checked against a source, archive images within license, product mention limited to the last sentence, 45 to 120 seconds.
```
