# The last one doing it by hand (satire character)

## Shape

A character speaks to camera and proudly presents himself as one of the last people doing a job the old way. He describes his craft with the solemnity of an artisan. Every line, without saying it, mocks the old way that the product replaces.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the self-introduction ("I'm one of the last remaining artisanal...").
2. 2 to 40 s: the craft described with pride, each detail more painful than the last.
3. 40 to 90 s: the character dismisses the new way, and his arguments make the new way look better.
4. Last seconds: a final proud line, and a closing card or mention of the product (or the sponsor).

## Make it yours

Pick the manual task your product automates and give it a proud defender. An AI bookkeeping tool films "one of the last artisanal receipt typists". A no-code website builder films "a master of hand-written HTML tables". An AI meeting notes app films "the last professional minute-taker who writes in shorthand". The founder can play the character, which shows humour, or hire an actor.

## Variations

- Workshop tour: the character shows his tools and workspace like a craft museum.
- Apprentice: he tries to train a young apprentice who keeps using the new tool.
- Customer testimonial parody: his only remaining client praises how slow he is.

## Why it works

The character turns the pain of the old way into a joke, so viewers laugh at a problem they have. People who still do that task recognize themselves and share it with colleagues.

## Watch out

- Mock the task and leave real workers in that job out of the target.
- If a brand sponsors the video, disclose it with the platform's paid partnership label.
- Keep product claims accurate in the closing line.
