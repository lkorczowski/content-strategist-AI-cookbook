# Street interview: one question about your market

## Shape

The interviewer asks the same single question about a market to strangers, or to people who work in that field, in the place where they gather. The answers are cut fast, one after another, a few seconds each. The video ends on the best or the most surprising answer.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the question on screen and spoken, already facing the first person ("What's one startup that you think is criminally underrated right now?").
2. 2 to 30 s: five to eight answers, cut hard, with the person's role or context as a short caption.
3. 30 to 50 s: a pattern appears (everyone names the same pain, or nobody knows the answer), shown with two answers back to back.
4. Last 5 to 10 s: the strongest answer, held a little longer, then an on-screen question that invites viewers to give their own answer in the comments.

## Make it yours

Pick the one question your buyers argue about and go where they are. An AI scheduling tool for clinics asks nurses at the end of a shift what wastes the most time in their week. A payroll SaaS for restaurants asks cooks and managers outside a trade show what they would fix first. A developer tool asks engineers at a meetup which tool they would delete tomorrow. You can stay behind the camera: the answers are the content, and your product appears only in the caption or in a pinned comment.

## Variations

- Field interview: ask inside the venue of the trade (a gym, a conference hall, a coworking space) instead of the open street, as in the gym example.
- Same question, new city: keep the exact question and run it as a numbered series.
- Answer reveal: end with your own answer to camera, in one sentence.

## Why it works

A single question opens a gap the viewer wants to close by hearing every answer, and comparing the answers with their own (Loewenstein 1994). Real people's words also act as free market research that the viewer can repeat.

## Watch out

- Ask for consent on camera and get a signed release from anyone you show, especially for ads.
- A vague question gives flat answers. Test it on three people before filming.
- Some venues forbid filming: ask the organizer first.

## For your AI

```
Plan a "street interview: one question about your market" video for [company], which sells [product] to [audience].
1. Propose 10 single questions [audience] would answer in under 10 seconds and that reveal a pain or an opinion about [market]. Mark the 3 most likely to give surprising answers.
2. For the best question, say where to film (a place where [audience] gathers), how to approach people in one sentence, and the consent and release step.
3. Write the edit plan: opening on-screen text (the question, under 12 words), the order of answers, the pattern beat, the final answer and the comment prompt.
Rules: real answers only, no paid actors presented as strangers, no product pitch inside the interview, release form for every recognizable person.
```
