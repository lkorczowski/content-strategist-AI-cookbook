# Satisfying making-of

## Shape

A machine or a pair of hands makes the product from start to finish, filmed in close-up. The real sound of the process carries the video, with no talking. It ends on the finished product, held up or in use.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the raw material and the first movement of the machine or the hands, in close-up.
2. 2 to 40 s: the steps of the process, each shot tight, with the real sound kept loud.
3. 40 to 55 s: the most satisfying moment: the reveal of a shape, a perfect fit, a peel.
4. Last seconds: the finished product, then a short caption on who made it.

## Make it yours

This shape fits founders who make physical things or who power the machines that do. A startup making custom laser engraving software films the laser drawing a customer logo. A 3D printing service films a part printed layer by layer. An AI-driven bakery equipment maker films the machine shaping dough. For pure software, film the physical output the software controls: a print, a cut, a pour. Keep the camera close and steady, and let the sound do the work.

## Variations

- Custom piece: the making-of of one object made for one client, as in the jewelry example.
- Impossible-looking process: a process that looks unreal, used as a scroll stopper.
- Reverse: start with the finished product, then rewind to the raw material.

## Why it works

A process that unfolds step by step makes the viewer want to see the finished result, and the close-up detail rewards every second (Loewenstein 1994). The real sound adds a sensory pull that keeps people watching without words.

## Watch out

- Use the real sound of the process. Licensed music on a brand account must come from the platform's commercial library.
- Show customer logos or custom pieces only with the customer's permission.
- Follow safety rules on camera: guards on, protective gear on.

## For your AI

```
Plan a "satisfying making-of" video for [company], which makes [product] or powers [machine or process].
1. List the steps of the process from raw material to finished product, and mark the 3 most satisfying moments (shape reveal, perfect fit, peel, pour).
2. Write a shot list of 6 to 10 close-ups in order, with the sound to capture for each and a length in seconds.
3. Write the final shot and a caption of under 15 words.
Rules: real sound only or the platform's commercial music library, no talking, customer pieces shown with permission, safety gear visible.
```
