# I tested a celebrity's routine

## Shape

The creator picks the routine, diet, workout or method of a famous person and follows it for real, for a day, a week or longer. The video shows the attempt in short scenes, with the hard moments kept in. It ends with an honest verdict on what worked and what did not.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the promise on screen with the famous person's routine named ("I tried an actor's superhero workout").
2. 2 to 40 s: the rules of the routine, then the first attempt.
3. 40 to 120 s: the struggle and the turning point: a failure, a surprise, a small win.
4. Last 10 to 20 s: the verdict to camera, in one or two sentences, and what you would keep.

## Make it yours

You test the routine of a famous founder or CEO on your own work week, so the topic stays close to your audience. "I ran my week like a famous CEO's schedule" works for any founder. A founder of an AI calendar assistant tries a famous executive's time-blocking method and shows the calendar. A founder of a focus app tries a famous writer's daily routine. A founder of a health-tracking startup tries a well-known athlete's sleep routine and shows the data. Your product appears where it helps, as a tool you used during the test.

## Variations

- Episode series: one goal over many episodes, like the series where a 48-year-old trains to dunk.
- Customer routine: test the routine of one of your own customers, with their consent.
- Duo test: two people try the same routine and compare.

## Why it works

The famous name gives the viewer a reason to care, and the open question "can a normal person do it?" holds until the verdict (Loewenstein 1994). Series episodes leave the result open, which brings viewers back (Zeigarnik 1927).

## Watch out

- Base the routine on what the person has said or shown in public, and say where it comes from.
- Avoid presenting the celebrity as endorsing you. Use their name to describe the test only.
- Health and diet routines can be risky: add a safety note and skip anything dangerous.

Rules: routine based on public sources only, no implied endorsement by the famous person, no dangerous health practice, honest verdict even if negative.
