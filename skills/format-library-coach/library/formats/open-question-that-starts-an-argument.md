# Open question that starts an argument

## Shape

The speaker asks one question to camera, a question with many valid answers that people hold strong opinions about. They give a few framing details, then give their own answer at the very end or keep it to themselves. The comments become the content.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the question, word for word, also written on screen.
2. 2 to 20 s: the frame: what counts, what does not, one example to get people started.
3. 20 to 45 s: a hint of your own position, enough to provoke disagreement.
4. Last seconds: your answer in one line, or "tell me yours first", then cut.

## Make it yours

Ask the question your market loves to argue about. A CRM for agencies asks "which tool did everyone love and then quietly stop using?". An AI coding assistant asks "what is the most overrated programming habit?". A hiring platform asks "which interview question tells you nothing about a candidate?". Each answer in the comments tells you what your audience thinks, and the best ones become your next comment-reply videos.

## Variations

- Ranked answer: come back with a second video ranking the top answers from the comments.
- Two-option version: force a choice between two answers, which makes commenting faster.
- Local twist: ask about your city, your niche or your industry only.

## Why it works

An open question is an unfinished task, and the viewer closes it by typing an answer (Zeigarnik 1927). People recognize their own view in the topic and tag the friend who disagrees, which spreads the video.

## Watch out

- Pick questions that start friendly arguments. Questions about identity, politics or tragedy bring hostility you cannot moderate.
- Answer and like comments in the first hours; a silent creator kills the thread.
- Avoid questions that invite naming and shaming real private people or companies.

## For your AI

```
Write "open question" videos for [company], whose audience is [audience] in [field].
1. Propose 10 questions with many valid answers that [audience] holds strong, friendly opinions about.
2. For the best 3, write: the question as said and written on screen, a 2-sentence frame with one example answer, and our own closing answer (or the "tell me yours first" ending).
3. For each, suggest the follow-up video we can make from the comments.
Rules: no questions on identity, politics or tragedy, no naming private people, plan to reply to comments in the first hours.
```
