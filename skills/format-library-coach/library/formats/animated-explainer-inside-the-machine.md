# Animated explainer (inside the machine)

## Shape

A 2D or 3D animation travels inside something people cannot see: a cell, a machine, a model, a data pipeline. A simple voice-over narrates each step as the camera moves through it. The video ends when the process completes and the camera pulls back out.

## Beats

1. 0 to 3 s: a question about something invisible ("How do you sense cold or heat?") and the camera starting its dive inside.
2. 3 to 40 s: the first steps of the process, one element at a time, each named on screen.
3. 40 to 100 s: the key moment, where the input turns into the output, slowed down.
4. Last seconds: the camera pulls out to the familiar outside view, with one sentence on what just happened.

## Make it yours

AI products are hard to show because the work is invisible. An animation shows what happens inside the model or the pipeline. A document AI shows a page turned into structured data, token by token. A fraud detection startup shows one transaction traveling through its checks. A vector database company shows a search query finding its nearest neighbors. Keep the science honest and simple; the animation can become a reusable asset for your site and sales deck.

## Variations

- Simple 2D: flat shapes and text, at lower cost (see the multiplication example).
- Founder voice: the founder narrates the animation for a personal touch.
- Series: one invisible process per episode.

## Why it works

Showing the inside of something everyone uses but nobody sees answers a question people did not know they had. Clear visual metaphors make a complex topic feel easy, and that feeling of understanding is what people share.

## Watch out

- Simplify without lying. Experts will flag a misleading animation of how a model works.
- Credit animators and any third-party assets, as the second example does.
- High effort: write and validate the script before any animation work starts.

## For your AI

```
Write an "Animated explainer (inside the machine)" script for [company], whose [product] does [invisible process] for [audience].
1. Write the opening question about the invisible process (under 12 words).
2. Break the process into 5 to 7 steps. For each: one sentence of voice-over and a visual description (what moves, what appears, what is named on screen).
3. Mark the key step to slow down and describe the pull-back shot at the end.
4. List what an expert should validate before animation starts.
Rules: simplify without misleading, credit animators and assets, no face needed, 45 to 120 seconds.
```
