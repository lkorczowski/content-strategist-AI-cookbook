# Hook stacking: titles the right reader cannot skip

A good title makes the reader it targets think "this is for me". They click because they disagree, or because they have never seen the idea before. A title any reader can skim past has failed, even when it is accurate.

## Clarity first

A non-expert must understand the subject in one read. Put the plain keyword the reader would search ("pay creators on performance", "cold email") in the title, ideally near the start. Hooks go around a clear subject, never in place of it. A plain question or claim plus one known name beats a pile of names and metaphors.

## The rule: three hooks or more, in few words

1. A known name, place or authority the reader respects or resents: a famous company, a legend of the trade, a platform, a startup board.
2. A concrete number or stake: an age, a share ("the wrong 10%"), a before and after, a cost, a deadline, "your startup will die".
3. A tension: a contrarian claim, an accusation, the death of an established model, a confession from inside, an unlikely hero, a fear.
4. Optional: a reader marker that filters the audience ("for founders who hate hype", "(I sat on one)").
5. Optional: a desired outcome carried by a character (desire hook, below).

Each hook is also a search keyword. Write the hooks next to the title: the list feeds the SEO keywords, the tags and the argument of the piece.

## Desire hook: outcome plus character

For a piece that should bring buyers or leads, not only views. The method was shared by Kallaway (TikTok @kallaway.marketing, "Desire Hook method", 2026-11-07); the wording below is ours.

1. Lead with the outcome the reader wants, as a before and after with a time frame, not with information. An information hook ("5 tips for X") pulls viewers who rarely buy.
2. Carry the outcome by a character the reader relates to: the author, the reader ("you") or a third party.
3. Keep the method hidden: the outcome plus the character create the wish to learn how.

Numbers in a desire hook must be real and cleared for use. Never invent a before and after.

## Steps

1. List the raw material: the story, the claim, the numbers, the people.
2. Find the reader's belief to attack, or the gap they do not know they have.
3. Draft three variants, each stacking different hooks.
4. Annotate each: "hooks stacked: legend, number, death of a model, AI".
5. Run the checks. Keep the variant with the most hooks that still reads as one natural sentence.
6. The H1 can be long and provocative. The SEO title is a separate, shorter line led by the keyword.

## Checks

- Would the target reader argue with it, or feel they have never read it before? If neither, rewrite.
- One concrete noun and one number or stake at least.
- It says who it is for, directly or through the hooks.
- Understood without explanation. A metaphor that needs decoding loses the click.
- A confession in the first person works when it shows insider authority ("I sat on one"). It fails when it shows a bad decision ("I wasted a fortune on agencies").
- Not a definition ("What does X do", "X: A, B, now C"). Turn it into a fight, a death, a rarity or a cost.
- A conversion piece with an information hook: rewrite toward the outcome.
- Confidentiality: no client name the author has not cleared.
- No em dash, no emoji. A clean negation is allowed when it carries the tension; prefer the affirmative form.

Then run statur-humanizer on the short list before showing it.

## Patterns that work

- "Why [respected group] struggles to [obvious goal]"
- "Why [new model] will kill [current model] thanks to [force]"
- "Your [thing the reader owns] will die if you don't solve [problem]"
- "Your [trusted tool] is optimizing the wrong [number] of [thing]"
- "Why [bad thing] gets [huge number] and [good thing] gets [tiny number]"
- "What [legend] would do with [modern tool]"
- "A [age]-year-old [unlikely hero] thought [common myth]. Now [outcome]"
- "The most [dull adjective] [thing] in [place] beat [giant] with [cheap means]"
- "Anyone with [absurdly low resource] can now [scary capability]"
- "What [group] get wrong about [topic] (I [insider credential])"
- "Anyone who [promises X] is lying. Here is the math."
- "How to go from [before] to [after] [outcome] in [time frame]"
- "How [character] went from [before] to [after] in [time frame] with this [N]-step system"

## Invented examples

| Weak | Why | Stronger | Hooks |
|---|---|---|---|
| What does a growth advisor do? | Definition, no stake | Why the best startups can't hire a growth advisor | startups, hiring, scarcity |
| Captions and virality | One hook, abstract | Your AI editor is polishing the wrong 10% of your video | AI tool, number, contrarian |
| Tips for local SEO | Information hook | How a small bakery went from page 4 to the map pack in 60 days | character, before and after, time frame |

## Rules

- Drafts only: the skill proposes titles, a person publishes.
- No passwords, no accounts.
- No promised views or results.
- No invented numbers: every number in a title comes from the author's data or a source they can open.
- Respect each platform's terms and the clients' confidentiality.
