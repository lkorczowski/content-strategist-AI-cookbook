# Cold-open history story

## Shape

The video drops the viewer in the middle of the most dramatic moment of a true story, with zero context. Then it rewinds to explain who these people were and how they got there. It closes on the lesson the story teaches.

## Beats

1. 0 to 3 s: the action, told in the present or as if it is happening ("the cyclists pushed their way through the crowd, stormed into the cafe...").
2. 3 to 60 s: the rewind: who, when, what was at stake.
3. 60 to 150 s: the events that lead back to the opening moment, then what happened next.
4. Last seconds: the lesson in one or two sentences, tied to the viewer's life or work.

## Make it yours

Pick true stories from the history of your industry or of the problem you solve. A cybersecurity startup opens on the moment a famous breach was discovered. A logistics AI opens on a historic shipping disaster. A fintech for small businesses opens on a merchant in a famous financial panic. You tell the story to camera or over b-roll, and the lesson at the end is the belief your company is built on, stated without a pitch.

## Variations

- Founder cold open: the most dramatic moment of your own company story, then the rewind.
- Customer cold open: a customer's crisis moment, told with their permission.
- Two-part: cut at the rewind and post the rest as part 2.

## Why it works

Starting in the middle raises questions (who are they, why are they doing this) that only the rest of the story answers, which holds attention (Loewenstein 1994). A true story with stakes carries emotion, and stories are easy to retell (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- The story must be true and checked. Name your source in the caption.
- The opening moment must be strong enough to stand alone, without context.
- Avoid sensational details about real victims or living people.

3. Tie the lesson to [audience]'s work in one or two sentences, without mentioning [product] by name.
4. Shot notes: facecam or b-roll, archive images within license, source in the caption.
Rules: true and checked facts only, respect real people, 60 to 180 seconds.
