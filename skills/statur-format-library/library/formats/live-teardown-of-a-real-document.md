# Live teardown of a real document

## Shape

The expert opens a real document from their field on screen (a pitch deck, an email, a landing page, a viral video) and walks through it part by part. At each slide or section, they point at what makes it work and say why. The expert stays visible in a corner or in front of the document with a greenscreen.

## Beats

1. 0 to 3 s: the context that makes the document worth seeing ("We just invested in this company, here is the deck") with the document already on screen.
2. 3 to 60 s: the first parts, one point each, with a highlight or a circle on the exact element.
3. 60 to 150 s: the part that breaks a common rule or surprises, held longest.
4. Last seconds: the one lesson to steal, and an offer to tear down a viewer's document next.

## Make it yours

Tear down documents your buyers write or read. A sales email tool breaks down a cold email that booked a meeting. A landing page builder breaks down a page that converts, section by section. A presentation AI breaks down a famous launch deck. You show your judgement on real material, which is the clearest proof of expertise you can post.

## Variations

- Viral video teardown: break down why one video spread (see the third example).
- Bad example edition: a real document that failed, with permission, and what to fix.
- Series: one teardown per week, same opening line.

## Why it works

Seeing a real document from behind the curtain feels like insider access, and sharing it makes the sharer look in the know (Berger 2013). Each pointed element is a lesson the viewer can apply to their own work the same day.

## Watch out

- Ask permission before showing a real document that is private, and blur names, numbers and contact details that were not made public.
- Public videos and pages can be discussed, but credit the creator and keep clips short.
- Point at what works; a teardown that only mocks reads as cheap.

## For your AI

```
Plan a "Live teardown of a real document" video for [founder name], founder of [company], which helps [audience] with [task].
1. Document: [a pitch deck, email, page or video] that is public or shared with permission. State why it is worth seeing in one line.
2. Write the opening line (the context, under 15 words) with the document already on screen.
3. List 5 to 7 points, each tied to one exact element to highlight, with one sentence on why it works.
4. Pick the most surprising point and give it the most time. End with one lesson to steal and an invitation to submit a document.
5. Shot notes: screen recording or greenscreen, founder visible, highlights on each element.
Rules: permission for private documents, blur private data, credit creators of public content, 60 to 180 seconds.
```
