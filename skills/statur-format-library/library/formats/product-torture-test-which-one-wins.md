# Product torture test: which one wins?

## Shape

The product and its alternatives go through the same test in the real world: speed, power, resistance. The video shows the setup, the measurement for each one and the moment one of them fails or wins. It ends on a clear winner.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the contenders side by side and the question ("Which of these is faster?").
2. 2 to 20 s: the method in one or two lines: same conditions, what is measured, how.
3. 20 to 120 s: each contender takes the test, with the result shown on screen as a number or a visible outcome, and the field narrows.
4. Last 5 to 15 s: the winner, the final measurement, and one sentence on what it means for the buyer.

## Make it yours

For software, the test is a real task with a stopwatch or a score. An AI transcription tool runs the same noisy recording through four tools and shows the error count. A spreadsheet automation startup times how long each tool takes to clean the same messy file. A website builder loads the same page on a slow connection and shows the load time. Show the screen for each run. You can include your own product, as long as the test is fair and you publish the method.

## Variations

- Escalation: raise the difficulty each round until only one contender is left.
- Viewer pick: viewers choose the contenders in the comments, you test them in the next video.
- Old versus new: your product today against your first version.

## Why it works

The question "which one wins?" opens a gap that only the result closes, and each elimination raises the stakes (Loewenstein 1994). A fair measurement is practical value: viewers save and share it when they have to choose (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- Same conditions for every contender, method shown on screen. A biased test invites public debunks.
- Naming competitors can raise legal issues on comparative claims. Stick to what you measured.
- Disclose that the account belongs to the company when your product is in the test.
