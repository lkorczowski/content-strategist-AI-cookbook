# Tier list of your own products

## Shape

The founders rank their own products, features or plans from best to worst, on a tier board in frame. Each item gets a place and a reason, and the founders argue when they disagree. The honest low rankings are the point: they show the team knows its weak spots.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the founders, the tier board and the promise ("We made 10 of these and today you'll know which one we prefer").
2. 2 to 20 s: the first items placed fast, with one reason each.
3. 20 to 90 s: the disagreements, the surprising low rankings, the item one founder defends.
4. Last 10 to 20 s: the top item, the final board, and a question to viewers about their own ranking.

## Make it yours

Rank what you make, with your co-founder or a teammate. An AI writing assistant ranks its own templates from most used to least loved. A project management SaaS ranks its integrations by how well they work today. A design tool ranks its own past logos and landing pages. Say why the low ones are low, and what you will do about them. Viewers trust a team that grades itself honestly.

## Variations

- Customer versus team: rank once yourselves, then show how customers ranked the same items.
- Old versions: rank every version of your product since the start.
- Solo with notes: one founder ranks, reading comments from the team.

## Why it works

Viewers guess where each item lands and stay to check, and the disagreement between founders adds a small stake (Loewenstein 1994). Honest low marks on your own work are rare, which makes the video memorable and the team credible.

## Watch out

- Keep the rankings real. A board where everything is top tier reads as an ad.
- Keep it about your own products. Ranking competitors is the next card, F115.
- Disclose that the account belongs to the company.

```
Plan a "tier list of your own products" video for [company], with [founder 1] and [founder 2], ranking [products, features, templates or plans].
1. List the items to rank (8 to 12) and propose an honest tier for each with a one-sentence reason, including at least 2 low rankings.
2. Mark the items where the founders are likely to disagree, and write the two sides of each argument.
3. Write the opening line, the order of placement, and the closing question to viewers.
Rules: honest rankings, reasons for every low rank and what you will do about it, only your own products on the board, disclose that the account belongs to [company].
```
