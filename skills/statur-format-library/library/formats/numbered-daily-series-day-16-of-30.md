# Numbered daily series (day 16 of 30)

## Shape

Every video opens with the same sentence: the series name, the counter and who you are ("Day 16 of pitch deck tips from me, a venture capitalist"). Then one tip or one example, delivered in under two minutes. The series name and the counter stay identical from episode to episode.

## Beats

1. 0 to 3 s: the fixed opening line, word for word, with the counter on screen.
2. 3 to 30 s: the tip or example of the day, stated in one sentence.
3. 30 to 90 s: one concrete illustration (a screen, an object, a case) and how to apply it.
4. Last seconds: a short closer that is the same every day, plus a teaser for tomorrow's topic if you have it.

## Make it yours

The series name is the format name, so pick one your buyers would search. A pricing analytics SaaS runs "Day X of 30 of pricing mistakes". A founder of an AI interview coach runs "one interview question a day". A design tool runs "one onboarding screen a day", each one a real public example. You shoot several episodes in one session, and the counter turns a simple tip into a habit for the viewer and a routine for you.

## Variations

- Open counter: "episode 21" with no end date (see the architecture example).
- Build in public: day X of building one feature, with the real progress.
- Weekly cadence: same opening, one episode per week, for founders with less time.

## Why it works

The counter tells the viewer there are earlier and later episodes, an unfinished series that people want to complete (Zeigarnik 1927). The fixed opening makes each new episode recognizable in the feed in the first second.

## Watch out

- Missing days breaks the promise of the counter. Batch-record before you start.
- Each episode must stand alone: a new viewer lands on day 16 without having seen day 1.
- Never change the series name mid-run; it is how people find the rest.

## For your AI

```
Plan a "Numbered daily series" for [founder name], founder of [company], which helps [audience] with [topic].
1. Propose 5 series names [audience] would search, each under 6 words, and pick the best.
2. Write the fixed opening line (series name, counter, who I am) and the fixed closing line.
3. List 30 episode topics, one tip or example each, ordered so the strongest come at days 1, 2 and 5.
4. Write the full script for day 1 with shot notes (facecam, counter on screen, one illustration).
Rules: each episode stands alone, the opening never changes, batch-record at least 7 episodes before posting day 1, 30 to 120 seconds each.
```
