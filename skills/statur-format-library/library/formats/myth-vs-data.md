# Myth vs data

## Shape

The speaker states a belief their market keeps repeating and says plainly that it is wrong. Then they show the number or the mechanism that breaks it, often on a greenscreen. The video ends with what to do instead.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the myth, in the words people use ("If you want more demos, post more often").
2. 2 to 8 s: the verdict. One sentence that says the belief fails.
3. 8 to 45 s: the proof. A chart, a study, your own data or the mechanism explained simply.
4. Last seconds: the action that replaces the myth, in one sentence.

## Make it yours

Your own data is the strongest proof you have, because nobody else can show it. An email deliverability tool debunks "send on Tuesday morning" with its own aggregated send data. An AI support chatbot debunks "customers hate bots" with its resolution logs, anonymized. A time-tracking app for agencies debunks "billable hours measure productivity" with a mechanism the founder explains on a whiteboard. Pick one myth per video and keep a running list from sales calls.

## Variations

- Greenscreen proof: the chart or study fills the background while you point at it.
- Long version: a repeated claim taken apart point by point (see the second example).
- Series: same opening card each time ("Myth #4"), one myth per episode.

## Why it works

Hearing that a belief is wrong opens a gap between what the viewer thought and what they need to know, the curiosity mechanism of Loewenstein 1994. A clear correction is useful to pass on, which gives it practical value in the sense of Berger 2013.

## Watch out

- The proof must be real and sourced. Show the source on screen or in the caption.
- Aggregate and anonymize customer data before showing it.
- Calling a belief wrong without a better action leaves the viewer with nothing to do.

## For your AI

```
Write a 30 to 60 second "myth vs data" script for [company], which makes [product] for [audience].
The myth: [belief my market repeats]. My proof: [our data, a study with its link, or the mechanism].
1. Write the myth as an opening line in my buyers' words, under 15 words.
2. Write the verdict, one sentence.
3. Write the proof beat: what to show on screen (chart, screenshot, whiteboard) and what to say over it.
4. Write the closing action, one sentence.
5. Add shot notes: facecam or greenscreen, source shown on screen.
Rules: only real numbers with a source, aggregate and anonymize customer data, no promised results.
```
