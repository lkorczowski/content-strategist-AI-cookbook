# Logic puzzle with a twist

## Shape

The speaker states a silly everyday problem as if it were a serious puzzle. Then they solve it with a real method from their field, drawn on a board or on screen, step by step. The twist is the contrast: a trivial goal, a rigorous method, and a solution that actually works.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the silly problem, said seriously to camera ("I want to arrive at work as late as possible without getting fired").
2. 2 to 20 s: the constraints, written on the board as if for an exam.
3. 20 to 60 s: the method applied, one step at a time, with the drawing growing.
4. Last seconds: the answer, the method named in one sentence, and where it is used for serious problems.

## Make it yours

This fits developer tools and data products, where the method is your daily work. A database company solves "which coffee shop line to join" with a queueing model. An optimization API plans the laziest route through a supermarket. An AI forecasting tool predicts when the office fridge runs out of milk. Your viewers learn a real concept, and the closing line connects it to the problem your product solves.

## Variations

- Viewer problem: solve a silly problem a commenter sends.
- Code edition: the solution is written and run on screen.
- Two methods: solve the same puzzle two ways and compare.

## Why it works

The silly problem makes the viewer curious about how a serious method could apply, a gap that only the solution closes (Loewenstein 1994). Learning a real concept through a joke gives people something smart to share.

## Watch out

- The method must be correct. Experts in your field will check every step.
- Keep the problem harmless: no advice on cheating an employer, a test or a system for real.
- One method per video. Stacking several loses non-experts.
