# Founder journey montage (day 1 to day 1,000)

## Shape

A fast montage of clips filmed since the first day of the company, in order, with a voice-over that counts the time in months and days. The voice names two or three hard moments as the clips pass. The last shot shows where the company stands today.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the count on screen and in the voice ("33 months, 1,009 days"), or a short quote that frames the journey.
2. 2 to 20 s: the starting point. The trip, the room or the conversation where the idea began, with the oldest clips.
3. 20 to 55 s: the hard part. Clips of failures, late nights, first customers, each tied to one short line of voice-over.
4. Last 10 to 15 s: today. The current team, product or office, and one sentence on what comes next.

## Make it yours

This format needs footage you filmed before you knew you would use it. Dig through your phone and your team chat. A developer tools startup shows the first terminal demo on a laptop, the first meetup, then the current dashboard. An AI note-taker for doctors shows the first pilot clinic, the rejected prototype, then the launch day. A hardware-plus-software team shows the garage bench, the broken units, then the first shipment. Post it on a milestone: launch, anniversary, funding round.

## Variations

- Founder on camera at the end: the montage closes on the founder today, speaking the last line to camera.
- Year by year: one chapter per year, each with a date stamp on screen.
- Team version: each team member narrates the month they joined.

## Why it works

The running count gives the viewer a clock to follow, and each hard moment raises the question of how it ends. Watching a real journey compressed into a minute triggers the pride and awe that Berger 2013 links to sharing.

## Watch out

- Without early footage the format falls flat. Start filming your days now, even if you never post them.
- Check the rights on any music, and blur customer data or screens visible in old clips.
- Ask former teammates before showing them.

1. Compute the exact time since day one in months and days, and write the opening line with that count.
2. Order the clips into 4 beats: the start, the hard moments, the turn, today.
3. Write the voice-over, one short line per clip, under 130 words in total.
4. Add shot notes: clip length, where to put date stamps, the final shot.
Rules: only real clips and true dates, name hard moments honestly, no invented numbers, flag any clip that shows a person or customer data that needs permission.
