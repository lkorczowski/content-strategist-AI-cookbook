# Split-screen face-off (us vs them)

## Shape

Two sides of a cliché face off on a split screen: two countries, two jobs, the old way and the new way. Each side answers the other line for line, often played by the same person. The exchange escalates until one side wins or both look absurd.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: both sides on screen with labels, and the first line from one side.
2. 2 to 25 s: line for line, each side answers with its own habit.
3. 25 to 50 s: the escalation, the most extreme contrast.
4. Last seconds: the final blow from one side, and the other side's silent reaction.

## Make it yours

Put the old tool against the new tool, or your user against the person they used to be. An AI spreadsheet assistant shows "analyst in 2015" against "analyst today", answering the same request. A design handoff tool shows the designer and the developer reacting to the same file. A no-code platform shows the engineering backlog against the ops team building it themselves. You can play both sides with a costume change.

## Variations

- Countries or cultures: two markets react to the same product moment, with care for clichés.
- Three panels: bad, average, great.
- Viewer pick: end with "which side are you?".

## Why it works

The contrast makes the point without a speech, and each line sets up the next. Viewers pick a side and tag friends who belong to the other one, which spreads the video.

## Watch out

- Keep cultural or national clichés friendly. Mocking a group your buyers belong to costs you trust.
- Avoid naming a competitor in a way that breaks comparative advertising rules.
- Lip-sync and timing matter in split screen: rehearse.

## For your AI

```
Write a "split-screen face-off" for [company], whose product [product] replaces [old way] for [audience].
1. Propose 5 pairs that contrast [old way] and [product] (old tool vs new tool, past self vs present self, two roles).
2. For the best pair, write: the side labels, 6 to 8 alternating lines that escalate, the final blow and the silent reaction. Add split-screen and costume notes if one person plays both sides.
Rules: friendly clichés only, no competitor named against comparative advertising rules, no invented claims.
```
