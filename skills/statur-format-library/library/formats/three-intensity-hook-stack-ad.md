# Three-intensity hook stack ad

## Shape

One ad body is shot once, and three hooks are shot separately at three intensities, each in its own setting. The safe hook is filmed at the desk and stacks authority, the middle hook in a meeting room, the bold hook outdoors and sounds the most personal. Each hook makes the right viewer recognize themselves, then the same body runs: problem, solution, origin story, metaphor, a case with numbers, proof, and a call to book a short eligibility call.

## Beats

1. 0 to 3 s: one of the three hooks; each ad version uses one.
2. 3 to 40 s: the problem made concrete, then the solution in one sentence.
3. 40 to 90 s: the origin story, one metaphor, one real case with its numbers, and short proof quotes.
4. Last 15 s: the call to action: who it is for, why now, and a short call to check eligibility.

## Make it yours

You write one body and spend most of the effort on the hooks. A sales coaching startup opens safe ("I have trained 200 sales teams, here is what the top ones do"), middle ("Your team is losing deals in the first call"), and bold, outdoors ("Most sales training is theatre"). An AI compliance tool does the same with its buyer's fear of an audit. Each hook spends most of its words grabbing attention and the rest telling the right person "this is for you". Run the three versions as separate ads and keep the one that wins. The method was written for a real program; this card stays a draft until results are in.

## Variations

- Hook only test: shoot five hooks for the same body and test them all.
- Customer hook: a client delivers the bold hook, the founder delivers the body.
- Short body: a 30-second body for retargeting viewers who already know you.

## Why it works

Different viewers stop for different signals: authority for the cautious, a sharp claim for the bold. Matching the setting to the intensity makes each hook coherent at a glance, and self-qualification brings the right people to the call.

## Watch out

- Every number in the case must be real, with the client's consent for quotes.
- No promised result: describe what the program does and how it is delivered.
- Follow the ad platform's rules for claims in your category.
