# App demo in the real place

## Shape

The app is used where the problem happens: in the supermarket, on the job site, at the front desk. A real case is solved from start to finish. The edit cuts between the place and the phone or laptop screen at each step.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the problem in context, in one sentence ("When you're diabetic, shopping gets complicated").
2. 2 to 15 s: the place and the real case: the product on the shelf, the broken pipe, the client waiting.
3. 15 to 55 s: the app in use, screen shown at each step, with the result visible in the real world.
4. Last 5 to 10 s: the outcome of the case and one line on who the app is for.

## Make it yours

Go where your user has the problem and solve one real case. An AI quoting app for plumbers is used at a customer's sink, from the photo to the quote sent. A food allergy scanner is used in a grocery aisle on three real products. A field inspection app is used on a real site, from the checklist to the report. You can be on camera or only your hands. Record the screen in high quality and cut to it at every action, so the viewer never loses the thread.

## Variations

- Customer demo: a real customer uses the app in their own place, with their consent.
- Speed run: the same real case solved against a timer.
- Series: one real place per episode, same opening line.

## Why it works

Seeing the app solve a problem in the place where it happens is practical value the viewer can picture in their own life (Berger 2013). The real setting also makes the demo more credible than a clean studio screen.

## Watch out

- Ask the store, site or client for permission to film, and keep other customers out of frame.
- Show a real case with real results. A staged result breaks trust with the buyers who try it.
- For health or safety apps, keep claims to what the app actually does.
