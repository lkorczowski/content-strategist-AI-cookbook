# Detail breakdown: watch this one thing

## Shape

The expert plays a scene, a song or a product on screen and asks the viewer to watch one small detail ("Watch this curtain"). They replay the moment, point at the detail each time it appears and explain how it holds the whole thing together. Once seen, the detail is impossible to miss.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the instruction ("Watch this...") over the clip, with the detail circled.
2. 2 to 40 s: the clip replayed slowly, the detail pointed at each time, counted out loud.
3. 40 to 90 s: why the creator put it there and what would break without it.
4. Last seconds: the clip at normal speed one last time, so the viewer sees it with new eyes.

## Make it yours

Pick one small detail of your product interface and explain why it exists. A note-taking app shows the half-second delay before autosave and the reason behind it. An AI email assistant shows one word it never uses and why. A checkout SaaS shows the exact placement of one button and the test behind it. You can also break down a detail in a famous product of your category, which positions you as someone with taste.

## Variations

- Product detail: your own interface, with the design decision behind it.
- Song or ad detail: one detail in a famous ad from your market.
- Count edition: count how many times the detail appears, then reveal the total.

## Why it works

The instruction to watch one thing turns the viewer into a searcher, and the first spotting feels like a small win. Learning to see a hidden detail is something people want to show friends (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- Clips from films, songs and ads are copyrighted. Keep them short, add commentary, and check each platform's rules on reused content.
- The detail must be real and verifiable. An invented intention gets corrected by people who know.
- One detail per video. Several details dilute the moment of seeing.

```

1. List 5 small details in [product] or in a famous product of [category] that carry more than people think.
2. For the best one, write the opening instruction ("Watch this...", under 6 words), the moments to replay and point at, and the explanation of why the detail is there.
3. End with the clip at normal speed and one line on what viewers will notice from now on.
4. Shot notes: screen recording or greenscreen, the detail circled each time.
Rules: one detail only, every design intention must be true, short clips with commentary for third-party content, 60 to 120 seconds.
```
