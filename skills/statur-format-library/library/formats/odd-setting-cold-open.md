# Odd-setting cold open

## Shape

The video opens with the speaker in an unexpected place or situation, and the first line names it ("You might be wondering why I'm sitting in a kayak"). The speaker explains how they got there, and the place turns into the metaphor for the point they want to make. The ending ties the setting back to the lesson.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the odd setting in a wide shot and the line that acknowledges it.
2. 2 to 20 s: the short story of why you are there.
3. 20 to 55 s: the turn. The setting becomes the metaphor for your point about work, your market or your product.
4. Last seconds: one sentence that links the place and the lesson, ideally with a final shot of the setting.

## Make it yours

Pick a place that already holds your idea, then build the script from it. A founder of an AI tool for unfinished side projects films in a garage full of abandoned hobby gear. A founder of a calm-inbox app films in a library where nobody can speak. A logistics software founder films sitting on a pallet in a warehouse to talk about the hidden cost of waiting. Ask yourself: "what does this place prove about my point?" If the answer is nothing, change the place.

## Variations

- Odd situation instead of odd place: halfway up a ladder, in a costume, mid-chore.
- Recurring setting: the same strange spot every episode, which becomes your signature.
- Silent cold open: the first 2 seconds show the setting without words, the line comes after.

## Why it works

An unexpected image in the first second raises a question ("why there?") that the viewer stays to answer (Loewenstein 1994). The metaphor makes the point easier to remember, because the place carries it.

## Watch out

- The setting must pay off. A random place with no link to the point reads as a gimmick.
- Respect safety and filming rules on location, and ask before filming on private property.
- Keep the setup short. The explanation of why you are there should stay under 20 seconds.
