# Read the post that changed my mind

## Shape

The speaker puts a post, thread or email on screen with a greenscreen. They say how long ago they read it and how often they still think about it. Then they read the key lines aloud and explain how it changed the way they work.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the post behind you and the line that frames it ("I read this four years ago and I think about it every week").
2. 2 to 40 s: the key lines read aloud, highlighted on screen as you read.
3. 40 to 80 s: what changed for you. One decision or habit that came from it.
4. Last seconds: the takeaway in one sentence, and the author credited.

## Make it yours

Pick a piece of writing that shaped how you build your company. A founder of an AI product analytics tool reads the essay that convinced them to talk to users every week. A founder of a developer tools startup reads the thread that made them open-source their core. A founder of a customer feedback platform reads the customer email that changed the roadmap, with the customer's permission. You borrow the authority of a great post and add the proof of what you did with it.

## Variations

- Book page: a paragraph from a book instead of a post, briefly quoted and credited.
- Customer email: a message from a user, anonymized or with permission.
- My own old post: the founder reads something they wrote years ago and says what they got wrong.

## Why it works

The framing line ("I think about this every week") tells viewers this idea has passed a long test, which makes them want to know it. Ideas that make people better at their work are shared for their practical value (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- Credit the author on screen and in the caption.
- Read a few key lines and explain the rest in your own words. Reading a whole post or book page can break copyright.
- Get permission before showing a private email or message.
