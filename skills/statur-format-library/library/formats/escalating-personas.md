# Escalating personas

## Shape

One person plays several versions of the same character, each at a higher stake. Every new version opens with the same sentence and a bigger number, then shows what changes in their day at that level. The last version reaches a level where the habits become absurd or surprising.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: version 1 says the setup line with the smallest stake, on camera.
2. 2 to 30 s: versions 2 and 3, same sentence, bigger stake, one new problem or habit each. A small costume change marks each level.
3. 30 to 50 s: the top version, where the job looks completely different.
4. Last seconds: a short closing line from the top version that sums up the gap, or a cut back to version 1.

## Make it yours

Use it to explain the tiers of your market or the plans of your product. A cloud cost tool plays the startup spending a little, the scale-up spending more, the enterprise with a whole team on it. An HR platform plays the founder with 5 employees, 50, then 500, and what payroll looks like at each. An AI customer support tool plays the shop answering emails by hand, the team with macros, the company with AI triage. Each persona shows what breaks at that level, which is where your product fits.

## Variations

- Reverse escalation: start at the top level and go down.
- Time escalation: same person at year 1, year 3, year 10 of the job.
- Two characters: a buyer and a seller escalate together.

## Why it works

The repeated opening sentence sets a pattern, and each bigger stake makes the viewer guess what changes next. It explains a complex market as a ladder anyone can follow, and the top level is an open question until the end.

## Watch out

- Every stake you name must be realistic for your market. Numbers in the script need a source or must be clearly fictional.
- A costume change per level helps; without it, the levels blur together.
- Keep each level to one idea. Too many details per persona kills the rhythm.
