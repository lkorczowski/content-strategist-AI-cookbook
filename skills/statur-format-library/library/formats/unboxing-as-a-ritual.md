# Unboxing as a ritual

## Shape

The product is opened slowly, layer by layer, in close-up. Every seal, flap and insert is shown with its real sound. The video ends with the first use of the product.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the closed box and a one-line hook ("Is there anything more satisfying than this?").
2. 2 to 30 s: the outer layers: seal, sleeve, lid, each opened slowly.
3. 30 to 70 s: the inner layers and accessories, laid out one by one.
4. Last 5 to 15 s: the first use, the product switched on or put to work.

## Make it yours

For hardware, the box is the stage. For software, make the "box" the first-run experience: the welcome screen, the setup steps, the first result, recorded slowly with clean sound design. A smart home device startup films its own packaging as a ritual. An AI camera startup films the unboxing and the first photo. A SaaS company that ships a welcome kit to new customers films the kit, then cuts to the first login. Keep hands steady and the light soft, and let each layer have its own moment.

## Variations

- Gift unboxing: a creator opens a gift from a brand, with the gift disclosed.
- Customer unboxing: a real customer's first unboxing, with their consent.
- Software ritual: the onboarding flow filmed as an unboxing, screen by screen.

## Why it works

Each layer hides the next one, so the viewer keeps watching to see what is inside and how the product looks in use (Loewenstein 1994). The slow pace and the real sounds give a sensory pleasure that makes the product feel valuable.

## Watch out

- Disclose gifted products and paid partnerships with the platform's tools.
- Use real sound, or music from the platform's commercial library on brand accounts.
- Show the product as it ships. Extra props or edited packaging mislead buyers.
