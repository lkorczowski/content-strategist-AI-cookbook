# This or that, answered

## Shape

The speaker asks a question with two answers people argue about, often on location where the question lives. They give a clear verdict in the first seconds. Then they explain the reason most people miss, which is the real value of the video.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the question with its two options ("A or B?").
2. 2 to 8 s: the verdict, said plainly, with a hint that the reason is unexpected.
3. 8 to 55 s: the reason most people miss, with one example shown on location or on screen.
4. Last seconds: the one exception, or the practical rule to remember.

## Make it yours

Your buyers argue about small choices all the time. You settle one per video, with a verdict and a reason. A project management tool answers "daily standup or weekly sync?". An AI customer support startup answers "chatbot first or human first?". A pricing tool for SaaS answers "monthly or annual billing by default?". Film where the question lives when you can (the office, the call, the dashboard), so the answer looks grounded in real work.

## Variations

- Viewer vote first: ask the question, pause, then give the verdict.
- Expert duel: two people defend each side, then one verdict.
- It depends, done well: the verdict is a simple rule that tells each viewer which side fits them.

## Why it works

The two options pull viewers into the debate in the first second, and the early verdict makes them stay to hear the reason. A clear rule for a common choice is practical enough to save and share (Berger 2013, practical value).

## Watch out

- Give the verdict early. Holding it to the end turns a useful video into a frustrating one.
- The reason must be true and specific to your field. A generic reason makes the verdict feel arbitrary.
- On health, money or safety, keep the rule general and safe.

## For your AI

```
Script a "this or that, answered" video for [company], which serves [audience].
1. List 10 A-or-B questions my audience argues about in [field].
2. For the best one, give the verdict and the reason most people miss, with the evidence or experience behind it: [what I know].
3. Write a 45 to 75 second script: the question, the verdict within 8 seconds, the reason with one example, the exception or rule to remember.
4. Add shot notes: where to film (the place where the question lives), text on screen for A and B.
Rules: verdict in the first 8 seconds, true and specific reason, no invented numbers, product mentioned at most once.
```
