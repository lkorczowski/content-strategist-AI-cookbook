# Mission launch: the problem nobody fixed, so we built it

## Shape

The founder names a big problem that everyone has accepted for years. They say they got tired of waiting for someone else to fix it, and reveal what the team built. The product is shown working, and the video ends with where to get it.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the problem in one sentence, stated as a fact the viewer already feels.
2. 2 to 25 s: why it stayed broken, and the moment the founder stopped waiting.
3. 25 to 75 s: the reveal. The product on screen, doing the job, with B-roll of real use.
4. Last 10 to 15 s: launch details. Where to get it, who it is for, one call to action.

## Make it yours

The hero of this video is the problem, and the product arrives as the answer. Your story only sets up the stakes. Name the problem in words your buyers use. A privacy tool for small companies opens on how every app tracks employees by default. An AI tutor for dyslexic students opens on how school software was never built for the way they read. A security scanner for indie developers opens on how one leaked key can end a side project. Shoot it for launch day, then reuse the reveal as an ad.

## Variations

- Teaser version: stop after the problem and the promise, reveal the product a week later.
- Customer opener: the first line comes from a user who lived the problem, then the founder takes over.
- Short cut: 30 seconds, problem and product only, for paid distribution.

## Why it works

A shared frustration said out loud makes viewers nod before the product appears, and the reveal answers the tension the first line created. A mission gives people a reason to share beyond the product itself (Berger 2013, emotion and social currency).

## Watch out

- An overstated problem sounds like an ad. Use a problem your audience already names.
- Every claim about the product must be true on launch day.
- If the account belongs to the company, the video is marketing: follow platform rules for branded content.
