# Insider: I designed it, here is what users did

## Shape

The person who built a feature or a product says who they are and what they intended. Then they show, with real footage or screen recordings, what users actually did with it, things the designer never saw coming. The video ends with what the team learned or changed because of it.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the credential and the twist in one line ("I designed this, and players beat it in a way I never planned").
2. 2 to 30 s: the original intention. What the feature was supposed to do, shown briefly.
3. 30 to 120 s: the surprise. Footage of what users did, one example after another, with your reaction.
4. Last seconds: what you learned, what you changed, or the next surprise you are watching for.

## Make it yours

Only the person who built it can tell this story, which makes it hard to copy. You say: "we built this feature for X, users used it for Y". An AI writing assistant built for emails turns out to be used for wedding speeches. A spreadsheet automation tool built for finance teams turns out to run a school's lunch orders. A voice transcription app for journalists turns out to be used by people learning a language. Show the usage with screen recordings or customer clips you have the right to use.

## Variations

- The workaround hall of fame: three user hacks ranked from clever to absurd.
- The feature that flopped: what you built with care that nobody used, and why.
- Before you ask: the design decision users complain about, explained from the inside.

## Why it works

The insider view is information viewers cannot get elsewhere, and the gap between plan and reality is a natural surprise. Users who see their own trick on screen share it, because it makes them look clever (Berger 2013, social currency).

## Watch out

- Anonymize customer data in every screen recording. Blur names, emails and amounts.
- Ask customers before showing their account or their workaround.
- Keep the tone curious. Mocking how users "misuse" the product pushes them away.
