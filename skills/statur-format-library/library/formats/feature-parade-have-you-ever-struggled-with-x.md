# Feature parade: have you ever struggled with X?

## Shape

A fast series of small everyday problems, each introduced with "Have you ever struggled with...?". After each question, one feature solves the problem on screen in a few seconds. The rhythm is question, solution, next question, until the last and most surprising feature.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the first micro-problem shown and asked as a question ("Have you ever struggled reaching the top shelf?").
2. 2 to 8 s: the feature solves it, with a short "well, not anymore" beat.
3. 8 to 50 s: three to six more problem and feature pairs, each faster than the last.
4. Last 5 s: the best feature, then the product shown whole.

## Make it yours

For software, each micro-problem gets one feature and one screen recording. A project management tool asks "Have you ever lost a file in a long chat thread?" and shows the search. An AI inbox assistant asks "Have you ever replied to the same question ten times?" and shows the saved reply. An invoicing app asks "Have you ever chased a late payment?" and shows the automatic reminder. Pick problems your users name in their own words, from support tickets or reviews.

## Variations

- Prejudice opener: start with a common belief about your category, then let the features answer it.
- Single room: all the features live in one place (one dashboard, one kitchen).
- Voice-over parade: the same structure with a narrator and no on-screen host.

## Why it works

Each question is a tiny gap that closes in seconds, and the next one opens right away, which keeps the viewer moving (Loewenstein 1994). Viewers recognize their own small frustrations and see a practical fix (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- One problem per feature, one feature per shot. Crowded shots lose the viewer.
- Show features that exist today. Roadmap items do not belong in this format.
- Disclose that the account belongs to the company or that the post is paid if a creator makes it.

## For your AI

```
Plan a "feature parade: have you ever struggled with X?" video for [company], which makes [product] for [audience].
1. List 8 micro-problems [audience] meets every week, in their own words, each solved by one existing feature of [product].
2. Pick the best 5, order them from familiar to most surprising, and write for each: the question (under 10 words), the screen recording or shot, and the solution beat (under 3 seconds).
3. Write the closing shot and the caption.
Rules: existing features only, one problem per feature, one feature per shot, disclose that the account belongs to [company].
```
