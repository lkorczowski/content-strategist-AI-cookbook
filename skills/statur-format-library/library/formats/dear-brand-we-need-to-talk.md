# Dear brand, we need to talk

## Shape

The creator speaks straight to a brand, tagging it in the opening line, about a product so extreme it deserves a talk. They walk through what makes it excessive, with b-roll of the product. They mix real admiration with jokes about the kind of person who buys it.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the address. "@Brand, can we talk about what you built?" with the product in frame.
2. 2 to 40 s: the extreme details, one by one, each with a shot and a short reaction.
3. 40 to 90 s: the jokes about who buys it, then the honest admiration for the craft.
4. Last 10 s: a final question to the brand or to viewers, which invites comments.

## Make it yours

Founder version: you address the category leader of your market, respectfully. You pick a product or a feature they shipped that is impressive or excessive, and you talk to them as a peer. Your expertise shows in what you notice. Examples: an AI video startup founder talking to the leading editing software about a new feature, a developer tools founder addressing a cloud giant about its very long pricing page, an AI hardware founder talking to a phone maker about its latest camera.

## Variations

- Customer to you: you film one of your users addressing your own company, with humour about your excesses.
- Letter series: one brand per episode, same opening line.
- Stitch version: you react to the brand's own launch video.

## Why it works

Talking to a brand by name feels like a public conversation, and people comment to join it. The mix of praise and teasing gives viewers something to share with friends who use that brand (Berger 2013, social currency).

## Watch out

- Stay respectful. Mockery of a competitor reads as bitterness and can raise legal issues on comparative claims.
- Use only footage you own or the brand's material within platform rules, and never imply a partnership that does not exist.
- Check every fact you state about the product.

Write a "Dear brand, we need to talk" video for me, founder of [company] in [market], addressed to [category leader].
1. Pick the product or feature of [category leader] that is the most impressive or excessive, and list 4 to 5 striking facts about it that I must verify.
2. Write the opening line (tag + question, under 12 words), then the details with b-roll notes, the jokes about who buys it, the honest admiration and a closing question.
3. Length 60 to 110 seconds.
Rules: respectful tone toward [category leader], no claim I cannot verify, no fake partnership, no footage I do not have the right to use.
