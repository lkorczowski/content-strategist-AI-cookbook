# Everything I do for X in 60 seconds

## Shape

The speaker announces the promise ("I'm going to walk you through everything I do for X in 60 seconds") and then runs a fast montage of every step, tool or ritual. Each item gets one shot and one line of voice. The video ends when the list is complete, close to the minute.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the promise, said to camera, with the topic on screen.
2. 2 to 40 s: the montage. One item every 3 to 5 seconds, each with a shot of the thing in use and one line on why.
3. 40 to 55 s: the one item people do not expect, given slightly more time.
4. Last seconds: a quick return to the face, the last item, and an offer to break down any step in a reply video.

## Make it yours

Founder version: every tool and ritual you use to run the company, in order of a week or a day. A solo founder of an AI writing assistant shows the stack that runs sales, support and shipping. A founder of a recruiting SaaS shows every step of their hiring process. A founder of a finance app for small businesses shows everything they do to close the month. Your product appears as one item among many, in real use, which reads as more credible than a pitch.

## Variations

- Team edition: one teammate per item, each says their line.
- Customer edition: a customer walks through everything they do with your category.
- Ten-second cut: the same list as pure b-roll with on-screen text, for a second post.

## Why it works

The promise sets a clear contract (the full list in one minute) and the viewer stays to collect every item, which is practical value they can copy (Berger 2013). The fast cuts reward rewatching to catch an item they missed.

## Watch out

- Every tool you name must be one you really use. Viewers ask follow-up questions.
- If a brand paid or gave you a tool, disclose it.
- Hiding passwords, client data and dashboards with real numbers in screen shots is your job before export.

Write an "Everything I do for X in 60 seconds" video for [founder name], founder of [company], which makes [product] for [audience].
1. Topic: everything I do for [running sales, shipping, hiring, my health as a founder].
2. List 10 to 14 items I really use, in a logical order. For each: one line of voice (under 12 words) and one shot (screen, object, place).
3. Pick the most surprising item and give it 5 extra seconds.
4. Write the opening promise word for word and the closing line inviting viewers to ask about one step.
Rules: only tools and habits I really use, disclose any sponsored tool, hide private data on screens, 45 to 60 seconds total.
