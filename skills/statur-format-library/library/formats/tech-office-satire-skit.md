# Tech office satire skit

## Shape

A short scripted scene exaggerates a situation everyone in tech recognizes: the ex big tech lead who brings it up constantly, the meeting taken from bed, the investor who mocks a pitch deck. One or two people play the roles in a real office or home setup. The scene builds to one punchline and stops.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the character's first line sets the cliché ("I'm the ex-FAANG tech lead").
2. 2 to 30 s: the situation escalates, each line a bit more absurd.
3. 30 to 90 s: the other character reacts or flips the situation.
4. Last seconds: the punchline, then cut before anyone explains it.

## Make it yours

Write the scenes your buyers live every week and exaggerate them. A developer tools startup plays the engineer who "just needs five minutes" before every standup. An AI recruiting tool plays the candidate who lists every buzzword in one breath. A remote work platform plays the colleague whose camera is always off for a different reason. The product does not need to appear: the account becomes the place your audience goes to laugh at their own job, and that is the brand.

## Variations

- One person, all roles: you play every character with a costume change.
- Series: the same cliché character returns each week.
- Reverse: the cliché character turns out to be right at the end.

## Why it works

Viewers recognize their colleagues and tag them, so the video spreads inside teams that are your buyers. Shared jokes about a trade signal membership in that group, which is social currency (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- Punch at situations and habits. Jokes about identity, origin or appearance alienate the very people you want to reach.
- Use only colleagues who agree to appear, and avoid copying real people's names or faces.
- On a brand account, keep the account name visible so viewers know who made it.

## For your AI

```
Write a "tech office satire skit" for [company], whose audience is [audience] (role, team, industry).
1. List 10 situations [audience] lives every week that are funny when exaggerated.
2. For the best one, write the script: characters, the first line that sets the cliché, 4 to 6 escalating lines, the flip, and the punchline. Add shot notes (location, who plays whom, cuts).
3. Suggest 3 follow-up episodes with the same character.
Rules: joke about situations and habits only, no identity jokes, no real names or faces, cast only consenting people.
```
