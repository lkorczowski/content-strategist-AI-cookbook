# Workplace trivia gotcha

## Shape

One employee bets a colleague on a surprising fact about something in their own workplace. The colleague doubts it out loud. The proof is shown on the spot, in one shot, and the colleague reacts.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the challenge, as a question ("Did you know these two have the exact same...?").
2. 2 to 8 s: the colleague's doubt, a quick back and forth.
3. 8 to 25 s: the proof, shown on camera with no cut if possible.
4. Last seconds: the reaction, then cut.

## Make it yours

Every product has odd facts hidden inside it. Find one your team argues about. An AI photo editing app bets that two photos that look identical are one original and one generated, and proves which is which. A password manager bets a colleague that a password that looks strong cracks in seconds, and shows the check. A spreadsheet automation tool bets that one formula replaces a full afternoon of copy-paste, and runs it. The proof is a product moment that lasts a few seconds.

## Variations

- Viewer guess: pause before the proof with "what do you think?" on screen.
- Losing streak: the same colleague loses a new bet every episode.
- Reverse: the doubter is right, and the bettor pays.

## Why it works

The bet creates a question the viewer wants to settle before the colleague does (Loewenstein 1994). A surprising fact is easy to repeat to friends, which makes the video worth sharing (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- The fact must be true and verifiable. One staged proof ruins trust in the whole account.
- Keep the proof in one continuous shot so viewers believe it.
- Colleagues on camera agree to appear.
