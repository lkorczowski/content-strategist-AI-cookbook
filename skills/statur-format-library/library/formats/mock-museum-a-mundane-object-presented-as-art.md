# Mock museum: a mundane object presented as art

## Shape

The camera moves slowly around a mundane object of the workplace, framed like a piece in a gallery. A solemn voice, in the tone of an art critic, gives it a title, a year, an artist and a deep interpretation. The more ordinary the object, the funnier the reading.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the object in a gallery-style shot, with a caption card (title, artist, year).
2. 2 to 15 s: the critic's voice introduces the work with full seriousness.
3. 15 to 35 s: the interpretation, each line more grandiose, linked to life at work.
4. Last seconds: a final verdict on the work, and a wide shot that shows where it really is.

## Make it yours

Every office has objects that tell its story. Present them as art. A dev tools startup exhibits the whiteboard covered in a three-month-old architecture diagram. An AI company exhibits a tangle of cables behind the GPU server rack. A remote team exhibits the founder's desk on the day before a launch. The interpretation can hint at the problem your product solves, in the critic's grand words.

## Variations

- Screen museum: the "artwork" is a cluttered desktop, a broken spreadsheet or a legacy codebase, shown full screen.
- Guided tour: three objects, one room, one critic.
- Customer submissions: followers send their own office objects for review.

## Why it works

The solemn voice clashes with the plain object, and the surprise of that contrast makes people laugh and replay. Viewers recognize the same object in their own office and share it with colleagues.

## Watch out

- Keep confidential information out of frame: whiteboards, screens and papers often hold it.
- Mock objects and situations. Keep colleagues' personal belongings out unless they agree.
- Use a voice and music you have rights to.
