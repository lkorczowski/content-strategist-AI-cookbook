# Reaction with a running counter

## Shape

A source video plays on one half of the screen and the reactor on the other. A big counter on screen tracks one absurd thing in the video (items ordered, steps taken, tools opened), and it ticks up every time it happens. The reactor's disbelief grows with the number.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the counter appears at zero with a label ("tools used: 0") and the source clip starts.
2. 2 to 60 s: the count climbs. Each tick gets a sound and a short reaction from you.
3. 60 to 150 s: the moment it should stop and does not. Say it out loud, the counter keeps going.
4. Last seconds: the final number held on screen, one punchline, and a question for the comments ("guess the final count before the end").

## Make it yours

Pick a public workflow video in your market and count the friction your product removes. A project management tool counts how many apps a manager switches between in one morning routine video. An AI meeting notes tool counts how many times someone says "can you repeat that" in a call clip. A spend management tool counts the receipts someone photographs in a "month-end with me" video. The counter is the argument, so your product only needs one line at the end.

## Variations

- Your own footage: record the old way yourself and count it, then show the same task with your product in one tick.
- Two counters: compare two people doing the same task side by side.
- Live guess: pin a comment asking for the final number before posting the answer video.

## Why it works

An open count is an unfinished task: the viewer stays to see where it stops (Zeigarnik 1927). Each tick is a small reward, and the growing absurdity turns into the joke people share.

## Watch out

- Use the platform's duet or stitch tools, or clips you have the right to use, and credit the original creator.
- Make fun of the situation, keep the person in the source video out of the target.
- The counter must be accurate. Viewers will recount and call out errors in the comments.
