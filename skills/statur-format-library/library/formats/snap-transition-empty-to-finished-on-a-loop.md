# Snap transition: empty to finished, on a loop

## Shape

Someone snaps their fingers or taps a surface, and the scene jumps to empty. Short shots then show it being set up piece by piece, from a fixed camera. In the last shot the person walks back into the same position as the first frame, so the video loops without a visible cut.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the finished scene and the snap.
2. 2 to 4 s: the cut to the empty scene.
3. 4 to 15 s: the set-up shots, each adding one element, at a steady rhythm.
4. Last seconds: the person returns to the opening position, matching the first frame for the loop.

## Make it yours

You need a scene that can go from empty to complete, and a fixed camera. A desk setup brand goes from a bare desk to the full workstation. An AI website builder goes from a blank browser tab to the finished site, filmed on a screen with the snap in front of it. A hardware startup goes from an empty bench to the assembled device. A seasonal version adds one festive object as the last element. This shape was shot in a real program.

## Variations

- Seasonal: the last element is a candle, a decoration or a gift, and the loop ends on it.
- Reverse: start empty, snap, and everything appears at once.
- Product names: each new element gets its name on screen as it appears.

## Why it works

The snap creates a small surprise in the first seconds, and the loop makes many viewers watch twice before they notice the restart. Watching a space fill up step by step is satisfying, and the final frame shows the product complete.

## Watch out

- The first and last frames must match: same position, same light, same framing.
- Lock the camera on a tripod; any shift breaks the effect.
- Use a sound from the platform library or one you have the rights to.
