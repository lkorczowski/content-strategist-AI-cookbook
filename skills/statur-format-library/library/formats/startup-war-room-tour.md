# Startup war-room tour

## Shape

At a key moment (2 a.m. before a launch, the last night of a sprint), the camera walks through the team at work. Each person gets one line: who they are and what they are doing right now. The tour ends on the person running the show or on the thing about to ship.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the time and the stakes ("it's 2 in the morning, launch is at 9") as the camera enters the room.
2. 2 to 40 s: the walk. One stop per person, one line each, fast cuts.
3. 40 to 55 s: the person in charge, or the screen with the countdown.
4. Last seconds: back to the room, or a cut to the launch moment.

## Make it yours

You film your own team on a real crunch night: a product launch, a big release, the end of a fundraise, a trade show set-up. Each person says their role in a few words, so viewers meet the team behind the product. Examples: an AI agent startup the night before a public launch, a fintech team on the night of a big migration, a design tool team preparing a live demo for an event.

## Variations

- Day in the life of one role: the same walk following one job for a day (see the accountant example).
- Calm version: a normal Tuesday, same format, to show the culture.
- Remote team: a split of video calls, one line per person.

## Why it works

The late hour and the stakes create a small story with a deadline, and viewers stay to see the ending. People who work in startups recognize the night and share it with their own team.

## Watch out

- Ask every person before filming, and let them skip the video.
- Keep screens with customer data or unreleased features out of frame.
- Avoid glamorizing exhaustion. Show the stakes and the team, and keep the tone light.
