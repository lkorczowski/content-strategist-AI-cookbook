# Custom order for a famous client

## Shape

The maker opens with "We received a letter from" and names a famous client. They tell the request, then design and make the custom piece on camera. The video ends on the finished result.

## Beats

1. 0 to 2 s: the famous name and the letter or message, on screen ("We received a letter from...").
2. 2 to 15 s: the request in the maker's words: what was asked, why it was hard.
3. 15 to 45 s: the design and the making, in quick steps, with the reference that inspired it.
4. Last 5 to 15 s: the finished piece, shown close and whole.

## Make it yours

This works when a well-known company, person or institution actually orders from you. A design tool startup tells how a famous museum asked for a custom template, then builds it on screen. An AI voice startup tells how a known podcast asked for a special voice feature, then shows the build. A packaging startup tells how a famous restaurant ordered a custom box. The famous name is the hook; the making shows your craft. Ask the client before you post, and agree on what can be shown.

## Variations

- Iconic place: a request from a place everyone knows (a landmark, a historic venue).
- Customer reaction: end with the client receiving the piece, if they agree to be filmed.
- Archive story: a famous order from the company's past, told with old documents.

## Why it works

A famous name gives the viewer a reason to stop, and the open question "what did they make?" carries them to the result (Loewenstein 1994). Telling friends that a famous client chose a small maker is a story worth sharing (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- Only real orders, with the client's written permission to name them and show the piece.
- Avoid implying an endorsement the client did not give. Describe the order only.
- Keep private details of the request (addresses, signatures, people's names) off screen.
