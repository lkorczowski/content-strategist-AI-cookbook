# Imagine-this voice-over

## Shape

A calm voice asks the viewer to imagine a situation ("Imagine it's the year 2256"), and the footage shows something ordinary from a new angle. As the voice describes the scene, everyday things start to feel extraordinary. The video ends on one sentence that turns the feeling back on the viewer's own life.

## Beats

1. 0 to 3 s: "Imagine..." and the situation, over a striking first image.
2. 3 to 60 s: the voice describes ordinary things as an outsider would, one at a time, each with matching b-roll.
3. 60 to 180 s: the scenes grow, from small details to the big picture, and the music rises.
4. Last seconds: a final line that brings the viewer back to the present.

## Make it yours

This is a vision format for a mission-driven company. A climate tech startup imagines explaining today's energy grid to someone in the future. A health AI imagines a doctor from the past seeing a modern diagnosis. An education platform imagines explaining a classroom to someone who never saw one. The voice can be yours, but no face is needed. Your company is never pitched: the final line states the belief your company exists for.

## Variations

- Past edition: imagine explaining today to someone from 1900.
- Customer edition: imagine your customer's day before your category existed.
- Short cut: a 30-second version with only the strongest three images.

## Why it works

"Imagine" invites the viewer to step into the scene, and seeing the ordinary through an outsider's eyes produces awe. That emotion is a strong driver of sharing (Berger 2013).

## Watch out

- Footage and music need licenses. Stock or your own footage only, and a cleared sound.
- Keep the vision grounded: grand claims about what your product will change read as hype.
- AI-generated footage should be labeled where the platform asks for it.

```
Write an "Imagine-this voice-over" video for [company], whose mission is [mission] for [audience].
1. Propose 5 "Imagine..." openings that show something ordinary in [field] through an outsider's eyes (future, past, another planet, another job).
2. For the best one, write the voice-over (60 to 220 seconds): the opening, 8 to 12 ordinary things described as extraordinary, rising from details to the big picture.
3. Next to each sentence, describe the b-roll shot.
4. Write the final line that brings the viewer back to the present and states the belief behind [company], without naming [product].
Rules: licensed footage and music, label AI-generated footage, no claims about what [product] will change, no face needed.
```
