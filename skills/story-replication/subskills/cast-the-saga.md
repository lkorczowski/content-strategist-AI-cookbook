# Cast the saga

## What the move does

In a story-led explainer, a real saga runs under the lesson. Each lesson is followed by a saga episode that proves it. The saga is what viewers remember, and the payoff of the video is the payoff of the saga. Casting the saga is the most important research task of a replication.

## How to find it in your source analysis

- `work/stats.json`: `thread_share.S` gives the share of runtime the story thread carries. Keep that share.
- `07-seed.yaml`: `saga_requirements` lists the slots the structure needs. Use them as your grid; the table below is a typical set.
- `05-graph.json`: the `proves` edges show which story beat proves which lesson; the `chronology` shows which episodes are told out of order.

## Requirements (a typical slot grid)

| Slot | What to find in the new domain | Example (invented) |
|---|---|---|
| HERO | One person or company that encountered SUBJECT repeatedly, preferably reckless or underestimated | A solo founder known for shipping too fast |
| DRAWS | 5 or more dated encounters with different outcomes | Five launches over three years |
| First draw | An early encounter that seems to pay off | The first launch brings a flood of signups |
| Promise | A public commitment to restraint, or a rule HERO set for themselves, later broken | A public promise to always launch with a beta group first |
| Stranger | Someone else who benefited from HERO's move, by luck | A freelancer who reused the launch template and got hired by a large company |
| Callback | That beneficiary seen again, years later, bigger | The same freelancer returns years later as an investor |
| Sandbox | A low-stakes or experimental episode with many outcomes at once | A hackathon weekend with five experiments at once |
| Worst draw | The worst outcome at the happiest moment | The worst launch lands on the company's anniversary |
| Live payoff | A public, measurable triumph in front of a crowd, that resolves the worst draw | A live demo on a conference stage that wins back the lost customers |

The examples are fictional and only show the shape. A real replication uses real, dated events.

## Process

1. List 3 to 5 candidate sagas. For each, build a dated timeline (date, event, source URL, confidence).
2. Score each candidate on the grid: 2 = real and documented, 1 = partly (needs a softer framing), 0 = missing.
3. Pick the highest total. With nine slots, 14 out of 18 is enough; missing slots are handled as below.
4. Write the casting table in the research dossier: slot, real event, date, source, how it maps.

## When a slot is missing

- Keep the function, change the content. A missing "stranger" can be a competitor who copied HERO and got lucky. A missing "promise" can be a public statement HERO later contradicted.
- If no real event fills a slot, say so in the script ("there is no record of..."), or use a clearly labeled hypothetical in the audience thread (A). The story thread (S) holds only documented events.
- A composite hero (several companies) breaks the identification that makes the payoff work. Use it only for the deck spectrum, never for the saga.

## Success criteria

- At least 5 dated encounters, each with a source.
- The worst draw and the payoff are causally linked (the payoff resolves the worst draw).
- The payoff is public, measurable and ideally witnessed by a crowd or an audience.
- Each slot has a confidence level; nothing low-confidence carries a turning point.
