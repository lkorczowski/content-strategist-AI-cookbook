# Find the ritual opening

## What the move does

The video opens on a ritual of a community: a phrase or gesture that insiders recognize at once. It says who owns the ritual and since when. It reports that the owner was asked for the most memorable instance, gives a known answer first, then a second answer as a date and a superlative with the content withheld. Much later, that withheld moment turns out to be the payoff of the saga. The ritual is also the last line of the video: a bookend.

It works because it opens a master loop in the first minute (GAP, ZEIG), rewards insiders (INGRP), and makes the ending feel planned (PEAKEND).

## How to find it in your source analysis

- In `07-seed.yaml`, the first block has beat COLD-OPEN and moves tagged RITUAL, OPEN, TEASE.
- In `03-loops.md`, a MASTER loop opens in the first block and closes in the CLIMAX or CODA block.
- In `02-map.md`, the same RITUAL tag appears on one of the first units and on one of the last.

If the source has no ritual, the move may be a plain cold open from the payoff event. Read the seed's purpose line for the first block.

## How to transpose it: find four pieces

1. RITUAL: a phrase, gesture or format that insiders of TRIBE recognize instantly and outsiders can understand in one sentence. Good: a catchphrase, a recurring announcement format, a traditional question, a ceremony. It must be possible to say it as the very last line of the video.
2. MASTER: the person most associated with the ritual, with real authority in TRIBE.
3. MEMORABLE MOMENT: a public statement (interview, post, talk) where MASTER or another authority names a most memorable, most important or biggest instance. If no such statement exists, a public record does the job (the most viewed, the largest round, the biggest crowd), stated as a fact with its source.
4. PROOF OF SCALE: one number a viewer can see (views, crowd size, money, users).

## How to search

- Queries: "<tribe> tradition", "<tribe> catchphrase", "<master> most memorable moment", "<master> favorite moment interview", "<event> most watched", "<event> record crowd". Add the year range of the saga.
- Prefer primary sources: the interview itself (video or transcript), the official post, the event page. Press coverage is acceptable with the outlet and date.
- Keep any quote under 15 words. Paraphrase the rest.

## Invented example

A video about a fictional design community whose members end every critique with the same short question. The opening says who started the ritual and when. It reports that the founder of the community was asked which critique they remember most: first the one everyone knows, then a second one named only by its date. That second critique is the payoff of the saga, forty minutes later, and the question closes the video.

## Success criteria (all must hold)

- The memorable moment is an event of HERO's saga, ideally its final payoff, so the loop can close at the end of the video.
- Every piece has a date and a URL in the research dossier.
- The ritual can close the video as its last line, and its meaning is clear by then.
- The first answer (the "known" one) is something the audience already knows; the second answer is withheld and dated.

## Failure modes

- A ritual unrelated to the payoff: the bookend feels glued on. Pick the ritual that is spoken at the payoff moment itself.
- An unverifiable superlative: write "one of the most watched" or give the number. A claim like "the biggest ever" needs a source that says it.
- A quote that you remember but cannot find: drop it, report the fact without quotation marks, cite where it appears.
- If nothing qualifies, build the cold open from the payoff event itself (date, place, crowd, one detail) and withhold the outcome. Keep the bookend by ending on the same image.
