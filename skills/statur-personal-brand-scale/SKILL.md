# Personal brand scale (the Korczowski scale)

Part of the guide "The 5 levels of personal branding: the Korczowski scale" (https://statur.co/personal-brand-levels/). For the full text, the sources and the examples, read the markdown version: https://statur.co/personal-brand-levels.md.

The scale measures how far a person's ideas travel when they are not in the room. Followers, views and invitations are symptoms used as evidence. The types:

| Type | Name | Ideas reach | Rough follower marker | Team |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | The Invisible Expert | their head and meetings | a few hundred at most | nobody |
| 1 | The Grinder | their followers, sometimes | under 10,000 | alone |
| 2 | The Niche Authority | their niche | 10,000 to 100,000 | 1 or 2 people |
| 3 | The Industry Face | their industry and beyond | 100,000 to several million | full-time team |
| 4 | The Magnate | society, culture, politics | many millions | the best team in the field |

## Single responsibility

This skill diagnoses the type and plans the move to the next one. It does not write posts (use statur-creative-led-growth or the writing skills), does not review formats on data (statur-format-review), does not collect metrics (statur-social-analytics) and does not build the founder's brand DNA (statur-brand-dna-audit).

## 0. Read the context first

Read the company context file if it exists (kb/01_BRAND.md from the blueprint, or .agents/product-marketing.md). Use the platforms, audience and proofs already written there. Ask only for what is missing.

## 1. Collect evidence

Ask for, or read from files: the platforms and follower counts, posting frequency over the last 90 days, the median views of the last 20 posts at day 7 and the best post, who works on content and how many hours a week, and the inbound of the last 6 months (DMs, deals, investor intros, hires, podcast or press invitations) with one example each. No evidence, no tick.

## 2. Score the 16 lines

Tick a line only with evidence the person could show.

- Type 1: publishes at least three times a week on one platform; has held it three months or more; keeps a written list of repeated formats or topics; reads the numbers at least weekly.
- Type 2: a stranger contacted them to buy, partner, invest or hire because of content; one post went past ten times their usual reach; one format or idea is recognized as theirs; at least one other person works on their content every week.
- Type 3: media or events outside their niche invite them; people in their industry repeat their ideas, sometimes uncredited; a full-time team scripts, edits and distributes while they bring ideas and presence; the company wins deals or hires because of their name without them.
- Type 4: mainstream media cover them outside their industry; a Wikipedia page that meets its notability rules; politicians or public institutions discuss their ideas; their audience followed them into another industry.

Score: the person reaches a type when they tick at least three of its four lines and they reached the type below. The lines ticked in the next type add a quarter each. Example: Type 1 reached, two lines of Type 2 ticked, score 1.5.

## 3. Name the trap

- Type 0: "later, when things calm down". The fix is a calendar slot, before any topic.
- Type 1: quitting inside the 300-view jail. The fix is packaging and formats, measured at day 7 against their own median.
- Type 2: stopping there. Attention rented from an algorithm fades within a few years if the ideas are not owned by the niche and a team does not carry the production.
- Type 3: the name fades behind the ideas. The fix is a body of work (book, public project) that holds the ideas.
- Type 4: everything is studied, copied and judged. The job becomes keeping the ideas true.

## 4. Write the 90-day plan to the next type

Output, in this order:
1. Score with the ticked lines and the evidence for each.
2. The trap, in one sentence about this person.
3. The three moves for the next 90 days, taken from the guide's "how to reach" steps for their type, each with an owner, a weekly time budget and a check date.
4. The one thing to do today.
5. What to stop doing.
6. Which skill to run next: statur-creative-led-growth (Type 0 to 1), statur-format-review and statur-video-post-mortem (Type 1 to 2), statur-meta-ads-scaling and statur-organic-to-paid for ads from organic winners (Type 2 to 3).

For Type 2, also draft the job description of a head of content: outcomes, weekly rituals, what the founder keeps (ideas, presence, decisions).

## Pitfalls

- Scoring on follower counts alone. An account with 200,000 followers that brings nothing behaves like a Type 1.
- Ticking lines on hope. A line needs an example with a date.
- Planning Type 4. Nobody can; plan the next type only.
- Comparing to someone two types above. Compare to the next type.

## Rules

- Drafts only: never post, send or publish for the user.
- No passwords: the user signs in to any platform themselves.
- No promised views, followers, deals or dates.
- No invented numbers: use only the user's evidence and the numbers written in the guide, with their sources.
- Respect platform terms: no bought followers, engagement pods or fake accounts in any plan.
