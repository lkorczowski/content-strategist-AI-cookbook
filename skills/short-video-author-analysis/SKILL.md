# Short video author analysis · from a profile to an author Fiche

Part of the Video Script Structure playbook (https://statur.co/video-script-structure/, page coming soon). Read https://statur.co/video-script-structure.md for the full method once it is published and you can open links.

If the workspace has a company context file (kb/01_BRAND.md from the blueprint, or .agents/product-marketing.md), read it first and ask only for what is missing. It supplies the creator's goals and the questions the analysis must answer.

This skill is the short-form, many-videos sibling of `video-story-analysis` (one long video). Its output feeds `short-story-replication`.

Why many videos: one short video is too small to separate the author's grammar from the topic of the day. Twenty videos give the recurring beats, the hook and ending habits, the phrases that come back, and the range of each score.

## How to work with the user

Ask for:
1. The creator profile, or the user's own account when the user is the creator.
2. The goal: learn a style, audit one's own habits, or prepare a replication.
3. The platform, and what the user can provide: captions copied from the platform, the creator's own export, or videos the user has the right to download.

## What you produce (case folder `case-NN-<slug>/`)

1. `source/`: `videos.tsv` (the profile inventory), `selection.md` (the rule and every skip), `transcripts/NN-<id>.txt` (one per video, cue times, header with stats), `visual-layer.md`, `acquisition-log.md`. Transcripts of other people's videos stay private.
2. `videos/NN-<id>/`: `meta.json`, `work/units-asr.tsv`, `01-blocks.md` (short beats), `02-map.md` (every unit on the 9 tiers), `03-loops.md`, `card.json` (hook, ending, turn time, signature moves, 8 scores), `04-stats.md` and `work/stats.json`.
3. `10-author-grammar.md` and `work/author.json` (generated by the aggregator).
4. `11-templates.md`: the 2 to 4 templates you name from the core skeletons, with when the author uses each.
5. `06-fiche.json` (narrative fields of the author Fiche) and `fiche.html` (built by `scripts/build_fiche_author.py`).
6. `07-seed-short.yaml`: the input of `short-story-replication`.

Vocabulary: `references/taxonomy.md` (all tiers) plus `references/taxonomy-short.md` (short beats HOOK, FRAME, SETUP, STORY, TURN, BUILD, PAYOFF, BUTTON, ASIDE, CTA; hook codes H-*; ending codes E-*). Scoring: `references/scoring-short.md`.

Commands run from the case folder. `scripts/` means this skill's scripts folder.

## Process

### Step 0 · Selection rule (write it before you look at the videos)
Default: the 10 most-viewed videos plus the 10 most recent above a view floor, 20 in total. Set the floor from the account size (for example the creator's median view count, or 100k views for a large account) and write it in `selection.md`. Skip duets or stitches where someone else's clip dominates, re-uploads, pure promos and videos without speech; replace each by the next candidate and log why. Why both groups: the most-viewed show what the algorithm rewarded, often years ago; the recent ones show the current format. Comparing them is the only way to see the author's drift.

### Step 1 · Acquire (the user collects, within the site's terms)
The user collects the inventory and the transcripts within each site's terms of use. Work from what the user provides or owns. Details, file formats and tools: `references/acquisition.md`.

Inventory (`source/videos.tsv`, one row per video: id, url, date, title or caption, view count as shown):
- Build it from what any viewer sees on the profile: title, date and the view count the page shows.
- When the creator is the user, use the creator's own analytics export instead. It is more complete and more exact.

Transcripts (`source/transcripts/NN-<id>.txt`), from one of these sources only:
- Captions or transcripts that the platform shows to any viewer, copied by the user.
- Caption files that the user or the creator owns, for example a creator analyzing their own account through the platform's export.
- A local speech-to-text run on videos the user has the right to download, such as their own.
- Transcripts the user pastes.

Prefer the creator's own captions over automatic ones: punctuation then comes from the author, and the units measure the author. Write the source of each transcript in its header (`track:`) and in `acquisition-log.md`.

Transcript file format (read by `scripts/short_units.py`):
```
# NN · <short title>
url: <video url>
date: <yyyy-mm-dd>
duration_s: <seconds>
views: <n> | likes: <n> | comments: <n> | shares: <n> | saves: <n>
track: <creator captions, platform transcript, export or local speech-to-text>
[00:00.0] First caption line.
[00:02.4] Next caption line.
```

Visual layer (`source/visual-layer.md`): three videos at three moments each (start, middle, end). Note what is on screen (face, cut-out over an image, screen recording, b-roll), the framing and the caption style (burned-in lines, word-by-word highlight).

Transcripts of other people's videos stay private: use them for analysis, commentary and education. Never republish them. Quote at most a short phrase, with credit to the creator.

### Step 2 · Units
`python3 -I scripts/short_units.py source/transcripts/NN-<id>.txt videos/NN-<id>` for each video. It writes the units (one sentence, or one caption cue when the captions have no punctuation), `meta.json` and a `02-map.md` skeleton. Every annotator then starts from the same unit ids, which is what makes the aggregation valid.

### Step 3 · Gold video (lead analyst)
Tag one video completely yourself (blocks, map, loops, card), run `python3 -I scripts/analyze.py videos/<folder> <duration_s>`, and write the annotation brief (`ANNOTATION.md`, template in `references/annotation-brief.md`) with the short-form scoring anchors and the scores of your gold video. Why: four annotators without a gold example produce four dialects of the taxonomy.

### Step 4 · Parallel tagging
Split the other videos into 3 to 5 sets of similar size, counted in units rather than videos, one agent per set, all with the same brief. Each agent fills the skeleton, writes blocks, loops and the card, and runs `analyze.py` on each video. Without parallel agents, tag the sets one after the other with the same brief.

### Step 5 · Aggregate
`python3 -I scripts/aggregate_author.py <case_dir>` writes `10-author-grammar.md` and `work/author.json`: per-video table, medians for all, top 10 and recent 10, beat skeletons and core skeletons (first occurrence of each beat, in order), length classes, median share of each beat, hook and ending counts, figures and mechanisms by number of videos, recurring 3-to-5-word phrases, score means and ranges.

### Step 6 · Name the templates, write the seed
Read the core skeletons by length class and name 2 to 4 templates in `11-templates.md` (name, skeleton, share of videos, median beat shares, when the author uses it, one example video). Give each template a plain name that says its shape, for example "the assumption to knock down" or "the mystery with a solution".

Then write `07-seed-short.yaml`: per template, the beats with their shares and moves, plus the author's rules. Abstract the roles (SUBJECT, MYSTERY, CULPRIT, VIEWER'S ASSUMPTION) so the template can carry any subject. Every number comes from your own `10-author-grammar.md`. Shape:
```yaml
author: <creator>
sample: <n videos, selection rule, date range>
templates:
  - id: T1
    name: <plain name of the shape>
    skeleton: HOOK FRAME SETUP TURN BUILD PAYOFF BUTTON
    share_of_videos: <n of N>
    median_runtime_s: <from your sample>
    turn_share: <median of this template>
    beats:
      - {beat: HOOK, share: <median>, move: "<what the beat does, with roles>"}
      - {beat: TURN, share: <median>, move: "<the fact that breaks the VIEWER'S ASSUMPTION>"}
rules:
  hooks: {<H-code>: <count>}
  endings: {<E-code>: <count>}
  signature_moves: ["<move> (<n> videos)"]
  per_minute: {loops: <median>, jokes: <median>, facts: <median>}
  words_per_minute: <median>
  you_share: <median>
```

### Step 7 · Author Fiche
Fill `06-fiche.json` (title, eyebrow, creator, handle, period, class, background, alignment, formula, abilities with evidence lines, templates with beat shares, feats, equipment, flaws, custom axes, notes) and run `python3 -I scripts/build_fiche_author.py <case_dir> fiche.html`. Hand the file to the user as a draft. Sharing it is the user's decision. A Fiche about another creator is commentary: it credits the creator and quotes at most short phrases.

### Step 8 · Verify
A blind agent re-tags 2 random videos from the units and the brief. Compare beats, hook, ending, turn time and the 8 scores: count the scores that differ by 2 points or more, the mean gap, and the block boundaries and loops that match. Report the disagreements in `12-verification.md`. Disagreements usually sit on edge cases: an ASIDE block, a last sentence split between PAYOFF and BUTTON, backstory tagged STORY or SETUP. When both annotators are models with the same brief, read the agreement as an upper bound on reliability.

## Calibration: compute your own numbers
Every reference number comes from your own sample. Read them in `10-author-grammar.md` after step 5: median runtime, words and words per minute; median turn position and its range; loops, jokes and facts per minute; share of sentences with "you"; share of but and therefore links; hook and ending counts; templates with their number of videos; score means and ranges. Compare the top group with the recent group. Copy no median from another author or from an example.

## Decisions and their reasons
- Units come from the creator's own captions, cut by sentence: rhythm metrics then measure the author, not the caption tool.
- Templates are named by the analyst from the core skeletons. A raw beat string is unique to almost every video; the core skeleton and the length class are what repeats.
- The scores keep the same 8 axes as the long-form Fiche, with anchors per minute instead of per 10 minutes, so long and short Fiches stay comparable.

## Errors to avoid
- Parsing "views: X | likes: Y" headers by splitting on the bar loses the first field, and the aggregator then shows 0 views for every video. `short_units.py` handles this header form; keep it.
- Rerunning the unit splitter after annotators started overwrites their map skeletons. Fix metadata in `meta.json` only.
- Reading the creator's voiced objection as their opinion. Many talking-head creators state the viewer's assumption in quotes in order to knock it down ("You're thinking: just raise the price."). Tag it OBJECTION, and tag the TURN where they answer it.
- Old captions without punctuation: let the splitter fall back to caption cues, never to fixed word counts.
- Creator caption tracks can come out of order or as very long sentences. Tag by spoken order and say so in the glosses.
- Annotators share the scratchpad: give each one its own subfolder name in the brief, or helper scripts get overwritten.
- A unit that fits no code: add the code to the taxonomy with a definition and an invented example, and log it in the changelog. APOPHASIS and E-FACT entered the taxonomy this way.
- The turn position can vary widely across one author's videos. Copy the template's order and its median turn position, never a fixed second.
- Top versus recent mixes format and era: the most-viewed videos are often older and of another format than the recent ones. Say "tendency" and name the confound.

## Sample

`sample/case-00-sample/` holds two invented short videos, already tagged (all names, numbers and events invented). Run analyze.py on each video, then `python3 -I scripts/aggregate_author.py sample/case-00-sample`, to check your setup before you tag a real creator. See `sample/README.md`.

## Rules
- Drafts only: never publish, send or schedule anything for the user.
- Never ask for a password. The user signs in to every platform.
- No promised views or results, and no invented numbers. Every fact in a script carries a source the user can open.
- Respect each platform's terms of use.
- Transcripts of other people's videos stay private: use them for analysis, write your own words, and credit the creator when you borrow a structure.
- Describe sensitive topics (crime, politics, health) neutrally in glosses and in the Fiche.
