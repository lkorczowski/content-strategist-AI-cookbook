# The 3D what-if format

Observed 2026-10-30 on public TikTok accounts. Counts are public counters read that day; they describe those accounts, not a promise.

## Beat structure of the reference (80 s, @man_ia_c, electrons)

| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0 to 5.5 s | Title question on screen, keyword in blue, icon; daylight low-poly park; real-time countdown in a HUD |
| 5.5 to 8.2 s | Second-person place line ("you are in ...") |
| 8.2 s | The event: hard switch of the same world to white wireframe on black; the HUD switches to a slowed clock |
| 8 to 63 s | About nine consequences, 3 to 4 s each, scale grows: hands, a person, the park, sand, people, Earth, Moon |
| 63 to 72 s | The return, and a twist that makes it worse |
| 72 to 74 s | Black, silence |
| 74 to 80 s | Loop card: the title again and one fact in small mono type |

## Why it travels (evidence grade in brackets)

- The promise is readable before any motion: question title on frame 1 [practitioner, two public tutorials say it].
- A number ticks the whole time, so leaving feels premature [practitioner].
- Second person and a real place put the viewer inside [practitioner].
- Escalating scale gives each caption a reason to wait for the next [observed].
- Twist at the end, then a loop card: the end rewards finishing and restarts cleanly [observed; loop effect is a hypothesis].
- Real numbers invite corrections in the comments [practitioner, unmeasured].
- No voice: works muted and in any language [observed].
- The reference post carries the platform's AI-generated label [observed].
