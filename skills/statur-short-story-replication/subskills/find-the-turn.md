# Find the turn

A common short-form engine is the wrong first reading. The hook states a verdict ("the worst invoice I have ever seen", "the strangest rule in our industry"). The video voices the sensible first reading. Then one plain fact makes the verdict true. The rest escalates. Read where your author places the turn in `10-author-grammar.md` (median turn position of the template) and plan for that share of the runtime.

## Process
1. Write the subject's first reading in the viewer's words, in quotes, as a fair person would say it ("Look at the numbers"). If you cannot make it sound sensible, the turn will feel cheap.
2. List 5 to 10 true, dated facts about the subject. Mark the ones that surprise you.
3. Pick the topper: the fact that makes the first reading look mild or upside down, and that fits in one sentence of under 20 words. Invented shapes: the meeting lasted eleven hours; the refund arrived before the order; the temporary fix has run for nine years.
4. Find the deadpan form: a question the viewer can answer wrongly, then the flattest possible answer. Invented example: "So what did the company do with the complaint? It framed it. In the lobby."
5. Keep two or three escalating facts for the build, each sourced.

## Success criteria
- The first reading is fair and quoted.
- The topper is a fact with a date and a source, stated in one sentence.
- The hook can contain the verdict without giving the topper away.

## Failure modes
- A topper that is an opinion. In this template the opinion comes late, after the facts.
- A turn that only adds detail. The topper changes how the hook word should be read.
