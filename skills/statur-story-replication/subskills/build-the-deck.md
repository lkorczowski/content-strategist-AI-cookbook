# Build the deck

## What the move does

An unpredictable subject is hard to explain in the abstract. The move names its possible outcomes as cards, from mild to world-changing and from good to catastrophic. The same cards come back several times: in the reveal (the scale grows card by card), in the pivot on why the subject is feared (the most brutal cards), and in a sandbox episode where several cards are drawn at once. Naming makes each outcome easy to recall (FLUENCY); the unknown next card keeps attention (VAR).

## How to find it in your source analysis

- `07-seed.yaml`: the REVEAL and PIVOT blocks, and the STORY block with many EXAMPLE moves in a row (the sandbox). Count how many outcomes each of them shows: these are your counts.
- `02-map.md`: units tagged EXAMPLE, LIST, CONTRAST and AWE inside those blocks.
- `06-fiche.json`: the `equipment` list often names the deck as an asset.

If the source has no deck, skip this sub-skill.

## How to transpose it

1. List 8 to 12 real outcomes of SUBJECT, each a documented case with a date and a source.
2. Rank them on two axes: size (mild to world-changing) and sign (good to catastrophic).
3. Give each a short, evocative card name in the target language: a noun a child understands.
4. Cover these roles when the seed needs them:
   - mild good; big good with a catch; a cruel loss of tools; a change of the rules of the game;
   - a trap with no way out; a total loss of wealth; a loss of identity;
   - a nemesis; a future betrayal; a tool or weapon; an ally; a wish granted to someone else;
   - a named mystery left open.
5. Use the counts from your seed: how many cards in the reveal (mild to world-changing), how many in the pivot (the most brutal), how many in the sandbox, one for the worst draw, one named mystery for an easter egg.

## Invented example

A 20-minute explainer about an unpredictable product: a referral program. The deck: Spark (a few signups), Wave (a spike that crashes the server), Drain (rewards eat the marketing budget), Rival (a competitor copies the program), Ghost (referred users leave as fast as they came), and a named Mystery (one spike nobody can trace). In a real script, each card points to a documented case with its source.

## Success criteria

- Every card has a real example; no card exists only as a metaphor.
- Reading the reveal cards in order, the viewer feels the scale grow.
- The worst card is clearly worse than everything shown before it.
- The named mystery is real and can be answered later, for example in a pinned comment.
