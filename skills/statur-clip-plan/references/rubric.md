# Clip rubric for lives, webinars and long videos

A clip earns its place when a stranger who never saw the recording stops scrolling, understands the subject in three seconds, learns or feels something, and leaves with a line worth repeating.

## 1. Hard gates

A candidate that fails one gate is not a clip. Fix it in the plan (add a passage, cut a reference) or drop it.

| Gate | Test |
|---|---|
| Length | 60 seconds minimum after the edit, 5 minutes maximum. The floor is a setting in the company context file. |
| Standalone | Nothing refers to context the clip does not show: "as I said earlier", "this person in the chat", "the slide behind me". Restate the question when the clip answers one. |
| One subject | The clip answers one question or defends one idea. Two ideas make two clips. |
| Payoff | The last sentence resolves the opening: an answer, a twist, a number, a rule to keep. |
| Safe | No unverified figure, no client name or number the brand forbids, no price where prices are never shown, no viewer's personal data, no third-party music or footage without rights. |

## 2. Scores (1 to 5)

| Criterion | 5 looks like | 1 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | The first 3 seconds open a gap, name a threat or state a claim people want to argue with. It can be a cold open: a strong line from later in the passage moved to the front. | A greeting, a recap, "so, yeah". |
| Clarity | A non-expert names the subject after one viewing. Jargon is explained or cut. | The viewer needs the full recording to follow. |
| Value | Specific: a number, an example, a story, a method in steps. New or contrarian for the audience. | Generic advice anyone could give. |
| Payoff | The last line lands and can be quoted. | The clip fades out mid-thought. |
| Delivery | Energy, fluency, clear audio. Hesitations are few or removable. | Long pauses, coughs, reading the chat silently. |
| Fit | Serves the brand's positioning and its buyers (company context file). | Off-topic for the business, even if fun. |

Total = 2 x Hook + Clarity + 1.5 x Value + 1.5 x Payoff + Delivery + 1.5 x Fit, out of 42.5. Rank by total, then break ties with the funnel need of the week.

## 3. Objective

Every clip carries one stage and one or two goals, so the calendar balances reach and conversion.

| Stage | Typical content | Goals |
|---|---|---|
| TOFU | Contrarian take, myth vs data, a story with a twist, a broad pain named in the buyer's words | Reach, virality (shares) |
| MOFU | A method in steps, a teardown, a framework, a mistake and its fix, an answer that shows expertise | Authority, trust, follows |
| BOFU | The objection heard on sales calls, a named result, how the offer works, who it is for and who it is not | Conversion: DMs, calls, sign-ups |

Format comes from the company's format library (comment reply, myth vs data, founder story, the objection, teardown, one number explained, the bet). A live Q&A maps naturally to the comment reply format.

## 4. Patterns specific to lives and webinars

- Questions from viewers: restate the question in the first seconds (the host's own words, or on-screen text "Someone asked: ..."). Never show a viewer's handle without a reason.
- Scattered answers: a host often comes back to a question 20 minutes later. Stitch the passages in logical order. The plan lists every passage with its source timecode.
- Overlaps are allowed: one long answer can give a 3-minute deep dive and a 60-second punchy cut with a different hook.
- Live noise to cut: greetings, thanks for gifts, reading names, "can you hear me", silences while reading the chat, technical fixes.
- Cold open: move the payoff or the most provocative sentence to the first 2 to 4 seconds, then play the passage from its start. Do not repeat the moved sentence at its original place.
- Ending: end on the payoff line plus at most one beat. A clip that keeps talking after its best line loses retention.
